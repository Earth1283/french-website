# Asking How Someone Is (And Actually Meaning It)

"How are you" is treated very differently in French than in English — and knowing the register ladder here, plus what people *actually* say back, will make you sound far more natural than the textbook phrase alone.

## The formality ladder

Just like *tu*/*vous*, "how are you" comes in matched formal and informal pairs:

| Register | Phrase | Literal meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Formal | *Comment allez-vous ?* | "How do you go?" |
| Neutral / semi-formal | *Comment ça va ?* | "How's it going?" |
| Casual | *Ça va ?* | "Going OK?" |
| Very casual | *Ça roule ?* / *Quoi de neuf ?* | "Rolling along?" / "What's new?" |

*Ça va ?* is by far the one you'll hear the most in everyday life — it's short, works in almost any moderately casual context, and, remarkably, works as **both the question and a valid answer**: *"Ça va ?" — "Ça va."* Question and response, same two words, different intonation. This economy is very French and very useful once it clicks.

## The textbook answer vs the real answer

Every beginner learns *"Très bien, merci, et vous ?"* — "Very well, thank you, and you?" — and it's not wrong, it's simply the most formal, slightly stiff option, roughly equivalent to answering "how are you" in English with "I am quite well, thank you for asking." Fine for a job interview, a little much for a friend.

In real, casual conversation, French speakers reach for a much wider, more honest-sounding range of responses:

- ***Ça va, et toi ?*** — "Good, and you?" — the everyday default, casual register
- ***Ça va bien, merci*** — "Going well, thanks" — a notch warmer than the bare minimum
- ***Pas mal*** — "Not bad" — genuinely common, mildly understated in the very French way
- ***Bof*** — an entire response on its own, a verbal shrug meaning roughly "eh, so-so" — extremely useful and impossible to translate in one word
- ***On fait aller*** — "We're managing" / "getting by" — resigned, good-humored, very idiomatic
- ***Ça pourrait être pire*** — "Could be worse" — dry, common among people who've had a long week

Notice that *"très bien"* barely appears in this authentic list. Real people, most days, are "not bad" or "getting by," not uniformly "very well" — and answering with the more honest, more casual register will sound considerably more natural than the textbook line, once you're in an actually casual setting.

## Always return the question

Whatever level you answer at, the conversation stalls if you don't hand it back — *"...et toi ?"* / *"...et vous ?"* is doing real social work, not just politeness filler. Leaving it off can read as mildly self-centered, or simply as an incomplete exchange, the conversational equivalent of a door left half-open.

## A realistic full exchange

> **A:** Salut ! Ça va ?
> **B:** Ça va, merci, et toi ?
> **A:** Ouais, pas mal. Un peu fatigué, mais ça va.

("Yeah, not bad. A bit tired, but I'm OK.") — notice how far this is from "very well, thank you," and how much more like an actual conversation between two actual humans it sounds. That gap is exactly the one this page is meant to close.
