# The Bonjour → Bonsoir Switch

*Bonjour* covers an enormous span of the day — from the moment you wake up until, roughly, early evening. Then, without warning or announcement, everyone around you quietly switches to *bonsoir*, and if you're still saying *bonjour* at that point, you'll sound like someone who hasn't checked a clock in hours.

## There's no official cutoff — but there's a real one

Nobody rings a bell. There's no rule printed anywhere that says "*bonsoir* begins at 18:00." And yet native speakers switch at roughly the same moment, guided by something closer to instinct than logic. As a rough, genuinely useful anchor:

- **Before ~6pm:** *bonjour*
- **Roughly 6pm onward:** *bonsoir*
- **The switch drifts with the season** — later in summer when it's still bright at 8pm, earlier in winter when it's dark by 5pm. Daylight matters more than the clock.

If you're unsure which side of the line you're on, listen to what the person greeting *you* says, and mirror it. That single tactic will get you through 95% of real interactions without ever having to think about the clock.

## The part almost nobody explains: *bonsoir* is also "goodbye"

This is the detail that trips up even careful learners. *Bonsoir* isn't purely an evening "hello" — it doubles as an evening "goodbye" too, in a way *bonjour* never does for daytime farewells (you'd use *au revoir* for that; more on this on the next page).

So this exchange is completely normal and not repetitive at all:

> *Customer, entering a shop at 7pm:* "Bonsoir !"
> *Shopkeeper:* "Bonsoir !"
> *[transaction happens]*
> *Customer, leaving:* "Bonsoir, merci !"

Same word, used to open **and** close the interaction. If that feels strange, think of it less as "good evening" and more as an all-purpose evening greeting-token that works in both directions.

## Bonne soirée — the one everyone confuses with *bonsoir*

Here's a genuinely common mix-up, made by learners at every level, not just beginners: *bonsoir* ("good evening," said on arrival or departure) is **not the same** as *bonne soirée* ("have a good evening," said only on departure, to someone who is continuing their evening without you).

| Phrase | Means | Used when |
|---|---|---|
| *Bonsoir* | "Good evening" | Arriving, or leaving, in the evening |
| *Bonne soirée* | "Have a good evening" | Leaving — said to someone whose evening continues after yours ends |
| *Bonne nuit* | "Good night" | Only when someone is actually about to sleep |

A shopkeeper closing up at 7pm doesn't say *bonne nuit* to you as you leave — they're not tucking you into bed, they're wishing you a nice rest of your evening: *"Bonsoir, bonne soirée !"* Save *bonne nuit* for the actual end of the day, said to family, a host, or a partner, right before everyone goes to sleep.

## Try the reflex

Next time you imagine walking into a café, don't ask "what time is it" — ask "is it still bright, or has it gone golden/dark?" That instinct will serve you far better than a mental clock ever will.
