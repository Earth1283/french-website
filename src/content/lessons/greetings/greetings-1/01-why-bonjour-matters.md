# Why "Bonjour" Is Not Optional

Here is the single most important cultural fact in this entire course, more important than any verb conjugation: in France, you do not enter a room and start talking. You enter a room, you say *bonjour*, and only then does the room agree to acknowledge that you exist.

This sounds like an exaggeration. It is not. Walk into a boulangerie, a pharmacy, a train station ticket window, a doctor's waiting room, or a small clothing shop, and skip the *bonjour* — go straight to "do you have this in a medium?" — and you will feel a very specific, very cold drop in temperature. The shopkeeper isn't being rude. You were.

## Greetings as a social contract, not small talk

In English-speaking countries, a greeting is optional lubrication — nice to have, not load-bearing. You can walk up to a counter, say "hi, can I get a coffee," and nobody blinks, because the greeting and the request are allowed to merge into one breath.

In France, they are two separate, sequential events. First you greet the person, as a person. *Then* you make your request, as a customer. Skipping step one and jumping to step two reduces the human behind the counter to a vending machine, and — reasonably, if you think about it — they resent that. *Bonjour* is the verbal handshake that says "I see you, you are a person, and now we may proceed to business." It costs you one word and buys you an enormous amount of goodwill.

> Test this yourself once you're there: say *bonjour* first, wait half a beat, then ask your question. Compare the reaction to the times you don't. The difference is not subtle.

## Where this rule applies

This is not limited to shops. The *bonjour*-first rule applies to:

- Entering **any** small or medium business — bakeries, pharmacies, hair salons, cafés, independent shops
- Getting into a **taxi**
- Approaching a **stranger** to ask for directions or the time
- Entering a **doctor's, dentist's, or any office waiting room** (yes, to the room in general, not just the receptionist)
- Stepping into a **shared elevator**, in many buildings — this one catches almost every visitor off guard
- Starting a **phone call** to a business, before you explain why you're calling

It applies less rigidly in large anonymous spaces — a supermarket, a big chain store, a crowded metro car — where there's no single person whose attention you're specifically claiming. But the safe default, everywhere a human is about to interact with you directly, is: greet first.
