# Politeness Combos for Real Situations

Everything in this lesson so far has been building blocks. This page is where they get assembled — four realistic, worked scenes showing how greetings, *tu*/*vous*, please, thank you, and the apology ladder all chain together the way they actually do in a real exchange.

## Scene 1: Ordering a coffee

> **You:** Bonjour !
> **Barista:** Bonjour !
> **You:** Un café, s'il vous plaît.
> **Barista:** Voilà !
> **You:** Merci beaucoup. Bonne journée !
> **Barista:** Merci, à vous aussi !

Every piece from this entire unit appears here: the mandatory opening *bonjour*, *vous*-register throughout (a stranger, a service interaction), *s'il vous plaît* turning a request into a request rather than a demand, an intensified *merci*, and a proper exit combo. Six short lines, and not one piece of politeness scaffolding missing.

## Scene 2: Asking a stranger for directions

> **You:** Excusez-moi, madame. Vous savez où est la pharmacie la plus proche ?
> **Stranger:** Oui ! C'est tout droit, puis à gauche.
> **You:** D'accord, merci beaucoup !
> **Stranger:** Je vous en prie, bonne journée !

Notice *excusez-moi* doing its real job here — claiming attention, not apologizing — followed immediately by the *vous* register (a stranger, correctly defaulted-to per the earlier lesson), and a response to *merci* that's a small step more polished than the bare-minimum *de rien*.

## Scene 3: Bumping into someone, then recovering

> **You:** *(bumps someone's shoulder in a crowded metro)* Pardon !
> **Stranger:** Ce n'est rien !
> **You:** *(a moment later, realizing you also stepped on their bag)* Ah, désolé !
> **Stranger:** Pas de souci !

Two separate small mistakes, two correctly-weighted responses: the reflexive *pardon* for the light bump, then the more considered *désolé* once it's clear you've actually caused a second, slightly bigger inconvenience. And two different, equally natural ways of brushing it off in return — *ce n'est rien* ("it's nothing") and *pas de souci* ("no worries," extremely common casual usage you'll hear constantly).

## Scene 4: A first meeting that turns friendly

> **A:** Bonjour, comment allez-vous ?
> **B:** Ça va bien, merci, et vous ?
> **A:** Très bien ! Comment vous appelez-vous ?
> **B:** Je m'appelle Marc. Et vous ?
> **A:** Moi, c'est Sophie. Enchantée.
> **B:** Enchanté ! On peut se tutoyer, si tu veux.
> **A:** Avec plaisir !

This one earns its place as the unit's closer because it shows the *entire arc* in miniature: the formal opening, the introduction ritual, and — the payoff from the very first page of this lesson — an explicit, natural proposal to drop from *vous* to *tu*, accepted warmly with *avec plaisir* rather than a flat *oui*.

## What to actually practice

Don't try to memorize these four scenes word-for-word — memorize the *shape*: greet first, match the register to the person, request politely, thank warmly, exit properly. Once that shape is automatic, you'll be able to build correct, natural-sounding exchanges for situations this lesson never specifically covered, because you'll understand the underlying logic rather than a fixed script.

That's the whole point of everything in this unit — not a phrasebook to memorize, but a set of instincts you now actually have.
