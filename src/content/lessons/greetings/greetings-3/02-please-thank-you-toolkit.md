# The Please/Thank-You Toolkit, Fully Loaded

You already know *s'il vous plaît* and *merci*. This page fills out the rest of the family — the intensifiers, the informal versions, and, critically, the correct ways to *respond* when someone thanks you, which is a whole vocabulary set most courses skip entirely.

## Please, at every register

| Phrase | Register | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| *S'il vous plaît* | Formal / *vous* | The safe default with strangers, staff, anyone you'd address as *vous* |
| *S'il te plaît* | Informal / *tu* | Friends, family, people you *tutoie* |
| *Stp* | Very casual, written only | Texting abbreviation — never said aloud, common in messages |

Notice the pattern: *vous* → *s'il vous plaît*, *tu* → *s'il te plaît*. The politeness word inherits the pronoun choice from the earlier lesson automatically — get the *tu*/*vous* decision right, and the "please" almost writes itself.

## Thank you, with volume control

*Merci* alone is always fine. But French has a genuinely useful set of intensifiers for when "thanks" doesn't feel like enough:

- ***Merci beaucoup*** — "thank you very much" — the standard upgrade, safe everywhere
- ***Merci bien*** — also "thanks a lot," slightly more old-fashioned/formal in tone, occasionally used with light irony among younger speakers (context tells you which)
- ***Merci mille fois*** — "a thousand thanks" — genuinely warm, used when someone did you a real favor
- ***Un grand merci*** — "a big thank-you," often used in writing (emails, cards) more than speech
- ***Merci infiniment*** — "thanks endlessly / infinitely" — the most formal, most heartfelt option, reserved for real gratitude

For everyday use, *merci* and *merci beaucoup* will cover nearly everything you need. The rest are good to *recognize* when you hear or read them, even before you're using them yourself.

## The part almost nobody teaches: how to respond to *merci*

Being thanked and not knowing how to respond leaves an awkward gap — and "you're welcome" has more French equivalents than you'd expect, each with a slightly different flavor:

| Response | Literal meaning | Flavor |
|---|---|---|
| *De rien* | "Of nothing" | The everyday default — casual, universal |
| *Je vous en prie* / *Je t'en prie* | "I beg you [not to mention it]" | More formal, a step up from *de rien* |
| *Avec plaisir* | "With pleasure" | Warm — implies you genuinely enjoyed helping |
| *Pas de problème* | "No problem" | Very casual, close to the English phrase it mirrors |
| *C'est rien* | "It's nothing" | Casual, close cousin of *de rien* |
| *Il n'y a pas de quoi* | "There's nothing to [thank me for]" | Slightly formal, warm |

*De rien* is your safe, all-purpose default — genuinely comparable to how versatile "you're welcome" is in English. But reaching for *avec plaisir* when someone thanks you for a real favor (not just holding a door) lands noticeably warmer, and is worth having ready.

## A quick worked exchange

> **A:** Vous avez l'heure, s'il vous plaît ?  *("Do you have the time, please?")*
> **B:** Oui, il est quinze heures.  *("Yes, it's 3pm.")*
> **A:** Merci beaucoup !
> **B:** Je vous en prie !

Five lines, and every single register choice — the *vous*, the *s'il vous plaît*, the intensified *merci*, the slightly-elevated response — is internally consistent. That consistency, more than any individual word, is what makes French sound fluent rather than just technically correct.
