# Story Signals That Choose the Past

Time expressions do not mechanically choose a tense, but they often reveal the role a verb plays. Learn to notice them as narrative signals rather than magical buttons.

## Background signals

*Pendant que* often introduces an action in progress, so it commonly goes with imparfait.

> *Pendant que je cuisinais, les enfants jouaient.* — While I was cooking, the children were playing.

Descriptions and routines likewise invite imparfait: *d'habitude, tous les jours, souvent, à cette époque-là*. They tell you what was generally true or repeatedly happening.

> *Tous les étés, nous allions chez mes grands-parents.* — Every summer, we went to my grandparents'.

## Event signals

*Soudain, tout à coup, puis, ce jour-là* often introduce a finished event, so passé composé is natural.

> *Tout à coup, quelqu'un a frappé à la porte.* — Suddenly, someone knocked at the door.

The classic interruption frame combines both tenses:

> *Je dormais quand l'alarme a sonné.* — I was sleeping when the alarm rang.

The first action is already underway; the second arrives and interrupts it. Reversing them changes the story and usually sounds odd.

## Avoid a false rule

*Hier* does not force passé composé. You can describe yesterday's weather with imparfait: *Hier, il faisait froid*. Conversely, a repeated action can be located in a finished period: *Pendant les vacances, nous avons visité trois musées*. The duration has an endpoint, so the visits are counted as a completed package.

## Quick contrasts

- *Il lisait.* — He was reading / used to read.
- *Il a lu le livre.* — He read the book (to completion or as an event).
- *Je connaissais Paris.* — I knew Paris.
- *J'ai connu Marie en 2019.* — I met / got to know Marie in 2019.

Make your own interruption with a familiar sound: a doorbell, message, alarm, or train announcement. Put one action in progress around it. Repeating this pattern makes the contrast feel like storytelling rather than an abstract tense exercise.

Signals help, but meaning is boss. Look at the storyteller's point of view before you choose a verb form.
