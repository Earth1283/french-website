# Let the Two Past Tenses Work Together

Good narration alternates between atmosphere and events. Start by placing the listener somewhere, then let completed actions disrupt or develop that scene. This is how French stories sound natural instead of like a chronology spreadsheet.

> *Il était tard, la pluie tombait et je cherchais un taxi.* — It was late, rain was falling, and I was looking for a taxi.

> *Puis une voiture s'est arrêtée devant moi.* — Then a car stopped in front of me.

The first sentence is a wide shot: time, weather, ongoing search. The second is the plot point. Notice that the same speaker can switch tense without changing topic.

## A compact narrative recipe

1. Set the scene with imparfait: weather, age, feelings, habits, ongoing action.
2. Introduce a completed event with passé composé.
3. Return to imparfait if the background continues.
4. Add the next event in passé composé.

> *Je rentrais du travail quand j'ai vu mon voisin. Il portait des cartons, alors je l'ai aidé.* — I was coming home from work when I saw my neighbour. He was carrying boxes, so I helped him.

## Verbs whose meaning changes

Some common verbs change meaning with tense because the storyteller frames them differently.

- *Je savais* — I knew.
- *J'ai su* — I found out.
- *Je pouvais* — I was able to / could generally.
- *J'ai pu* — I managed to / got the chance to.
- *Je voulais* — I wanted.
- *J'ai voulu* — I decided / tried to want, depending on context.

These are not random traps. Passé composé presents a boundary or result; imparfait presents a state or ongoing possibility. Read the surrounding sentence before grabbing a dictionary gloss.

Try narrating one small mishap: describe what was happening, then name the event that changed everything. A missed bus is enough drama for excellent grammar.

Afterward, underline every imparfait and circle every passé composé. If the underlined verbs build the atmosphere and the circled verbs advance the story, your paragraph is doing exactly what a French listener expects from a clear past-tense account.
