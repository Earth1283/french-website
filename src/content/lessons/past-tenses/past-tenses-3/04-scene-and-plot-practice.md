# Scene and Plot: Worked Past-Tense Practice

## Listen for the camera change

When you listen to a story, imagine a camera. Imparfait is the wide shot: weather, feeling, routine, and an action already in progress. Passé composé is the cut to a specific event: a door opened, a phone rang, someone decided something. Replay the story once and underline the wide-shot verbs; replay it again and circle the cuts. This is excellent speaking practice too. Instead of asking “which tense sounds French?”, ask “am I describing what was going on, or reporting what happened?” The answer usually arrives before the verb ending does.

Read this short account. The imparfait supplies the world before the problem; the passé composé records what happened. That distinction matters more than whether an English translation happens to use “was” or “did.”

## The rainy appointment

> *Il faisait très chaud et j'attendais mon amie devant le cinéma. Les gens passaient rapidement parce qu'il commençait à pleuvoir. À huit heures, mon téléphone a vibré : elle a écrit qu'elle avait raté le métro. J'étais déçue, mais j'ai acheté deux billets pour la séance suivante.*

*Faisait, attendais, passaient, commençait, étais* are setting or ongoing states. *A vibré, a écrit, a raté, ai acheté* are completed developments. The weather does not “finish”; the message and ticket purchase do.

## Spot the mistake

1. *Je dormais quand je me suis réveillais.* → *Je dormais quand je me suis réveillé(e).* The waking is a completed change.
2. *Tout à coup, il pleuvait.* → usually *Tout à coup, il a commencé à pleuvoir.* The event is the start.
3. *Chaque dimanche, nous avons mangé chez eux.* → *Chaque dimanche, nous mangions chez eux* for a past habit.
4. *Il a était fatigué.* → *Il était fatigué.* A state uses imparfait, and never doubles auxiliaries.

## Self-check

- Which tense describes weather? (Usually imparfait.)
- Which tense narrates an interruption? (Usually passé composé.)
- Complete: *Je ___ quand tu ___.* (*travaillais* / *as appelé*.)
- Can *hier* appear with imparfait? (Yes: *Hier, il faisait froid.*)

Write three lines of your own: one scene, one surprise, one consequence. If the background and plot are easy to identify, your tense choices will be easy to justify.
