# Être Past Tense in Full Sentences

## Practise the frame aloud

Say the full frame before filling in the details: *je me suis*, *tu ne t'es pas*, *elle s'est*, *nous nous sommes*, *vous êtes-vous*, *ils ne se sont pas*. Reflexive verbs look crowded only until the pronoun and auxiliary become one familiar rhythm. Then add a common participle: *levé(e), trompé(e), reposé(e), rencontré(e)(s)*. Speaking these frames slowly makes word order automatic and leaves your attention free for meaning, agreement, and the story you want to tell.

The être past has a few moving parts, especially with reflexive verbs. The good news is that each piece has a fixed home. Put the reflexive pronoun before the auxiliary; put the past participle after it; put agreement on the participle.

> *Je me suis réveillé tôt.* — I woke up early.

> *Elle s'est habillée rapidement.* — She got dressed quickly.

## Negation

With a reflexive verb, *ne* comes before the pronoun and *pas* comes after the auxiliary.

> *Je ne me suis pas trompé.* — I did not make a mistake.

> *Ils ne sont pas arrivés.* — They did not arrive.

The common English-speaker error is putting *pas* after the participle. French keeps the negative wrapped around the conjugated helper: *ne sont pas arrivés*, never *ne sont arrivés pas*.

## Questions

In speech, use intonation or *est-ce que*. Both keep the normal past-tense order.

> *Tu es arrivé ?* — Did you arrive?

> *Est-ce qu'elle est sortie ?* — Did she go out?

Formal inversion exists: *Est-elle arrivée ?* It is useful to recognise, but do not force it into a relaxed café conversation. French is not impressed by a learner who sounds like a 1950s newsreader.

## Time and agreement together

> *Hier, nous nous sommes promenés au bord de l'eau.* — Yesterday, we walked by the water.

> *Mes amies ne sont pas restées longtemps.* — My friends did not stay long.

> *Vous êtes rentré(e)(s) quand ?* — When did you come home?

In real conversation, agreement may be invisible in speech, but it still matters on a message, exam, or application. Build the sentence in this order: subject, pronoun if needed, *être*, negative if needed, participle, then details. The structure stays stable while the story changes.
