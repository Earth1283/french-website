# Être Past Tense: Travel Day Practice

## A five-second grammar routine

Before sending or saying an être-past sentence, pause long enough to check three things: identify the subject, choose *être* in the present, then make the participle agree on paper. This small routine also catches reflexive pronouns and negative placement. For example, a group of women says *nous ne nous sommes pas perdues*; a mixed group says *nous ne nous sommes pas perdus*. In speech these endings often disappear, but in writing they show that you have connected the action to the people who performed it. Accuracy here is less about memorising a chart than noticing who is in the sentence.

This travel-day conversation uses movement verbs, agreement, a reflexive verb, and one verb that changes auxiliary when it takes an object. Read it once for the story, then hunt for the grammar.

## At the station

> **Zoé:** *Tu es arrivé à temps ?*  
> **Noah:** *Oui, mais le train est parti avec dix minutes de retard.*  
> **Zoé:** *Moi, je me suis levée à six heures et je suis quand même arrivée en retard.*  
> **Noah:** *Tes amies sont déjà entrées dans le train ?*  
> **Zoé:** *Oui, elles sont montées il y a cinq minutes.*

*Zoé* is feminine, so we see *levée* and *arrivée*. *Tes amies* is feminine plural, so *entrées* and *montées* agree too. The narrator does not get to skip those endings simply because the platform is hectic.

## Spot the mistake

1. *Elle a arrivée hier.* → *Elle est arrivée hier.*
2. *Nous sommes partisE.* → *Nous sommes partis* for a mixed group; *parties* for women only.
3. *Je ne suis arrivé pas.* → *Je ne suis pas arrivé(e).* 
4. *Il est sorti son passeport.* → *Il a sorti son passeport.* It has a direct object.

## Choose the auxiliary

- “I went out at midnight.” → *Je suis sorti(e) à minuit.*
- “I took out my passport.” → *J'ai sorti mon passeport.*
- “They came back late.” → *Ils sont revenus tard.*
- “We got dressed.” → *Nous nous sommes habillés.*

## Self-check

- What auxiliary does *venir* use? (*Être*.)
- Where does *pas* go? (After *être*, before the participle.)
- What ending does *elles sont tombées* need? (*-ées*.)
- Does *courir* use *être*? (No: *j'ai couru*.)

If you can make those choices deliberately, the être club is no longer a list of mysterious exceptions.
