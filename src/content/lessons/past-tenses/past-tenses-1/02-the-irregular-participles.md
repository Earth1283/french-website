# The Irregulars Every Avoir Verb Hides

Regular endings cover a lot of ground, but some of French's most common verbs refuse to follow them. Their participles must simply be memorised — the good news is that there are only a handful you'll use constantly.

## The core ten

> *faire → fait* — *J'ai fait la cuisine.* (I cooked.)
>
> *prendre → pris* — *J'ai pris le métro.* (I took the metro.)
>
> *mettre → mis* — *J'ai mis mon manteau.* (I put on my coat.)
>
> *dire → dit* — *J'ai dit la vérité.* (I told the truth.)
>
> *voir → vu* — *J'ai vu ce film.* (I saw that film.)
>
> *avoir → eu* — *J'ai eu de la chance.* (I got lucky.)
>
> *être → été* — *J'ai été malade.* (I was sick.)
>
> *pouvoir → pu* — *Je n'ai pas pu venir.* (I couldn't come.)
>
> *vouloir → voulu* — *Il a voulu partir tôt.* (He wanted to leave early.)
>
> *devoir → dû* — *J'ai dû travailler.* (I had to work.)

## Family verbs inherit the irregularity

Verbs built on *prendre*, *mettre*, and *dire* keep the same irregular pattern: *comprendre → compris*, *apprendre → appris*, *permettre → permis*, *promettre → promis*, *redire → redit*. Learn the root verb well and the compounds come almost for free.

## Eu and été: the two that look nothing like their infinitive

*Avoir* and *être* are the two verbs every sentence depends on, and in the passé composé they are also the two that look least like themselves. *Eu* is pronounced exactly like the letter "u" — nothing left of *avoir* at all. *Été* is at least recognisable, but note it never changes: "elle a été" not "elle a étée", because the auxiliary here is *avoir*, and *avoir*-participles don't agree with the subject.

## The dû trap

*Devoir* gives *dû*, complete with a circumflex accent — but only in the masculine singular. Drop the circumflex everywhere else: *due, dus, dues*. The circumflex exists purely to keep *dû* (had to) visually distinct from *du* (some, of the): *j'ai dû partir* versus *j'ai bu du café*. Miss the accent and a proofreader will notice; miss it in speech and nobody will, because they sound identical.

## Building the habit

There's no shortcut around memorising these ten — but they are also the ten you will hear most in daily conversation, so repetition does the work fast. Say them in short sentences, out loud, with a subject and an object each time, and they'll stick well before you need to reach for a list.
