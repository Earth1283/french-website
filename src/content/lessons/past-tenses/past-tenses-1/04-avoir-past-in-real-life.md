# Avoir Past Tense: A Small Investigation

Passé composé becomes useful when several ordinary details arrive at once. Read the mini-story first for meaning, then look at the verbs. Every completed action has an *avoir* form plus a participle; no verb has been left to fend for itself.

## The missing wallet

> **Camille:** *Tu as vu mon portefeuille ?*  
> **Nabil:** *Non, mais j'ai trouvé des clés près de la porte.*  
> **Camille:** *J'ai cherché partout ce matin. J'ai même regardé dans le frigo.*  
> **Nabil:** *Tu as peut-être posé ton sac dans la voiture.*  
> **Camille:** *Ah oui ! J'ai oublié que nous avons pris la voiture hier.*

Notice the regular participles: *cherché, regardé, posé, oublié*. The odd one is *pris*, from *prendre*. It is still doing the same job after *avoir*.

## Spot the mistake

1. *Hier, je mangé au café.*  
   It needs the auxiliary: *Hier, j'ai mangé au café.*
2. *Nous avons finir le projet.*  
   The participle is required: *Nous avons fini le projet.*
3. *Elle n'a vu pas le message.*  
   Keep *pas* around the auxiliary: *Elle n'a pas vu le message.*
4. *Ils ont déjà arrivés.*  
   *Arriver* uses *être*, so save it for the next lesson: *Ils sont déjà arrivés.*

## Build your own

Choose a real completed action and give it a time anchor:

- *J'ai acheté ... hier.*
- *Nous avons regardé ... samedi.*
- *Je n'ai pas compris ... ce matin.*
- *Tu as déjà essayé ... ?*

## Self-check

- What are the two parts of *j'ai vendu*? (*ai* + *vendu*.)
- Where does *pas* go? (After the auxiliary and before the participle.)
- Which ending makes a regular *-re* participle? (*-u*.)
- Does French choose between “I ate” and “I have eaten”? (No: *j'ai mangé* can cover both.)

If you can produce those four answers without translating each word, the avoir past is becoming a usable tool rather than a museum exhibit.

For another rehearsal, change every sentence in the dialogue from *je* to *nous*. Only the auxiliary changes: *j'ai trouvé* becomes *nous avons trouvé*. The participle stays stable with *avoir*, which is a useful calm corner in French grammar.
