# *Vous*, *Tu*, and the Grammar of Courtesy

French has two words for “you,” and choosing between them is a social decision as well as a grammatical one. The grammar is simple. The social rules take longer to learn, so this chapter explains both: how the forms work and why the choice matters.

## *Vous* is the respectful choice

***Vous*** is the polite form of “you.” It is also the plural form, which is why French speakers use it when addressing several people. Use *vous* with strangers, shopkeepers, older people, people in authority, and anyone you have just met. Its grammar follows the plural pattern: *vous êtes* (“you are”), *vous avez* (“you have”).

## *Tu* is the familiar choice

***Tu*** is the informal singular “you.” Use it with close friends, family, children, and colleagues who have suggested it. The verb changes: *tu es*, *tu as*. Switching from *vous* to *tu* is usually a decision made by the older or more senior person, so wait until you are invited.

## Possessives follow the same logic

The choice affects possessives too. With *vous*, you say *votre* (your): *votre nom*, *votre adresse*. With *tu*, you say *ton* or *ta*: *ton nom*, *ta voiture*. Learning *vous* first is the safe route; it lets you address everyone correctly before you start using *tu*.

## *S'il vous plaît* and *s'il te plaît*

The informal version of *s'il vous plaît* is ***s'il te plaît***. Both are pronounced with the *s'il* running into the next word, so *s'il vous plaît* sounds like *sil vou plè*. Use the formal version with strangers and the informal one with friends.

## *Enchanté* agrees with the speaker

*Enchanté* is an adjective describing you, so it changes for gender in writing: ***enchanté*** for a man and ***enchantée*** for a woman. The sound is the same. In speech you will rarely hear the difference, but the spelling shows the adjective agreement.

## *Je vous en prie*

When someone thanks you, the formal reply is ***je vous en prie***, meaning “you are welcome.” The word *en* refers back to the thing being thanked for. Everyday speech uses *de rien*, but *je vous en prie* is more formal and is appreciated in shops and offices.

The grammar gives you two registers. Start with *vous* and let the other person decide when to move to *tu*.
