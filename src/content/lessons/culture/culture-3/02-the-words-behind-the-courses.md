# The Words Behind the Courses

The meal vocabulary is short, but its grammar shows up in every menu and every toast. Understanding the gender of each noun and the position of each adjective will help you read a menu confidently and say the right phrase at the right time.

## Gender decides the article

Every French noun has a gender, and the article must match it. *L'entrée* is feminine, so the singular indefinite is *une entrée*. *Le plat* is masculine, *le fromage* is masculine, and *le dessert* is masculine. The gender is not always obvious from the word's meaning, so learn each noun with its article.

## The adjective follows the noun

In *le plat principal*, the adjective *principal* comes after the noun, which is the usual French pattern. The masculine ending *-al* changes to *-ale* for a feminine noun, such as *la salade principale* in a menu that offers a salad as the main event. The position is the important habit to acquire.

## *C'est* plus an adjective

Describing a meal uses a simple frame: *C'est* plus an adjective. *C'est convivial* means “it is warm and sociable.” *C'est délicieux* means “it is delicious.” Because the frame is fixed, you can describe almost any meal without memorising extra structures.

## *Bon appétit* is a fixed phrase

*Bon appétit* is not built from parts you can rearrange. It is a set expression, said before eating. *Bon* is the adjective, *appétit* the noun, and they are used together every time. Pronounce it as a single phrase, roughly *bon a-pé-ti*, with the final *t* silent.

## Pronouncing *convivial*

*Convivial* is pronounced *kon-vi-vyal*, with the final *l* sounded and no silent letters at the end. It is a useful word because it describes a meal, a room, or a person, and the same frame works for all three.

## Pronouncing the courses

*Entrée* is *ã-tré*, with the nasal vowel. *Fromage* is *fro-maj*, and the final *e* is silent. *Dessert* is *dé-sèr*. Spend a moment practising each one before the dinner, because the host will hear the difference if you hesitate.

Once you know the gender and the frame, the menu reads more like a sentence than a list.
