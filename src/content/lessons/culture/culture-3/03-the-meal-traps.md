# The Meal Traps

English speakers arrive at a French table with reasonable habits that happen to be wrong here. The mistakes are mostly about vocabulary and timing. None are catastrophic, but each one is noticed, so it is worth learning the corrections before the meal starts.

## Trap 1: ordering the main course as *entrée*

This is the classic error. In American usage, *entree* means the main dish. In France, ***l'entrée*** is the starter. If you ask for *une entrée* expecting a main course, you will receive a small plate and a puzzled waiter. Ask for *le plat* when you mean the main course.

## Trap 2: confusing *plat* and *assiette*

*Un plat* is a dish, both the food and the course. ***Une assiette*** is a plate, the object you eat from. A menu offering *un plat du jour* gives you a dish of the day, not a plate.

## Trap 3: expecting dessert before cheese

Some guests expect the cheese course to be skipped or served with dessert. In traditional meals, the cheese arrives first and dessert follows. If you are not interested in cheese, say *Non merci, je ne prends pas de fromage* and continue. Declining is accepted; reversing the order is not something you can request.

## Trap 4: starting before *bon appétit*

Do not begin eating before the host or the person serving says *Bon appétit*. It is the signal that the meal has officially started, and beginning early is a visible error. If you notice you have started too soon, say *Oups, j'ai commencé trop tôt* and smile. The table will forgive you.

## Trap 5: asking for a digestif too casually

A digestif is a strong drink served after the meal, and you can refuse it. Ask with *Vous prenez un digestif ?* if you are the host, or *Je veux bien un petit digestif* if you would like one. Do not ask for it as if it were a dessert.

The meal is structured, and the structure is part of the pleasure. Following it signals that you understand the occasion as well as the food.
