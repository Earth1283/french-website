# Money, Tipping, and Paying

Paying is where small cultural differences show. This chapter covers how to ask, what to expect, and what "service compris" really means.

## How to pay

| Question | Meaning |
|---|---|
| *Vous payez comment ?* | How are you paying? |
| *Par carte ou en espèces ?* | By card or cash? |
| *Je peux payer par carte ?* | Can I pay by card? |
| *Vous acceptez les cartes ?* | Do you accept cards? |
| *Sans contact* | contactless |

Cards are widely accepted, but some small businesses set a **minimum amount** (often a few euros), and some only take cash. It is wise to carry a little cash.

## Cash vocabulary

- *les espèces* — cash
- *un billet* — a banknote
- *une pièce* — a coin
- *la monnaie* — change (coins)
- *faire l'appoint* — to pay the exact amount

> *Vous avez la monnaie ?* — Do you have change?
>
> *Désolé, je n'ai pas la monnaie.* — Sorry, I don't have change.

Bakeries and small cafés may grumble if you offer a large note for a small purchase.

## *Service compris*

In France, **the service charge is included in menu prices** by law. The phrase *service compris* means "service included." You are not obliged to tip.

A small extra is a gesture of appreciation, not an obligation:

- a few coins on the table for good service
- rounding up in a café
- about 5 to 10 percent in a restaurant when it was excellent

Tipping 15 to 20 percent as in some countries would be generous but unnecessary.

## Counter versus table

In many cafés, the **same coffee costs less at the counter** (*au comptoir*) than at a table (*en salle*) or on the terrace (*en terrasse*). The price list sometimes shows three columns. Sitting down pays for the table and the time you spend there.

## The bill

French restaurants do not bring the bill unprompted. Staff assume you want to stay. Ask for it:

> *L'addition, s'il vous plaît.* — The bill, please.

Waving or catching the waiter's eye is also fine.

## Splitting

*On partage l'addition ?* ("Shall we split the bill?") is natural among friends. Ask for *des additions séparées* if you want separate checks, ideally before ordering.
