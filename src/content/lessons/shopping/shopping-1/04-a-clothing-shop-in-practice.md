# A Clothing Shop in Practice

This is a complete exchange in a small clothing shop in Lille. You are looking for a jacket, the first size is wrong, and you find one you like. Read it aloud, then cover the English and explain what each line achieves.

> **You:** *Bonjour. Je cherche une veste.*
>
> **Assistant:** *Bien sûr. Quelle taille ?*
>
> **You:** *Avez-vous ça en taille 40 ?*
>
> **Assistant:** *Oui, voilà. Vous voulez essayer ?*
>
> **You:** *Oui, je peux essayer ça ?*
>
> **Assistant:** *Bien sûr. Les cabines sont là-bas.*
>
> **You:** *Merci. ... C'est trop grand.*
>
> **Assistant:** *Vous voulez une taille en dessous ?*
>
> **You:** *Oui, s'il vous plaît. Ça me va. Je vais la prendre.*

You opened with *Je cherche*, named the size, asked to try, and gave a single clear verdict on the fit. When the size was wrong, you did not explain at length. You asked for the next option and then closed the sale with *Je vais la prendre*.

## What to say if the size is not there

If the shop has nothing in your size, ask for the alternative:

> *Vous l'avez dans une autre taille ?* — Do you have it in another size?

Or, if you want a different colour, *Vous l'avez dans une autre couleur ?* The pronoun *l'* stands for *la veste*. You can also say *Je reviendrai peut-être* (“I may come back”), which is polite and leaves the door open without commitment.

## When you do not want it

If the item is simply wrong, use *Ce n'est pas ce que je cherche*, and move on. You are under no obligation to buy. Thank the staff member with *Merci, bonne journée*, and leave the way you came in.
