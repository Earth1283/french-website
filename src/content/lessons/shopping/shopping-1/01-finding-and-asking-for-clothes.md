# Finding Clothes and Asking for the Right Size

Shopping in France is quieter than in many places. Staff tend to leave you alone until you want something, and then they are very helpful. Your job is to ask clearly, with one or two phrases, and let them do the searching.

## Start with *Je cherche*

> *Je cherche une veste.* — I'm looking for a jacket.
>
> *Je cherche une chemise.* — I'm looking for a shirt.

*Je cherche* is the present tense of *chercher*, “to look for,” and it is the most natural opening. *Je regarde* means “I'm just looking,” which is the polite reply if someone asks *Vous cherchez quelque chose ?* and you do not need help yet. Use *Je regarde, merci* to end the conversation, and *Je cherche* when you need assistance.

## Asking about size

> *Avez-vous ça en taille 40 ?* — Do you have this in size 40?

*Taille* is the word for size, and *ça* means “this” or “that” in everyday speech. *Avez-vous ça en...* is the frame for asking about a specific item in a specific size or colour. French sizes are usually numerical, so know the size in French terms before you go. Use a size chart on the shop's website if you are unsure, and ask for help with a fitting if the chart is not clear.

## Trying it on

> *Je peux essayer ça ?* — Can I try this on?

*Essayer* means to try, and in a shop it means to try on. The fitting rooms are *les cabines d'essayage*. If you are not sure where they are, point and ask *C'est où, les cabines ?* A staff member will show you.

## Telling them how it fits

> *C'est trop grand.* — It's too big.
>
> *C'est trop petit.* — It's too small.

*Trop* means “too much,” placed before the adjective. *Assez* means “enough.” *C'est assez grand* means “it's big enough,” and it is the phrase for a fitting that works. With *c'est*, the adjective stays in the masculine form, so *C'est trop grand* is correct even for a feminine item such as *la veste*.
