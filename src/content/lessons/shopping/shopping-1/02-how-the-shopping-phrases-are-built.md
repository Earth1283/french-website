# How Shopping Phrases Are Built

Shopping French is mostly three grammar tools: the verb *chercher*, the pronoun *le* or *la* that stands in for a noun, and the phrase *ce que*. Learn those and you can build sentences the vocabulary list does not cover.

## *Chercher* and *trouver*

*Chercher* means to look for, and *trouver* means to find. *Je cherche* describes the search; *Je trouve* describes the result. Both are regular in the first-person present, so you only need to change the stem once you have learned them. In a shop, *Je cherche* is almost always the one you want.

## *Le* and *la* stand in for the item

*Je vais le prendre* means “I'll take it.” The *le* refers back to a masculine noun that was already mentioned, such as *le pull* or *le manteau*. For a feminine item, use *la*: *Je vais la prendre* (“I'll take it,” for *la veste*). For a plural, use *les*. Pronouns of this kind come before the verb, which is why *le* sits in front of *prendre*.

## *Ça* as a neutral pronoun

*Je peux essayer ça ?* uses *ça* for “this” or “that” without a gender. It is informal and everyday. In more formal settings, *cela* is more precise, but in a shop *ça* is the natural word and you will be understood.

## *Ce que* means “what”

*Ce n'est pas ce que je cherche* means “that's not what I'm looking for.” The construction *ce que* introduces a relative clause, and it is the standard way to say “what” in this sense. The phrase *ce qui* is used when the word is the subject of the clause, so you do not need it here.

## The negation around *ce que*

Notice that *ne... pas* wraps around *c'est*, becoming *ce n'est pas*. The *ne* elides before the vowel. In relaxed speech, you may hear *c'est pas ce que je cherche*, which is common and understood.

## Adjective agreement after *trop*

*Trop grand* in the masculine form is the default with *c'est*. *La veste est trop grande* agrees with the feminine noun. Both are correct, but learn the agreed forms as well, because you will need them when describing a specific item.
