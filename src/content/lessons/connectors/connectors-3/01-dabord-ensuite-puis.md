# D'abord, Ensuite, Puis: Telling a Story in Order

Once you can name causes and contrasts, the next skill is sequencing: walking a listener through a series of events, steps, or arguments in a clear order. French uses a small toolkit of sequencing words, and once you have them down, even a rambling story sounds organized.

## D'abord: setting the starting point

*D'abord* means "first" or "at first," and it opens a sequence, marking the very first step or event.

> *D'abord, on va à la boulangerie.*
>
> First, we're going to the bakery.

It works for recipes, instructions, stories, and arguments alike: *D'abord, laissez-moi vous expliquer la situation* ("First, let me explain the situation"). Whatever comes next needs a matching word to continue the sequence — that's where *ensuite* and *puis* come in.

## Ensuite and puis: the middle steps

*Ensuite* and *puis* both mean "next" or "then," and for most everyday purposes they're interchangeable. You can chain several of them together to walk through multiple steps.

> *On visite le Louvre, ensuite on déjeune, puis on se promène le long de la Seine.*
>
> We visit the Louvre, then we have lunch, then we walk along the Seine.

If there's a subtle difference, it's that *puis* feels a touch quicker and more literary, often used to snap from one event to the next without much elaboration, while *ensuite* can carry slightly more weight as a full transitional word. In practice, native speakers mix them freely within the same story, partly just to avoid repeating the same word over and over.

*Puis* has a second life outside of sequencing, too: paired with *et*, *et puis* means something closer to "and besides" or "and anyway," adding a casual extra reason to whatever was just said.

> *Je ne veux pas sortir ce soir. Et puis, j'ai pas le temps.*
>
> I don't want to go out tonight. And besides, I don't have time.

## Building a full sequence

Put the pieces together and you get a clean, ordered account of events — the exact opposite of the disconnected, telegram-style sentences a beginner produces.

> *D'abord, j'ai appelé le médecin. Ensuite, j'ai pris rendez-vous. Puis, j'ai attendu deux semaines.*
>
> First, I called the doctor. Then, I made an appointment. Then, I waited two weeks.

## What's still missing

Notice that none of these three words tell you how the story ends — they just move it forward. For that, French reaches for two very different words that both claim to mean "finally," and mixing them up is one of the most common mistakes at this level. That trap is next.
