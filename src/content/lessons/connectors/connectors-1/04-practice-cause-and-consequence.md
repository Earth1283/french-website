# Practice: Cause and Consequence in the Wild

Time to see all seven connectors from this lesson working together in realistic exchanges, then catch the mistakes a learner would actually make.

## At the office

> **Léa:** *Pourquoi tu es en retard ?*
>
> **Marc:** *Parce que le métro était bloqué. Comme j'habite loin, j'ai dû prendre un taxi.*
>
> **Léa:** *Ah, c'est pourquoi tu es essoufflé !*
>
> **Marc:** *Exactement. Et puisque j'étais déjà en retard, j'ai couru depuis la station.*

Marc answers a direct question with *parce que*, then opens a new sentence with *comme* to explain a background fact. Léa uses *c'est pourquoi* to name the visible consequence — his breathlessness. Marc closes with *puisque*, treating his lateness as something they both already know about.

## At a dinner party

> **Sami:** *Ce restaurant est excellent, grâce à son nouveau chef.*
>
> **Inès:** *Vraiment ? Moi, j'ai dû annuler ma réservation la semaine dernière à cause d'une grève des transports.*
>
> **Sami:** *Ah oui, alors tu n'as pas pu goûter le menu. C'est dommage, car il change chaque mois.*

Sami praises the chef with *grâce à* (a positive cause), Inès blames a strike with *à cause de* (a negative cause), and Sami closes with *car* — mid-sentence, formal, never at the front — to add a justifying detail.

## Spot the mistake

Each sentence below breaks a rule from this lesson. Find the error before checking the answer.

1. *Car il pleuvait, nous sommes restés à la maison.*
2. *J'ai perdu mon travail grâce à la crise économique.*
3. *Comme je suis fatigué.* (as a full, standalone answer to "Pourquoi tu pars ?")

**Answers:**

1. *Car* can never open a sentence. Fix it with *comme*: *Comme il pleuvait, nous sommes restés à la maison.*
2. Losing a job is bad news, so *grâce à* is the wrong tool — it implies gratitude. Use *à cause de* instead: *J'ai perdu mon travail à cause de la crise économique.*
3. *Comme* needs to lead a full sentence with a result clause, not stand alone as a short answer. *Parce que je suis fatigué* is the natural reply here.

## Self-check

Before moving on, make sure you can:

- [ ] Explain why *car* never starts a sentence — **because it links two independent clauses; the first clause has to come first.**
- [ ] Build a sentence where the cause comes before the result, using *comme* — **e.g. *Comme il faisait froid, on est rentrés.***
- [ ] Choose between *grâce à* and *à cause de* based on whether the outcome was good or bad — **grâce à = positive, à cause de = negative/neutral.**
- [ ] Use *puisque* to point at a reason your listener already knows about — **e.g. *Puisque tu parles français, tu peux m'aider.***

Five short sentences from this lesson can now become one — and it will actually sound like something a person would say.
