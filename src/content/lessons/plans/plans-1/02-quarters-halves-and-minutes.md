# Quarters, Halves, and Minutes

Once you can say the hour, minutes are an addition or subtraction. French uses *et* ("and") up to the half hour, then *moins* ("minus") for the minutes before the next hour.

## The quarter and the half

| Time | French |
|---|---|
| 3:15 | *Il est trois heures et quart.* |
| 3:30 | *Il est trois heures et demie.* |
| 3:45 | *Il est quatre heures moins le quart.* |

*Moins le quart* means "a quarter to." Look at 3:45: it is a quarter to four, so the hour you name is four, not three.

## Minutes after the hour

Just add the number after *heures*:

> *Il est trois heures cinq.* — 3:05
>
> *Il est trois heures dix.* — 3:10
>
> *Il est trois heures vingt.* — 3:20
>
> *Il est trois heures vingt-cinq.* — 3:25

For the first half hour, speakers use the number directly. *Et quart* and *et demie* are the only two pieces that take *et*.

## Minutes before the hour

After the half hour, name the next hour and subtract:

> *Il est quatre heures moins vingt-cinq.* — 3:35
>
> *Il est quatre heures moins vingt.* — 3:40
>
> *Il est quatre heures moins dix.* — 3:50
>
> *Il est quatre heures moins cinq.* — 3:55

You can also hear the direct form (*trois heures trente-cinq*), especially in written timetables and announcements.

## *Demi* versus *demie*

The half hour changes form depending on what it follows:

- *trois heures et demie* — feminine, because *heure* is feminine
- *midi et demi* — masculine, because *midi* is masculine
- *minuit et demi* — masculine

Spoken, *demi* and *demie* sound identical, so it only matters when you write. It is a good test of careful writing.

## Quick conversions

Practice by translating:

1. 2:15 → ___
2. 7:30 → ___
3. 9:45 → ___
4. 12:30 → ___
5. 5:50 → ___

**Answers:** 1. *deux heures et quart*. 2. *sept heures et demie*. 3. *dix heures moins le quart*. 4. *midi et demi*. 5. *six heures moins dix*.

## A tip for listening

When you hear *moins*, the number is **ahead** of the real hour. A speaker saying *huit heures moins le quart* means 7:45, not 8:45. This is the single most common mistake learners make when making appointments.
