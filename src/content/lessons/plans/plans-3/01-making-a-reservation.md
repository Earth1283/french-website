# Making a Reservation Without Panic

Booking a table or a room in France is less stressful than it sounds, but the phone call is where most people freeze. Restaurants fill up quickly, and staff often answer between other tasks. Know the core phrases before you dial, and the rest of the exchange becomes predictable.

## The opening sentence

> *Bonjour, je voudrais réserver une table pour deux.* — Hello, I'd like to book a table for two.

That one sentence covers the greeting, the request and the party size. After it, expect a short series of questions:

- *Pour combien de personnes ?* — For how many people?
- *Pour quelle heure ?* — For what time?
- *Au nom de qui ?* — In whose name?

Hotels follow the same pattern: *Je voudrais réserver une chambre pour deux nuits.* — I'd like to book a room for two nights.

## Giving your name

When asked for a name, reply with *C'est au nom de…* and the surname. *C'est au nom de Dupont.* — It's under the name Dupont.

You do not need *Je m'appelle* on the phone. The staff need the booking name, not an introduction, and a clear surname saves time.

## Cancelling and confirming

If plans change, say *Je voudrais annuler ma réservation.* — I'd like to cancel my booking. To be certain it went through, ask *Pouvez-vous confirmer la réservation ?* — Can you confirm the booking?

Many restaurants now take a card number to hold a table, and cancellation policies have tightened. Ask about them at the start of the call, not after you have failed to show up.
