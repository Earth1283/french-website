# A Booking Call, Start to Finish

Here is a realistic phone exchange. Read the French first, then cover the translation and explain each line to yourself.

> **You:** *Bonjour, je voudrais réserver une table pour deux, ce soir.*
>
> **Staff:** *Pour quelle heure ?*
>
> **You:** *Vers vingt heures, si possible.*
>
> **Staff:** *Je suis désolé, à vingt heures nous sommes complets. Il reste une table à dix-neuf heures trente.*
>
> **You:** *D'accord, je la prends. C'est au nom de Dupont.*
>
> **Staff:** *Très bien. Pouvez-vous me donner un numéro de téléphone ?*

*Je la prends* uses *la* because *table* is feminine, and it points back to the table just offered. *Vers* is a quick way to say "around," which gives the restaurant room to offer alternatives.

## When it goes wrong

If you miss the time, say *Pardon, pouvez-vous répéter ?* If the only slot is too late, ask *Il n'y a rien de plus tôt ?* — Is there nothing earlier? If plans collapse, say *Je voudrais annuler ma réservation, finalement.* Be direct and brief; the staff member is probably standing at a crowded desk.

## The same call at a hotel

The hotel version changes only the nouns and the questions. *Je voudrais réserver une chambre pour deux nuits.* — I would like to book a room for two nights. The receptionist may ask *Pour quelle date ?* — For which date? or *Vous arrivez quand ?* — When are you arriving? Answer with the date, then confirm the name as before.

## The pattern

Greet, request, answer the questions, give your name, confirm. Every call follows the same five steps, even when the details change.
