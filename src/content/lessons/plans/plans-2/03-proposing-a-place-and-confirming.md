# Proposing a Place and Confirming

Once you have agreed on a time, the next step is the place. The question *On se retrouve où ?* does most of the work, and the confirmation phrase *Ça vous convient ?* closes the loop. Both are short, and both are polite.

## Asking where

> *On se retrouve où ?* — Where shall we meet?

*Se retrouver* means "to meet up" or "to find each other again." It is a reflexive verb, which is why you need *se* in front of it: *on se retrouve*. The subject here is *on*, which in everyday speech means "we." Do not replace *on* with *nous* in relaxed conversation.

In the answer, you will often hear a place name or a landmark: *devant la gare*, "in front of the station," or *au café*, "at the café." Use *devant* for in front of and *au* for "at the" before a masculine noun.

## Confirming the time

> *Ça vous convient ?* — Does that work for you?

This is the formal version. In a professional email or a call, use it to check a time. For friends, the informal version is *Ça te va ?*, which uses the *tu* form.

*Convenir* takes an indirect object, which is why you need *vous* in the phrase. The sentence works like "does this suit you?" It is a question that invites a yes or a counter-proposal.

## Responding

> *Oui, ça me convient.* — Yes, that suits me.
>
> *Non, je préfère vers dix-neuf heures.* — No, I'd prefer around seven.

*Vers* means "around" or "about," which is useful when you cannot be exact. Propose an alternative time plainly. A counter-proposal is normal and expected.

## Watch the pronoun

In the answer above, *ça me convient* uses *me* because the verb *convenir* takes an indirect object. Be careful not to say *ça convient moi*, which is a common mistake.
