# Saying When You Are Free

To agree on a time, you need to describe your availability. The core construction is *je suis libre à* plus a time, and the rest of the conversation builds from it. This chapter covers the grammar behind the phrases.

## *Être libre* and the time

> *Je suis libre à dix-huit heures.* — I'm free at six p.m.
>
> *Je suis libre à midi.* — I'm free at noon.

The preposition *à* introduces the exact time. You do not need *heures* when you say *à midi*, because *midi* is already a time of day. For a clock time, use *à dix-huit heures* or *à 18h*, which are the same in writing.

## Time expressions

> *Ce soir* — tonight
>
> *Cet après-midi* — this afternoon
>
> *La semaine prochaine* — next week

*Ce* becomes *cet* before a vowel sound, which is why you say *cet après-midi* and *cet hiver*. Only the form changes; the meaning stays the same. Do not write *ce après-midi*.

*La semaine prochaine* is literally "the next week." *Prochain* agrees with the feminine noun *semaine*, so it takes the feminine form *prochaine*.

## Busy, not unavailable

> *Je suis occupé(e) demain.* — I'm busy tomorrow.

*Être occupé* is the everyday way to say you have plans. It is softer than a flat refusal. You can then offer an alternative: *Je suis libre jeudi.* ("I'm free on Thursday.")

## A short exchange

> **You:** *Je suis occupé demain. Je suis libre jeudi soir.*
>
> **Friend:** *Jeudi, c'est parfait.*

Keep the time frames short. Two or three words usually carry enough meaning, and it is more useful to be clear than to be grammatically complete.
