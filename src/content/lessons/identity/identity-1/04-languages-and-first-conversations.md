# Languages and First Conversations

Telling people what you speak, and what you do not yet speak, is one of the most social things you can do in a new language. This chapter puts the lesson together into short, usable conversations.

## Talking about languages

The verb is *parler*, and languages are masculine nouns with no article after *parler*:

> *Je parle anglais.* — I speak English.
>
> *Je parle un peu français.* — I speak a little French.
>
> *Je ne parle pas allemand.* — I don't speak German.

Natural variants use *de*: *Je parle un peu de français*. Both are correct.

## Helpful phrases for the learner

You will need these in every real conversation:

| French | Meaning |
|---|---|
| *Je comprends.* | I understand. |
| *Je ne comprends pas.* | I don't understand. |
| *Pouvez-vous répéter ?* | Can you repeat? |
| *Plus lentement, s'il vous plaît.* | More slowly, please. |
| *Comment dit-on… en français ?* | How do you say… in French? |
| *Qu'est-ce que ça veut dire ?* | What does that mean? |

*Plus lentement* is probably the most valuable. French speakers often speed up when they sense you are struggling, and this phrase politely asks them to do the opposite.

## A first conversation

> **—** *Bonjour, je m'appelle Sam. Vous êtes d'où ?*
>
> **—** *Je viens du Canada, de Montréal. Et vous ?*
>
> **—** *Je suis australien, mais j'habite à Paris.*
>
> **—** *Vous parlez très bien français !*
>
> **—** *Merci, je parle un peu. Je suis en train d'apprendre.*

This short exchange uses *de* with a country, *à* with a city, a nationality without an article, and a language without an article. Each form comes from this lesson.

## A note on compliments

When a French speaker says *Vous parlez très bien français*, the polite answer is not *non, non*. Say *Merci, c'est gentil*, and you will be remembered as gracious. Self-deprecating replies are normal, but overdoing them can sound like fishing for a second compliment.

## Next steps

Practice by writing your own three-line introduction: where you are from, where you live, and what you speak. Say it aloud five times. Then adjust it to the person in front of you.
