# Saying Where You Live: *à, en, au, aux*

Telling someone where you live uses the same logic as saying where you come from, but the prepositions change. There are four, and one pattern covers almost everything.

## The pattern

| What you are naming | Preposition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| a **city** | *à* | *J'habite à Paris.* |
| a **feminine country** | *en* | *J'habite en France.* |
| a **masculine country** | *au* | *J'habite au Canada.* |
| a **plural country** | *aux* | *J'habite aux États-Unis.* |
| a **vowel-initial masculine country** | *en* | *J'habite en Iran.* |

The last row is the surprise: countries that begin with a vowel use *en*, regardless of gender. *En Israël*, *en Iran*, *en Équateur*.

## Compare with "coming from"

| Meaning | Feminine | Masculine | Plural |
|---|---|---|---|
| I live in… | *en France* | *au Canada* | *aux États-Unis* |
| I come from… | *de France* | *du Canada* | *des États-Unis* |

The same three categories, two different sets of words. If you can name the category, the preposition follows.

## Beyond countries

Larger regions and districts follow their own habits:

- **Regions:** *en Bretagne, en Normandie, en Provence* (feminine)
- **US states and Canadian provinces:** *en Californie, au Texas, au Québec*
- **Islands:** most use *à* (*à Tahiti, à Madagascar*), but *en Corse*, *en Sicile*

You do not need to memorize all of these. When in doubt, say *J'habite dans la région de…* or just give the city.

## The verb *habiter*

*Habiter* is a regular *-er* verb, and the *h* is a mute h, so it elides:

| | |
|---|---|
| *j'habite* | I live |
| *tu habites* | you live |
| *il/elle habite* | he/she lives |
| *nous habitons* | we live |
| *vous habitez* | you live (formal/plural) |
| *ils/elles habitent* | they live |

Note *habite*, *habites*, and *habitent* sound identical. The context tells you the person.

## Putting it together

> *J'habite à Toronto, au Canada, mais je viens de France.*
>
> I live in Toronto, in Canada, but I come from France.

That one sentence uses *à*, *au*, and *de* correctly. Master it and you have the whole system.
