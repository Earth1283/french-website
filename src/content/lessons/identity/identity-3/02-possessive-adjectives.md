# *Mon, ma, mes*: Possessive Adjectives

French possessives agree with the thing owned, not with the owner. This reverses the English logic and is the reason *son frère* can mean "his brother" or "her brother."

## The full table

| Owner | Masc. sing. | Fem. sing. | Plural |
|---|---|---|---|
| my | *mon* | *ma* | *mes* |
| your (informal) | *ton* | *ta* | *tes* |
| his / her / its | *son* | *sa* | *ses* |
| our | *notre* | *notre* | *nos* |
| your (formal/plural) | *votre* | *votre* | *vos* |
| their | *leur* | *leur* | *leurs* |

## The rule that surprises English speakers

*Son*, *sa*, and *ses* depend on the **gender and number of the object**, not on whether the owner is male or female.

> *Marie parle à son frère.* — Marie speaks to her brother.
>
> *Paul parle à son frère.* — Paul speaks to his brother.
>
> *Marie parle à sa sœur.* — Marie speaks to her sister.
>
> *Paul parle à sa sœur.* — Paul speaks to his sister.

*Son* goes with *frère* (masculine) whether Marie or Paul is the owner. To clarify, French can add *à elle* or *à lui*: *sa mère à elle*. You will only need this in confusing situations.

## The vowel exception

Before a feminine noun that starts with a vowel, French uses the **masculine** form to avoid two vowels clashing:

- *mon amie* (not *ma amie*)
- *ton école* (not *ta école*)
- *son enfance*

This is the same sound-based logic as elision. Do not worry that *mon amie* "sounds masculine"; the noun's gender is unchanged.

## Singular owners with plural things

*Mes parents*, *tes amis*, *ses frères*. The plural form does not distinguish gender: *mes* serves both *mes frères* and *mes sœurs*.

## Our, your, their

These forms are easier: they have only two shapes, singular and plural.

- *notre mère, notre père* → *nos parents*
- *votre sœur, votre frère* → *vos enfants*
- *leur fille* → *leurs enfants*

## Check yourself

1. ___ mère (my)
2. ___ amie (my, a female friend)
3. ___ parents (her)
4. ___ enfants (our)
5. ___ frère (his)

**Answers:** 1. *ma*. 2. *mon*. 3. *ses*. 4. *nos*. 5. *son*.
