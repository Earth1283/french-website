# Family Vocabulary: From *maman* to *beau-frère*

Family words are some of the first vocabulary you use with a new friend, because "do you have brothers and sisters?" is an easy question to ask and answer. This chapter lays out the main terms with their feminine pairs.

## The core family

| Masculine | Feminine | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| *le père* | *la mère* | father / mother |
| *le frère* | *la sœur* | brother / sister |
| *le fils* | *la fille* | son / daughter |
| *le mari* | *la femme* | husband / wife |
| *le grand-père* | *la grand-mère* | grandfather / grandmother |
| *l'oncle* | *la tante* | uncle / aunt |
| *le cousin* | *la cousine* | cousin |
| *le neveu* | *la nièce* | nephew / niece |

*La femme* means both "woman" and "wife." *Ma femme* is "my wife," and context never confuses it.

## Informal and affectionate forms

| Formal | Informal |
|---|---|
| *mère, père* | *maman, papa* |
| *grand-mère* | *mamie, mémé* |
| *grand-père* | *papi, pépé* |

Adults say *maman* and *papa* all their lives. It is not childish.

## In-laws

French adds *beau-* and *belle-* for relatives by marriage:

- *le beau-père* — father-in-law (or stepfather)
- *la belle-mère* — mother-in-law (or stepmother)
- *le beau-frère* — brother-in-law
- *la belle-sœur* — sister-in-law

The same words also mean stepfather and stepmother, so context clarifies. *Demi-frère* and *demi-sœur* are half-siblings.

## The "parents" trap

*Les parents* means "the parents": a mother and father. It does not mean "relatives."

> *Mes parents habitent à Lyon.* — My parents live in Lyon.

For relatives in general, use *la famille* or *les proches* (close ones). A learner who says *Je visite mes parents* to mean aunts and uncles will be understood to mean mother and father.

## Plural forms

- *les enfants* — children (also "kids")
- *les grands-parents* — grandparents
- *les frères et sœurs* — siblings

Mixed groups default to the masculine plural: *mes cousins* can include female cousins.

## Practice

Say your own family aloud: *J'ai un frère, une sœur, deux cousins…* The structure *J'ai* + number + noun repeats endlessly. Fluency with family words comes from saying real sentences about real people, not from reading lists.
