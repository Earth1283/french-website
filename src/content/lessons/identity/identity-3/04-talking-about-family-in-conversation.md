# Talking About Family in Conversation

Asking about family is a normal and friendly topic in France. Questions about brothers, sisters, and children come up early, and they are a safe way to keep a conversation going. This chapter gives you the questions and the shape of a natural exchange.

## Questions you can ask

| Question | Meaning |
|---|---|
| *Vous avez des frères et sœurs ?* | Do you have siblings? |
| *Vous avez des enfants ?* | Do you have children? |
| *Il / Elle s'appelle comment ?* | What's his / her name? |
| *Il / Elle a quel âge ?* | How old is he / she? |
| *Vous habitez près de votre famille ?* | Do you live near your family? |
| *Vous êtes d'une grande famille ?* | Do you come from a big family? |

*Tu* versions: *Tu as des frères et sœurs ? Tu as des enfants ?* Use them with friends, young people, and anyone who has invited you to say *tu*.

## Answering naturally

Don't answer with a bare number. Add one detail:

> *J'ai un frère et une sœur. Mon frère habite à Londres, et ma sœur est encore étudiante.*
>
> I have a brother and a sister. My brother lives in London, and my sister is still a student.

This answer uses *j'ai*, a possessive, and a short description. It is enough for a conversation, and it invites a follow-up.

## A model dialogue

> **—** *Vous avez des enfants ?*
>
> **—** *Oui, j'ai deux filles. Elles ont sept et dix ans. Et vous ?*
>
> **—** *Moi, je n'ai pas d'enfants, mais j'ai beaucoup de neveux et nièces.*
>
> **—** *Ah, c'est bien ! Vous les voyez souvent ?*

Notice the negative with *d'enfants*, the age with *avoir*, and the quick return question. This is the shape of the conversation you will have dozens of times.

## Sensitive topics

Family questions can touch on loss, divorce, or not having children. If someone's answer is short or sad, follow their lead. A gentle *Ah, je comprends* or *Désolé(e)* is enough.

In France, questions about **salary, religion, and voting** are considered more private than in many other countries. Family and region of origin, on the other hand, are comfortable subjects.

## A final exercise

Write four sentences about your own family using the patterns above: one with a possessive, one with an age, one with a negative, and one with a status word. Read them aloud. If you can say them smoothly, you have this lesson.
