# Saying What You Do: No Article After *être*

English says "I am **a** teacher." French says *Je suis professeur*, with no article. This is the first thing to learn about talking about work, and it applies to professions, nationalities, religions, and political affiliations as well.

## The basic rule

After *être* (to be), a bare profession takes **no article**:

> *Je suis médecin.* — I am a doctor.
>
> *Elle est avocate.* — She is a lawyer.
>
> *Nous sommes étudiants.* — We are students.
>
> *Mon père est boulanger.* — My father is a baker.

The profession works like an adjective, describing who someone is rather than naming a thing.

## When the article returns

Add an adjective or a description, and the article comes back, but you must switch to *c'est* or *il/elle est* depending on structure:

| Pattern | Example |
|---|---|
| *il/elle est* + bare noun | *Elle est infirmière.* |
| *c'est* + article + noun | *C'est une infirmière.* |
| *c'est* + article + adj + noun | *C'est une très bonne infirmière.* |

So *Elle est infirmière* and *C'est une infirmière* both mean "She's a nurse," but the second is used to introduce or point out someone. *C'est une infirmière* is natural when pointing at a photo. *Elle est infirmière* is natural when answering a question about her job.

## Why it feels strange

English speakers keep adding *un* or *une*. Hearing *Je suis un professeur* from a learner is understandable, and it will be corrected gently, but it is a clear learner marker.

Compare these pairs:

- *Je suis professeur.* ✔
- *Je suis un professeur.* ✘ (unless followed by a description)
- *Je suis un bon professeur.* ✔ (with an adjective, the article returns)

## Other things that behave the same way

The no-article rule also covers:

- **Nationalities:** *Je suis canadien.*
- **Religions:** *Elle est catholique.*
- **Status:** *Il est célibataire.*
- **Roles:** *Il est directeur de l'entreprise.*

Once you notice the pattern, it stops feeling like a special rule and starts feeling like how description works in French.

## One short exercise

Add or remove the article as needed:

1. Je suis ___ ingénieur. → no article
2. C'est ___ bon médecin. → *un*
3. Il est ___ avocat. → no article
4. Elle est ___ très bonne actrice. → *une*
