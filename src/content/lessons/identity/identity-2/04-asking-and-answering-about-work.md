# Asking and Answering: *Qu'est-ce que vous faites ?*

The standard question for someone's job has a hidden trap. *Qu'est-ce que vous faites ?* literally means "what are you doing?", which can refer to your job or to what you are doing right now. Context usually decides, but you can remove the doubt.

## Questions that mean "what do you do for a living"

| Question | Register |
|---|---|
| *Qu'est-ce que vous faites dans la vie ?* | formal, clear |
| *Vous faites quoi dans la vie ?* | informal |
| *Qu'est-ce que tu fais comme travail ?* | informal, very clear |
| *Quel est votre métier ?* | formal |
| *Vous travaillez dans quoi ?* | casual |

Adding *dans la vie* ("in life") is the simplest fix. It turns the ambiguous question into a clear one.

## The verb *faire*

*Faire* is irregular, and you need it for this question:

| | |
|---|---|
| *je fais* | I do |
| *tu fais* | you do |
| *il/elle fait* | he/she does |
| *nous faisons* | we do |
| *vous faites* | you do (formal/plural) |
| *ils/elles font* | they do |

Note the *vous faites* ending. It is one of the few verbs that does not end in *-ez*.

## Answering naturally

Short answers work best:

> *Je suis infirmière.*
>
> *Je suis dans l'informatique.* — I'm in IT.
>
> *Je travaille dans une école.* — I work in a school.
>
> *Je suis étudiant en histoire.*

*Je suis dans* + field is the casual shortcut. It sounds natural and saves a verb.

## A dialogue

> **—** *Qu'est-ce que vous faites dans la vie ?*
>
> **—** *Je suis ingénieur. Je travaille chez Airbus, à Toulouse. Et vous ?*
>
> **—** *Je suis à la retraite depuis deux ans. J'étais professeur.*
>
> **—** *Vous avez de la chance !*

*J'étais professeur* uses the imperfect, which you will see later, to say "I used to be a teacher." You can recognize it before you can form it.

## A cultural note

In France, asking someone's job early in a conversation is normal, but conversation often moves to interests, family, and regions quickly. Money questions (*Vous gagnez combien ?*) are much more private than in some English-speaking countries and are best avoided.

Answer simply, then ask back: *Et vous ?* or *Et toi ?* The return question keeps conversation balanced.
