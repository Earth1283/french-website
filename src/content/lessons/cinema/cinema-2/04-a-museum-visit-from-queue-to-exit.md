# A Museum Visit from Queue to Exit

Here is a realistic visit to a large museum, from the moment you reach the ticket desk to the moment you leave. Each exchange does one job: find the price, add the extras, understand the queue, and recover when you misunderstand.

## At the desk

> **You:** *Bonjour. C'est combien, l'entrée ?*
>
> **Staff:** *Quatorze euros. Il y a un supplément pour l'exposition temporaire.*
>
> **You:** *D'accord. Je voudrais l'entrée et un audioguide, s'il vous plaît.*
>
> **Staff:** *En français ou en anglais ?*
>
> **You:** *En anglais, s'il vous plaît.*

The first question gets the base price. The word *supplément* signals an extra charge, so you know the temporary exhibition is not included. The request for the audioguide follows the same frame as before: *je voudrais*, then the noun.

## In the queue

> **Visitor:** *Il y a une grande file d'attente aujourd'hui.*
>
> **You:** *Oui, je vois. Est-ce qu'on peut acheter les billets en ligne ?*
>
> **Visitor:** *Oui, sur le site du musée.*

Do not panic at a long queue. The useful question is whether there is a faster route. *Est-ce qu'on peut* is a polite, impersonal way to ask, and it works in most situations.

## Reading a sign

You pass a sign that says *Interdit de photographier*. You can check whether the rule applies to the whole museum. Ask a guard: *Pardon, la photo est interdite dans toutes les salles ?* If the answer is no, a sign in the room will show the exception.

## When you do not understand

> **Guard:** *Il faut passer par la sortie de secours.*
>
> **You:** *Pardon, je n'ai pas compris. Vous pouvez répéter, s'il vous plaît ?*
>
> **Guard:** *La sortie de secours, là-bas.*
>
> **You:** *D'accord, merci.*

*Il faut* means “one must” or “you must,” and it signals a required instruction. The guard repeated the phrase more slowly, which is what you asked for. If you still cannot understand, point at the map and say *Je suis perdu(e)*.

## The pattern for any visit

Price, extras, queue, rules, and help. Learn the questions in that order and you will manage almost any museum. The French phrases are short because the situations are repetitive, and the museum staff have heard every question before.
