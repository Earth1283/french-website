# The Grammar of *Gratuit* and *Il Y a*

The museum vocabulary is full of small grammar that makes a big difference: noun gender, adjective agreement, and the elisions that French uses to join words together. Understanding these patterns lets you read a sign you have never seen before.

## Gender decides the ending

Every French noun has a gender. *L'entrée* is feminine, so the adjective describing it agrees: *entrée gratuite*. The same rule applies to *une exposition temporaire*: *temporaire* works for both masculine and feminine nouns, so it does not change. *Payant* is masculine and becomes *payante* for a feminine noun, so the sign may read *entrée payante*.

Notice the position too. In French, most descriptive adjectives come after the noun, which is why the sign says *entrée gratuite* rather than *gratuite entrée*.

## Elision: *l'* and *d'*

French drops the final vowel of *le*, *la*, or *de* before a word that starts with a vowel sound. That is why you write *l'entrée*, *l'exposition*, and *une file d'attente* (“a queue of waiting”). Speak the words as one unit. The *de* in *d'attente* becomes *d'* because *attente* starts with a vowel.

## *Il y a* and the long queue

*Il y a* means “there is” or “there are,” and it does not change for plural nouns. Compare:

> *Il y a une grande file d'attente.* — There is a long queue.
>
> *Il y a deux files.* — There are two queues.

*Grande* appears before *file* here, because adjectives of size and quality are frequently placed before the noun in everyday speech. The meaning is “long” in a queue sense, or “big” in general.

## *Interdit de* plus a verb

*Interdit* is the past participle of *interdire*, used as an adjective. The structure is *interdit de* plus an infinitive:

> *Interdit de toucher.* — Do not touch.
>
> *Interdit de photographier.* — No photography.

Once you spot *interdit de*, you can read almost any prohibition sign. Replace the verb to understand another rule.

## Pronunciation check

*Gratuit* is pronounced roughly *gra-tui*, with the final *t* silent. *Payant* is *pay-an*, and the *nt* is silent. Practise the difference between *gratuit* and *gratuite*: in speech, the feminine ending is silent, so the pronunciation is the same. The grammar matters in writing, and in your head when you read the sign.
