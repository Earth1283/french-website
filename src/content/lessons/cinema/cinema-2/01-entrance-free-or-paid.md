# Entrance, Free or Paid: Reading the Museum Door

Museums in France are full of signs that tell you what you may pay, what you may touch, and what you may photograph. You do not need to read every word on them. Learn the handful of phrases that control your visit, and most of the rules will explain themselves.

## *L'entrée*: the door and the ticket

***L'entrée*** has two meanings. It can be the physical entrance, the door you walk through. It can also be the admission price, the cost of getting in. Context usually decides which one you mean:

> *Où est l'entrée ?* — Where is the entrance?
>
> *C'est combien, l'entrée ?* — How much is admission?

When you see *Entrée gratuite* on a sign, it means free admission. When you see *Entrée payante*, admission costs money.

## *Gratuit* and *payant*

These two adjectives are the most useful pair on any museum poster. ***Gratuit*** means free of charge. ***Payant*** means paid, or charged for. Notice that *gratuit* is the masculine form, and it changes to *gratuite* for a feminine noun such as *entrée*.

If you are not sure whether a museum is free, ask directly: *L'entrée est-elle gratuite aujourd'hui ?* (“Is admission free today?”) Free days are common in French national museums, so the question is worth asking before you buy a ticket.

## Ordering the extras

Big museums sell an ***audioguide***, which is spelled the same way in French as in English. To ask for one:

> *Je voudrais un audioguide, s'il vous plaît.* — I would like an audioguide, please.

If you want one in English, you can add *en anglais* after the request.

## Temporary exhibitions

***L'exposition temporaire*** is a show that runs for a limited period, usually on top of the permanent collection. It often needs a separate ticket. Ask: *Est-ce que l'exposition temporaire est incluse ?* (“Is the temporary exhibition included?”) If the answer is no, you will know before you reach the counter.

Read the price, check whether the exhibition is included, and choose the audioguide only if you will use it. The rest of the museum vocabulary follows from those three decisions.
