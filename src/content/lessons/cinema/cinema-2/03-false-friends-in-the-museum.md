# False Friends in the Museum

Museum vocabulary sounds familiar, which is exactly why it causes trouble. The following words look like English, but they mean something else. Most of them are harmless, but a few will send you to the wrong room or the wrong desk.

## *Exposition* is an exhibition, not exposure

In English, “exposition” can mean a showcase or a display, but it can also mean the sun or the light that hits something. In French, ***une exposition*** is an exhibition. If you hear *une exposition temporaire*, it is a show in a gallery, not a sun-related event. A sign that says *Exposition fermée* means the exhibition is closed.

## *Libre* is free as in available, not free of charge

English speakers often reach for *gratuit* when they mean “free” in the sense of “unoccupied.” For a seat or a bench, the correct word is ***libre***:

> *Cette place est-elle libre ?* — Is this seat free?

*Gratuit* means free of charge. *Libre* means available. Confusing them will get you a puzzled look, not a fine, but it is worth getting right.

## *Attendre* means to wait

*Attendre* means “to wait.” It does not mean “to attend.” To attend an event in French, you say *assister à*. So if you want to say “I am waiting in the queue,” say *J'attends dans la file*. Using *attendre* for “attend” will make the sentence about waiting, which is not what you intended.

## *Librairie* is a bookshop

In a museum, you may pass a *librairie*. This is a bookshop, not a library. The library is *la bibliothèque*. Museum shops sell postcards and catalogues in *la librairie-boutique*, and many visitors do not realise they are in the bookshop section until they reach the till.

## *Toucher* is about physical contact

*Toucher* means “to touch.” It does not mean “to contact” someone in the sense of calling or emailing. If you need to contact the staff, use *contacter*. On the sign, *Interdit de toucher* means do not touch the works. The word has nothing to do with feelings or messages.

Learn these few words as exceptions. Most of museum French is transparent, and these are the places where the English shortcut takes you wrong.
