# Buying Tickets from Start to Finish

Here is a complete exchange at a cinema counter. Read it through once, then look at the role of each line: greeting, version, time, number of seats, and a plan for the unexpected.

## The main exchange

> **You:** *Bonsoir. Deux places pour le film américain, s'il vous plaît.*
>
> **Ticket seller:** *En VO ou en VF ?*
>
> **You:** *En VO, s'il vous plaît. À quelle heure est la prochaine séance ?*
>
> **Ticket seller:** *À vingt heures, salle trois.*
>
> **You:** *Salle trois, à vingt heures. Merci, bonne soirée.*

The greeting comes first, as it does everywhere in France. *Le film américain* identifies the film by description, which is useful if you do not know its French title. The seller's question is a simple choice, so answer with the version you want. Repeat the time and the screen back to the seller; it confirms what you heard.

## When the showing is full

Sometimes the answer is different:

> **Ticket seller:** *Désolé, il n'y a plus de places pour cette séance.*
>
> **You:** *Il y a une autre séance ce soir ?*
>
> **Ticket seller:** *Oui, à vingt-deux heures.*
>
> **You:** *Très bien, deux places pour vingt-deux heures.*

*Désolé* is what French staff say before bad news. Do not take it as a rudeness. Your next question is practical: *ce soir* means “this evening,” and it narrows the search.

## When you hear the wrong version

If you arrive at the screen and the film is dubbed when you expected original sound, do not sit there silently. Go back to the staff and say:

> *Pardon, je ne comprends pas. Le film est en VF, non ?*

Use *Pardon* to attract attention and *non ?* to ask for confirmation. If they reply that the film is dubbed, ask for a ticket change if the cinema allows it: *Est-ce que je peux changer de séance ?*

## The reusable pattern

Every cinema purchase follows the same sequence: greet, state the film, choose the version, confirm the time, confirm the seats, and thank the seller. If something goes wrong, ask one clear question and repeat the answer back. A short, accurate exchange is worth more than a long apology.
