# A Room Change in Practice

This is an exchange at a hotel in Nice. The room you were given faces a building site and the air conditioning has opinions. Read it aloud, then cover the English and explain what each French line does.

> **You:** *Bonjour. Pouvez-vous m'aider ? La climatisation ne fonctionne pas.*
>
> **Receptionist:** *Ah, je suis désolée. Je vais vérifier.*
>
> **You:** *Merci. Est-ce que la chambre a une vue sur la mer ?*
>
> **Receptionist:** *Non, malheureusement, la vue est sur la rue.*
>
> **You:** *Je voudrais changer de chambre, si c'est possible.*
>
> **Receptionist:** *Pourquoi ?*
>
> **You:** *Il fait trop chaud, et j'ai besoin de dormir.*
>
> **Receptionist:** *D'accord. Je peux vous proposer la chambre 214.*

You described the problem first, then asked the question, then made the request with *je voudrais*. The reason came when asked, and it was short and true. Honest, specific reasons work better than vague complaints.

## If the answer is no

Staff sometimes cannot move you. Keep the door open with *Est-ce que c'est possible plus tard ?* (“Is it possible later?”) or ask for an alternative: *Pouvez-vous m'apporter un ventilateur ?* A fan is a good fallback, and *un ventilateur* is masculine.

## What to say if it still does not work

If the heating or cooling is still broken, ask for a written confirmation of the fault, which is useful if you are charged for a later problem:

> *Pouvez-vous noter le problème, s'il vous plaît ?* — Could you note the problem, please?

Then, if you want to be more specific, *Le chauffage ne marche pas depuis hier*. The date does more work than the adjective.
