# Asking for What the Room Lacks

A hotel room is a list of small failures waiting to be discovered. The towels are thin, the heating is either on or off, and the view is a wall. Requests are the fix, and French gives you a polite frame for almost all of them.

## The frame: *Pouvez-vous...?*

*Pouvez-vous* plus an infinitive is the most useful request pattern in hotel French:

> *Pouvez-vous apporter des serviettes ?* — Could you bring some towels?
>
> *Pouvez-vous changer les draps ?* — Could you change the sheets?

The verb after *Pouvez-vous* stays in the infinitive, so you never conjugate it. Change the infinitive and you have a new request. *Pouvez-vous vérifier le chauffage ?* (“Could you check the heating?”) works the same way.

## Asking about the view

*Est-ce que la chambre a une vue sur la mer ?* asks whether the room has a sea view. *Une vue sur* is the standard expression for a view of something. English speakers want *view of*, and the *de* is wrong in French. Replace *la mer* with *la cathédrale*, *le jardin* or *la rue* as needed.

## Heating and cooling

*La climatisation* is air conditioning and is feminine. *Le chauffage* is heating and is masculine. Both are common nouns, but the phrases that follow them are the useful part:

> *La climatisation ne fonctionne pas.* — The air conditioning isn't working.
>
> *Le chauffage ne marche pas.* — The heating doesn't work.

## Asking to change rooms

*Je voudrais changer de chambre* means “I'd like to change rooms.” The construction is *changer de*, “to change [something],” which is the one you need here. Compare it with *changer les draps*, where *changer* takes its object directly. *Changer de* is about swapping one thing for another; *changer* on its own is about altering or replacing. Hotel staff will ask you why, so have a reason ready.
