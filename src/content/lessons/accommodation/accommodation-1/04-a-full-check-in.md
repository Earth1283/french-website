# A Full Check-in, Including the Things That Go Wrong

This is a complete exchange at a small hotel in Lyon. Read it aloud, then cover the English and explain what each French line is doing.

> **You:** *Bonjour. J'ai une réservation au nom de Chen.*
>
> **Receptionist:** *Ah oui, bonjour. Une chambre pour deux nuits ?*
>
> **You:** *Oui, c'est ça. Est-ce que je peux avoir ma clé, s'il vous plaît ?*
>
> **Receptionist:** *Voici votre carte. La chambre est au troisième étage.*
>
> **You:** *Merci. À quelle heure est le petit-déjeuner ?*
>
> **Receptionist:** *De sept heures à dix heures.*

Notice what you did: you opened with the booking, confirmed the detail the staff offered, asked for the key, and asked about breakfast. Five short turns, no filler.

## When the room is not ready for you

Problems usually surface upstairs. Say what is wrong, then ask for the fix:

> *Il n'y a pas d'eau chaude.* — There's no hot water.
>
> *Pouvez-vous envoyer quelqu'un ?* — Could you send someone?

If the receptionist replies *Je vais voir ça* (“I'll look into it”), that is a reasonable answer. Ask *Quand ?* (“When?”) if you need a time. If the answer is *Demain*, you may want a second plan for the shower.

## If nobody understands you

Stay calm and fall back on simple fragments: *Clé*, *chambre*, *eau chaude*, with a gesture toward the tap. Point to the booking confirmation on your phone. Hotel staff are used to international guests, and written booking details are a universal language.

## What to say when it all goes wrong

If you are told that your reservation cannot be found, the most useful sentence is:

> *Je n'ai pas de confirmation ?* — I don't have a confirmation?

Then show the email. A reservation exists somewhere, and so does the proof. Patience and a visible screen beat frustration every time.
