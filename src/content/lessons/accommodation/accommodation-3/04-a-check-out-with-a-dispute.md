# A Check-out With a Dispute, Start to Finish

This is a complete check-out in a Bordeaux hotel. The bill includes a minibar charge you did not incur, and your flight leaves in the evening. Read it aloud, then cover the English and explain what each line achieves.

> **You:** *Bonjour. Je voudrais partir. À quelle heure dois-je libérer la chambre ?*
>
> **Receptionist:** *Midi, madame. Vous avez besoin d'un taxi ?*
>
> **You:** *Oui, pouvez-vous appeler un taxi pour dix-huit heures ? Et puis-je laisser mes bagages ici ?*
>
> **Receptionist:** *Bien sûr. Voici votre note.*
>
> **You:** *Merci. Il y a une erreur sur la note. Je n'ai pas utilisé le minibar.*
>
> **Receptionist:** *Ah, pardon. Je vérifie.*
>
> **You:** *Merci. Puis-je avoir une facture, aussi ?*

Notice the order. You asked about time, asked for a taxi and luggage in one go, then raised the error calmly. The receipt and invoice came last, once the dispute was in motion.

## When the error is not fixed at once

If the receptionist cannot remove the charge, do not argue. Ask for the next step:

> *Pouvez-vous noter ma réclamation, s'il vous plaît ?* — Could you note my complaint, please?

Then ask for the manager with *Je voudrais parler au responsable*. Keep your tone level, your voice low, and your notes ready.

## If nothing works

Ask for the bill to be sent by email, and request a written reply. Then, if you have a card dispute process, your bank can handle the rest. Mention the date, the amount and the room number in one sentence:

> *Il y a une erreur de douze euros, chambre 214, le 12 juin.*

Clear facts, calmly stated, are the strongest tool you have at a check-out desk.
