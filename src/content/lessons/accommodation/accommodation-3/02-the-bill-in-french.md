# The Bill, the Receipt and the Invoice

French has three words for what you owe, and they are not interchangeable. Using the right one shows you understand what you are asking for, which makes the desk take you seriously.

## *La note* is the hotel bill

In a hotel, *la note* is the bill for your stay. It lists the room, the extras and anything you added. *Il y a une erreur sur la note* means “there's an error on the bill.” This is the most useful sentence in the whole billing vocabulary, and it works whether the mistake is large or small.

## *La facture* is the formal invoice

*La facture* is an invoice in the formal sense: the document your employer's accounts team wants, with the business name, the tax details and the breakdown. Ask for it with *Pouvez-vous me faire une facture au nom de ma société ?* Note that *facture* is not the same as *note*. If you are on a business trip, you need the *facture*.

## *L'addition* belongs to restaurants

You may have heard *l'addition* for a restaurant bill. In a hotel, the word you hear is more often *la note*. Using *l'addition* is not wrong in conversation, but it is a small signal that you have confused the setting.

## *Un reçu* is a receipt

*Puis-je avoir un reçu ?* asks for a receipt, the simple proof of payment. The gender is masculine, so *un reçu* takes *un*. The word is *reçu*, with the accent, and it is pronounced differently from the verb *recevoir*.

## The minibar, plainly

> *Je n'ai pas utilisé le minibar.* — I didn't use the minibar.

*Utiliser* means to use, and the passé composé (*ai utilisé*) expresses the completed action. The negation wraps around *avoir*: *je n'ai pas*. Stay calm, point at the line in question, and ask for it to be checked.

## The deposit

*Le dépôt de garantie* is the security deposit, sometimes called *la caution*. Ask when it is refunded if the answer is not immediate. Hotels can take a few days to release the hold on a card, and you want the date in writing if possible.
