# Traps English Speakers Fall Into at Check-out

Check-out is where the English politeness reflex does the most damage. You say “sorry, I think there might be a small issue” and the receptionist assumes you have accepted the charge. French is more direct, and it is more effective to be direct.

## *Je pense que* over-softens

English speakers often say *Je pense qu'il y a peut-être une petite erreur*. It is grammatically fine, but it sounds like you are apologizing for the complaint. Say *Il y a une erreur sur la note* and stop there. Clear beats hedged.

## *Il y a une erreur* versus *C'est une erreur*

Both appear in hotel conversations, but they mean different things. *Il y a une erreur* says there is a mistake on the bill. *C'est une erreur* identifies a specific item as a mistake. Use the first when you are not yet sure which item is wrong, then point at the line.

## *Partir* and *sortir*

English speakers sometimes reach for *sortir* to mean “check out.” *Sortir* means to go out, as in leaving a building for the street. Use *partir* or *libérer la chambre*. The desk will understand *sortir* eventually, but *partir* is correct.

## Avoid *Je veux* at the desk

*Je veux* is too blunt for a billing conversation. *Je voudrais* is the softer form. The same applies to *Je veux parler au directeur*, which is better as *Je voudrais parler au directeur*. Politeness here is not weakness; it keeps the conversation from turning into a confrontation.

## *Régler* is to settle, not *payer* in every case

*Je voudrais régler la note* means “I'd like to settle the bill,” and it is the natural phrase at the desk when you are paying. *Payer* works too, but *régler* sounds like the language of the bill itself. Notice the accent *è* in *règle* in the present tense; the infinitive has an *é*.

## *Le gérant* and *le directeur*

If you need a manager, *le directeur* is the general term, and *le responsable* is often used for a person in charge of a team or shift. Ask *Est-ce que je peux parler au responsable ?* and you will be understood.
