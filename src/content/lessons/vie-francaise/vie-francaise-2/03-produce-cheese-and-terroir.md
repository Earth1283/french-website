# Produce, Cheese, and *Terroir*

French markets have a vocabulary of their own, especially for cheese and regional products. This chapter gives you the words that matter most.

## Fruits and vegetables

| Fruits | Vegetables |
|---|---|
| *les pommes* (apples) | *les tomates* (tomatoes) |
| *les poires* (pears) | *les carottes* (carrots) |
| *les fraises* (strawberries) | *les pommes de terre* (potatoes) |
| *les cerises* (cherries) | *les haricots verts* (green beans) |
| *les abricots* (apricots) | *les courgettes* (zucchini) |
| *les raisins* (grapes) | *les champignons* (mushrooms) |

Note that *le raisin* is "grapes," not "raisins." For raisins, say *les raisins secs*. English speakers fall for this false friend all the time.

## Cheese

*Le fromager* sells hundreds of cheeses. You don't need to know them all. These phrases get you what you want:

> *Un morceau de comté, pas trop affiné.* — A piece of Comté, not too aged.
>
> *Quelque chose de doux.* — Something mild.
>
> *Quelque chose de fort.* — Something strong.
>
> *Pour six personnes.* — For six people.

Cheese is described by age and strength: *frais* (fresh), *affiné* (aged), *fort* (strong), *doux* (mild). Ask for tasting: *Je peux goûter ?*

## *Terroir*

*Le terroir* means the combination of soil, climate, and local tradition that gives a product its character. A cheese, a wine, or a vegetable is described as having *un goût de terroir* when it tastes of its place of origin.

You will also see labels:

- *AOP* (*Appellation d'Origine Protégée*) — protected designation of origin
- *Bio* — organic
- *Label Rouge* — a quality label
- *Fait maison* — homemade

An *AOP* label means the product was made in a specific area according to traditional rules.

## Regional products

Each region has its specialties. You will find *le miel* (honey), *les olives*, *les saucissons*, and *les confitures* (jams) at different stalls. Try asking:

> *C'est de chez qui ?* — Who is it from? (Which producer?)
>
> *C'est de la région ?* — Is it local?

These questions show interest and often lead to a story.

## Tasting

Vendors often offer a sample. Accept with *Merci, c'est très bon.* If you do not like it, a polite *Ce n'est pas tout à fait pour moi, merci* is fine. Do not feel obligated to buy, but if you try several samples, buy something.
