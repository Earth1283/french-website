# How a French Market Works

A French market is more than a place to buy food. It is a weekly ritual, a social space, and often the best way to taste the local region. Understanding how it works makes you a better shopper and a more welcome visitor.

## Types of markets

| French | Description |
|---|---|
| *le marché en plein air* | outdoor market, usually one or two mornings a week |
| *le marché couvert* | covered market hall, often daily |
| *le marché de producteurs* | farmers selling their own produce |
| *le marché aux puces* | flea market |

Most towns have an outdoor market on a fixed morning, such as Saturday. Finding the day is a matter of asking: *Le marché, c'est quel jour ?*

## Who sells what

- *le maraîcher* — the market gardener, who grows and sells vegetables
- *le primeur* — the greengrocer
- *le fromager* — the cheese seller
- *le boucher* — the butcher
- *le poissonnier* — the fishmonger
- *le charcutier* — the seller of cured meats and pâtés
- *le boulanger* — the baker

At some stalls, the seller is also the producer. At others, they buy from wholesalers. Signs such as *producteur* or *du producteur au consommateur* indicate direct sales.

## Etiquette

**Greet first.** *Bonjour, madame / monsieur* is expected before you ask for anything. Starting with the product is considered abrupt.

**Wait your turn.** Markets do not have numbered queues. Sellers often ask *C'est à qui ?* ("Whose turn is it?"), and you answer *C'est à moi* when it is yours. To check where you are in line, ask *Qui est le dernier ?*

**Do not touch.** In many stalls, the seller picks the produce for you. A sign saying *Merci de ne pas toucher* means just that. Tell the seller what you want and when you will eat it:

> *Un melon pour ce soir, s'il vous plaît.* — A melon for tonight, please.

The seller will select a ripe one. This is the real benefit of not choosing yourself.

## Seasonal logic

Market stalls follow the seasons. Strawberries in May, tomatoes in summer, mushrooms and chestnuts in autumn. Asking *Qu'est-ce qui est de saison ?* ("What's in season?") is a good way to start a conversation.

## What to bring

A reusable bag (*un cabas*, *un sac réutilisable*) or basket. Plastic bags are discouraged, and a paper bag is common for bread or fruit.
