# A Dinner Invitation, a Toast, and a Polite No

Read the exchanges by role, then cover the French and explain what each line achieves. The sequence is the thing to learn: invitation, drinks, toast, and an exit if you need one.

## The invitation

> **Friend:** *Ça te dit de prendre l'apéro samedi ?*
>
> **You:** *Avec plaisir ! À quelle heure ?*
>
> **Friend:** *Vers dix-huit heures, chez nous.*
>
> **You:** *Très bien. Je peux apporter quelque chose ?*

*Ça te dit de…* is the informal way to ask "would you like to...?" It uses *tu*, which suits a friend. *Avec plaisir* is the natural reply to an invitation. Offering to bring something is a polite reflex, and the answer is often *Non, rien, venez !* ("No, nothing, come!").

## The toast

> **Friend:** *Voilà, les verres sont prêts. Santé !*
>
> **You:** *Santé ! Merci pour l'invitation.*

When the drinks are poured, look each person in the eye as you clink. Keep the toast short. *Merci pour l'invitation* is a complete and warm thank-you that fits any dinner.

## Choosing a drink

> **Friend:** *Qu'est-ce que tu prends ?*
>
> **You:** *Un kir, s'il te plaît.*

Notice the change to *te* in the request, because you are speaking to a friend. Ordering with *s'il te plaît* keeps the register consistent with *tu*.

## Declining politely

> **Friend:** *Tu veux un pastis ?*
>
> **You:** *Merci, mais je ne bois pas d'alcool. Un jus d'orange, si c'est possible.*

*Je ne bois pas d'alcool* is the precise, polite refusal. Notice *de* after the negative: it becomes *pas d'alcool*, not *pas de l'alcool*.

## If it goes wrong

If the group is drinking faster than you want, you can say *Je prends mon temps, merci.* ("I'm taking my time, thanks.") If you need to leave early, say *Il faut que j'y aille, merci pour l'apéro.* ("I have to go, thanks for the drinks.") Leave with thanks, and the evening will not hold it against you.
