# *Prendre*, *se balader*, and the Word *juste*

The phrases for a relaxed afternoon are built from a few verbs and one small adverb. Learn the patterns, and you can order, suggest and linger without a script.

## *Je prends* and the verb *prendre*

*Prendre* means "to take," and it is used for drinks, meals and time. Its present tense is irregular:

| Person | Form |
|---|---|
| *je* | *prends* |
| *tu* | *prends* |
| *il / elle* | *prend* |
| *nous* | *prenons* |
| *vous* | *prenez* |
| *ils / elles* | *prennent* |

*Je prends un café* is the standard way to order. The *s* at the end of *prends* is silent, so the pronunciation is [ʒə pʁɑ̃]. *Prenez le temps* uses the *vous* form, which you use with staff and strangers.

## *Juste* means "just"

*Juste* is the adverb that makes an order sound modest. *Je prends juste un café* means "I'll just have a coffee." It does not change its form, and it sits directly before the thing it limits. Use it when you want to reassure staff that you are not planning a three-course meal, and that you are happy to stay.

## *Se balader* and the reflexive verb

*Se balader* is the casual verb for taking a stroll. The *se* is the reflexive pronoun, and it signals an action you do for yourself. *On se balade ?* means "Shall we go for a wander?" The *on* is the everyday "we," and the verb is conjugated as if it were *il*.

The reflexive pattern is also what you see in *se promener*, the more formal equivalent. In conversation, *se balader* sounds more relaxed and fits a Saturday afternoon better.

## A quick pronunciation check

*Balader* is pronounced [balade], with a short first vowel and no stress on the final syllable. *Flâner* has the circumflex on the *a*, but modern pronunciation is simply [flɑne]. The accent records history rather than sound.
