# A Saturday Afternoon, Start to Finish

Read the exchanges by role, then cover the French and explain what each line does. The shape of the afternoon is the lesson: invitation, wander, terrace, and an exit that feels unhurried.

## The invitation

> **Friend:** *On se balade ? Il fait beau.*
>
> **You:** *Bonne idée. On va où ?*
>
> **Friend:** *Nulle part. On descend vers le quartier du canal.*
>
> **You:** *Parfait. Pas de rendez-vous, pas de problème.*

*Nulle part* means "nowhere," and in this context it is the whole point. *On descend vers…* means "we head down towards...," a natural way to describe a route without fixing a destination.

## Sitting on the terrace

> **You:** *On s'installe en terrasse ?*
>
> **Friend:** *Oui, il y a une place là-bas.*
>
> **Waiter:** *Qu'est-ce que je vous sers ?*
>
> **You:** *Je prends juste un café, merci.*
>
> **Friend:** *Pour moi, un thé.*

The waiter's question *Qu'est-ce que je vous sers ?* is the standard way to ask what someone will have. *Juste* keeps the order modest, and the message is clear: you intend to stay.

## Lingering without pressure

> **Waiter:** *Prenez votre temps.*
>
> **You:** *Merci, on est bien ici.*

*On est bien ici* means "we're comfortable here." It is a natural way to signal contentment, and the waiter will be pleased to hear it.

## Leaving when you are ready

> **You:** *L'addition, s'il vous plaît.*
>
> **Waiter:** *Voilà. Vous payez ensemble ?*
>
> **You:** *Oui, merci.*

## If it goes wrong

If the waiter seems to be hurrying you, say *Pas de souci, on reste encore un peu.* ("No problem, we're staying a little longer.") If you cannot find a table, ask *Vous avez une place en terrasse ?* ("Do you have a place on the terrace?"). If the weather turns, say *Il pleut, on rentre ?* ("It's raining, shall we go home?") and head off with a relaxed goodbye.
