# Weather Small Talk in Practice

Weather conversation in France has its own rhythm: a quick remark, a quick agreement, and a gentle exit. This chapter puts the pieces together with short dialogues and a cultural note.

## A first exchange

> **—** *Bonjour ! Il fait beau aujourd'hui, non ?*
>
> **—** *Oui, enfin ! Hier, il a plu toute la journée.*
>
> **—** *Et demain, d'après la météo, il va faire chaud.*
>
> **—** *Tant mieux !*

*Tant mieux* means "so much the better." Its opposite, *tant pis*, means "too bad."

## The complaining tradition

The French love to comment on weather that is "not quite right." It is almost a ritual:

- *Il fait trop chaud.* — It's too hot.
- *Il fait lourd, on va avoir un orage.* — It's muggy, we're going to get a storm.
- *Il n'y a plus de saisons !* — There are no seasons anymore!

The last one is a joke and a cliché at the same time. Using it correctly makes you sound local.

## Regional flavor

France has strong regional weather, and the words come with it:

- *le mistral* — a cold, strong wind in the south
- *la canicule* — a heatwave
- *le brouillard* — fog, common in autumn
- *la grisaille* — grey, dull weather

If you travel, ask a local: *Il fait toujours aussi chaud ici ?* ("Is it always this hot here?")

## Adapting to the person

The question *Quel temps fait-il ?* works with everyone, but what comes next depends on whether you use *tu* or *vous*:

- Formal: *Vous avez vu la météo ? Il va pleuvoir.*
- Informal: *Tu as vu la météo ? Il va pleuvoir.*

The weather vocabulary does not change, but the pronouns do.

## Practice

Write two sentences and say them aloud:

1. Today, it is cold and cloudy.
2. Tomorrow, it is going to rain.

**Answers:** 1. *Aujourd'hui, il fait froid et il y a des nuages.* 2. *Demain, il va pleuvoir.*

Then add a third sentence about your own city, using one expression from this chapter. When you can describe today's weather in two or three sentences without pausing, you have what you need to start nearly any conversation.
