# The Four Seasons and How the French Talk About Them

Seasons are a safe place to start talking about weather. Everyone has an opinion about them, and the vocabulary is short. Learn the four nouns and their genders first, then the prepositions that go with them.

## The four nouns

> *le printemps* — spring
>
> *l'été* — summer
>
> *l'automne* — autumn
>
> *l'hiver* — winter

*Le printemps* is masculine and takes *le*. The other three begin with a vowel, so they take *l'*. You cannot see the gender in *été*, but it is masculine too, which matters once you add a preposition. The article is part of the word, so learn these as *le printemps* and *l'été*, not as bare nouns.

## Why the seasons matter in France

The French calendar is shaped by summer. July and August are *les grandes vacances*, when many offices and shops close for weeks. Paris can feel strangely quiet in August, because the city empties out for the holidays. If you plan a trip, check opening hours before you travel.

Spring is the season most French people enjoy. The weather improves, the parks fill, and terraces reopen. Winter is often grey rather than extremely cold, which is why many people complain about it with great energy.

## Talking about a season

You will rarely say a season on its own. You will say it with a preposition, and that is the next section. For now, notice that the season name is the subject you are describing, not a time you are reporting.

## A small, useful sentence

> *J'adore le printemps.* — I love spring.
>
> *Je déteste l'hiver.* — I hate winter.

These two sentences are simple, but they let you join a conversation. *Adorer* and *détester* are regular -er verbs, and they work with any season. If you want something softer, *Je préfère l'été* means "I prefer summer."
