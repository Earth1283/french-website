# Forecasts, Heatwaves, and the Art of Complaining About the Weather

The French have a strong relationship with the forecast, and they will share their opinions loudly. Learn the vocabulary for the *météo*, the heat and the complaint, and you will be able to join in.

## The forecast

*La météo* is short for *la météorologie*, and it is the word for the weather forecast. You will hear it on the radio, see it on the television, and find it on every phone.

> *Quelle est la météo pour demain ?* — What's the forecast for tomorrow?

You can also simply say *Il fait quel temps demain ?* ("What's the weather like tomorrow?"), which is more conversational.

## Temperatures and heatwaves

After the catastrophic heatwave of 2003, which killed many thousands of people, the French take a *canicule* seriously. The word is feminine, so it takes *une*: *une canicule*. When the authorities announce one, the news reports it with a level of seriousness that English speakers might not expect.

A temperature of twenty degrees is pleasant. Thirty is hot enough to complain about. Above that, the complaints become detailed and personal.

## Complaining in French

French complaints are often inventive. Some common ones are:

> *Il fait trop chaud !* — It's far too hot!
>
> *Quelle chaleur !* — What heat!
>
> *Quel temps de chien !* — What a dreadful day!

*Trop* means "too much" and goes before the adjective. *Quel* followed by a noun makes an exclamation. *Temps de chien* is an idiom that means foul weather, and it is perfectly normal in conversation.

## A warning about *chaud*

Be careful with *chaud* and *chaude*. *Il fait chaud* refers to the weather. *J'ai chaud* means "I'm hot," as in you personally feel warm. The verb changes, and so does the meaning.
