# Weather Small Talk and a Full Dialogue

Small talk about the weather is the easiest way to start a conversation in France, and it is also a good way to practise the grammar you have just learned. Read the exchanges by role, then cover the French and explain each line.

## Opening the conversation

> **You:** *Bonjour. Il fait combien de degrés à Lyon aujourd'hui ?*
>
> **Shopkeeper:** *Il fait trente-deux degrés. C'est une canicule !*
>
> **You:** *Ouf ! Il fait trop chaud pour marcher.*

*Il fait combien de degrés* is the anchor question. *Ouf* is the sound people make when they are relieved or exhausted, and it fits here. Notice that *faire* is used even when the weather is extreme.

## Looking ahead

> **Shopkeeper:** *Vous avez vu la météo pour la semaine ?*
>
> **You:** *Non, pas encore. Ça va durer ?*
>
> **Shopkeeper:** *Oui, la canicule va continuer jusqu'à samedi.*

*Ça va durer ?* asks whether something will last. It is informal and useful. *Jusqu'à samedi* means "until Saturday," and *jusqu'à* is the phrase you need for an end point.

## Responding with feeling

> **You:** *Quel temps de chien ! Je n'en peux plus.*
>
> **Shopkeeper:** *Je vous comprends. Il faut boire beaucoup d'eau.*

*Je n'en peux plus* means "I can't take any more," a common complaint that works well with weather. The shopkeeper's *Je vous comprends* shows sympathy, and *il faut* is the phrase for advice or obligation.

## Closing the exchange

> **You:** *Merci. Bon courage pour la canicule !*
>
> **Shopkeeper:** *Merci, à bientôt !*

## If you do not understand

If the forecast is spoken too quickly, say *Pardon, je n'ai pas compris le mot "canicule". Vous pouvez répéter ?* The phrase names the exact word that caused trouble, and that helps the speaker help you. Small talk works best when you ask one clear question and listen for the number.
