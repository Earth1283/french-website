# Build 20–69 Instead of Memorizing Every Number

French numbers are regular from 20 through 69. Learn the tens, then attach the units. This turns fifty separate items into five building blocks and one pattern.

## The five regular tens

| Number | French |
|---:|---|
| 20 | *vingt* |
| 30 | *trente* |
| 40 | *quarante* |
| 50 | *cinquante* |
| 60 | *soixante* |

To form most numbers, join the ten and unit with hyphens:

- 22: *vingt-deux*
- 34: *trente-quatre*
- 48: *quarante-huit*
- 59: *cinquante-neuf*
- 63: *soixante-trois*

Modern spelling recommends hyphens throughout compound numbers, so this course uses them consistently.

## The special role of *et un*

Numbers ending in 1 use *et un* through 61:

- 21: *vingt et un*
- 31: *trente et un*
- 41: *quarante et un*
- 51: *cinquante et un*
- 61: *soixante et un*

The conjunction does not appear before other units: 22 is *vingt-deux*, not *vingt et deux*. It also disappears in 81 and 91, which you will learn with the vigesimal pattern.

## Pronunciation details

The final consonant of a number may behave differently depending on context. *Six* can sound roughly like *seess* when said alone, like *see* before a consonant (*six personnes*), and with a /z/ link before a vowel (*six amis*). Do not let these variants stop you from speaking; the number remains recognizable.

In *vingt*, the final *t* is usually silent, including in *vingt-deux*. A linking /t/ may be heard before a vowel in expressions such as *vingt et un*. Learn the complete spoken chunk rather than forcing a single pronunciation rule into every context.

To practice, choose a ten and count its decade aloud: *trente, trente et un, trente-deux… trente-neuf*. Patterned counting builds the system much faster than a random list.
