# Twelve Months and One Silent Août

The months of the year in French follow the same logic as the days: lowercase, no capitals, and a few pronunciation surprises. Once you know them, you can state your birthday, plan a holiday, or understand a reservation without guessing.

## The twelve months

> *janvier, février, mars, avril, mai, juin*
>
> *juillet, août, septembre, octobre, novembre, décembre*

Like the days of the week, months are written in lowercase. *Juillet* comes after *juin* and before *août*, so a sequence is easy to memorize if you say it aloud in order.

## The tricky pronunciations

*Août* is the one that catches people. The *oû* sound is short and often barely audible in everyday speech, and many native speakers say it almost like *oo*. Do not worry about getting it perfect. Recognizing it when you hear it matters more.

*Février* is pronounced with a clear *r* and a soft *ie* ending. *Juin* ends in a nasal vowel, and *janvier* has a nasal *on* in the first syllable. If a month name sounds unfamiliar, try the written form in your head. Most French spellings are more regular than they look.

## Saying a month in a sentence

Use *en* before a month, just as English uses "in":

> *Je pars en juillet.* — I'm leaving in July.
>
> *Il fait froid en décembre.* — It's cold in December.

The phrase *au mois de* is also common, especially in formal writing: *au mois de mars*. In everyday speech, *en mars* is the natural choice.

## Why months matter

Months are the anchor for seasons, school holidays, and bills. If a clinic says *le rendez-vous est en octobre*, you need to know which month that is before you agree.
