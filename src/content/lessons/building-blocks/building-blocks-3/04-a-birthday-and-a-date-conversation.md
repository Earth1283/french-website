# A Birthday and a Date Conversation

Read this exchange by role, then cover the English and explain what each French line accomplishes. The conversation combines the date pattern, the month names, and one reliable question for checking you have understood.

> **Friend:** *Quelle est la date de ton anniversaire ?*
>
> **You:** *Je suis né le trois avril.*
>
> **Friend:** *Le trois avril ? Tu es Bélier, alors !*
>
> **You:** *Pardon, je ne comprends pas. Qu'est-ce que c'est, Bélier ?*
>
> **Friend:** *C'est un signe du zodiaque. Ça veut dire que tu es né en avril.*
>
> **You:** *Ah, d'accord. Merci.*

*Je suis né le trois avril* uses *né* for a man. A woman would write *née*, and the spoken form is the same for both: the ending is silent unless you hear the feminine version. The phrase *le trois avril* follows the day-then-month rule from the previous chapter.

## If it goes wrong

If you miss the month, ask: *Vous pouvez répéter le mois ?* If the other person speaks too quickly, use *Plus lentement, s'il vous plaît.* If you are asked about a date you cannot remember, be honest: *Je ne suis pas sûr.* That is an acceptable answer in conversation.

To check a date you have heard, repeat it back as a question: *Le premier octobre ?* Or confirm with *C'est bien ça ?* (Is that right?). Repeating dates is the single most effective way to avoid confusing *le cinq* with *le quinze*, two numbers that sound similar when spoken quickly in noisy places.
