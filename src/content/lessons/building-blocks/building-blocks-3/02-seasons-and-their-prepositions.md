# Seasons and Their Little Prepositions

The French seasons are all masculine, which is reassuring once you know it. Their prepositions are less logical. You will hear *au printemps* in one breath and *en été* in the next, and the difference is a convention rather than a rule you can derive. Treat each pairing as fixed.

## The four seasons

> *le printemps* — spring
>
> *l'été* — summer
>
> *l'automne* — autumn
>
> *l'hiver* — winter

Notice that *le printemps* keeps its article, while the other three contract with a vowel: *l'été*, *l'automne*, *l'hiver*. All four are masculine, so no adjective needs an extra *-e* when describing a season.

## Choosing the preposition

The pattern for seasons is:

> *au printemps* — in spring
>
> *en été* — in summer
>
> *en automne* — in autumn
>
> *en hiver* — in winter

The exception is *printemps*, which takes *au*. The rest take *en*. Memorize the pairs together. Do not try to apply the logic of *au* and *en* from other contexts, because seasons follow their own rules.

## Using a season in a sentence

> *Je travaille beaucoup en hiver.* — I work a lot in winter.
>
> *Au printemps, il fait meilleur.* — In spring, the weather is better.

*Meilleur* means "better" and is the comparative of *bon*. You will hear it often in weather forecasts, so it is worth learning alongside the seasons.

## A cultural note

French people talk about seasons constantly, especially when planning holidays or discussing the weather. A question like *Vous partez en été ?* (Are you going away in summer?) is ordinary small talk, not a personal question. A short answer is enough: *Oui, en juillet.* Yes, in July.
