# Booking an Appointment Without Confusion

Read this exchange by role, then cover the English and explain what each French line accomplishes. A booking is a simple negotiation: you propose a day, the other person accepts or counters, and you confirm. Days of the week do most of the work.

> **You:** *Bonjour, je voudrais un rendez-vous, s'il vous plaît.*
>
> **Receptionist:** *Quel jour vous convient ?*
>
> **You:** *Jeudi, si possible.*
>
> **Receptionist:** *Jeudi, c'est complet, malheureusement. Vendredi matin, ça vous va ?*
>
> **You:** *Oui, vendredi matin, c'est parfait.*
>
> **Receptionist:** *Très bien, à vendredi.*

*Quel jour vous convient ?* means "Which day suits you?" and it is the most common question you will hear in booking. *Ça vous va ?* is a useful alternative meaning "Does that work for you?" Notice that *jeudi* appears without *le*, because you are proposing one specific day.

## If it goes wrong

If you miss a day name, ask for it again: *Pardon, quel jour ?* If the time does not work, propose another option with *Et un autre jour ?* (And another day?). If the receptionist speaks too fast, use *Parlez plus lentement, s'il vous plaît.*

Finish by confirming the day in full. *Donc, vendredi matin, c'est bien ça ?* (So, Friday morning, is that right?) This confirmation is the step that prevents arriving on the wrong day. It is the same habit you learned in the emergency lessons: repeat the key information back before you hang up or leave.

If the receptionist offers a time you cannot manage, decline politely rather than accepting and missing it: *Malheureusement, vendredi, je ne peux pas. Vous avez autre chose ?* (Unfortunately, Friday, I can't. Do you have anything else?) This sentence states the problem in one breath and invites the next option, which is far more efficient than a long apology.
