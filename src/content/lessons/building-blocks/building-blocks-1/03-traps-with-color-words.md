# Three Color Traps to Avoid

Color vocabulary looks simple, which is why English speakers relax too early. The mistakes below are easy to make and easy to fix once you recognize them.

## Trap one: putting the color before the noun

English says "a red car," and the natural impulse is to say *une rouge voiture*. That is wrong. The correct phrase is *une voiture rouge*, with the color after the noun. There are a few exceptions with common adjectives such as *petit* and *grand*, but colors belong after the noun. When in doubt, put the color last.

## Trap two: adding endings to invariable colors

*Orange* and *marron* do not change. Writing *des chaussures marrons* adds an *s* that the word does not take. In speech you can't hear the difference, but it shows on a written list or a sign. Memorize these two as fixed forms, and remember that *rose* is the odd one out: *des robes roses*.

## Trap three: *rose* the flower and *rose* the color

*Rose* means both "pink" and "rose," the flower. *Une rose rouge* is a red rose. *Une robe rose* is a pink dress. Context almost always makes the meaning clear, but do not be surprised if a florist and a clothing shop both use the same word. When you need the color of a dress, *rose* is the adjective.

## A useful habit

Before saying a color phrase, check the noun's gender. Is it *un* or *une*? Then match the color's ending. Before the sentence is finished, make sure the color comes after the noun. These two checks prevent most errors and also make your French easier to follow when others speak to you.
