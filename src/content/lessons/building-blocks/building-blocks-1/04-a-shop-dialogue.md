# A Shop Dialogue in Colors

Read this exchange by role, then cover the English and explain what each French line does. The customer does not know the shop's stock, so the conversation depends on color words and the simple question *Et en ... ?*, which asks whether something comes in another color.

> **You:** *Bonjour. Je cherche un pull rouge.*
>
> **Assistant:** *Voilà, ce modèle est en rouge.*
>
> **You:** *Vous l'avez en bleu ?*
>
> **Assistant:** *Oui, il est en bleu, mais la taille quarante est en noir.*
>
> **You:** *D'accord, le bleu, s'il vous plaît.*
>
> **Assistant:** *Très bien.*

The phrase *Vous l'avez en bleu ?* uses *en* before the color, which is the standard way to ask about a product's color. Notice that the color itself stays unchanged after *en*, and the product takes the pronoun *l'*, which stands for *le pull*.

## If it goes wrong

If the assistant speaks too quickly, say so: *Pardon, pouvez-vous répéter ?* If you cannot find the word for what you want, point and describe it: *Ce pull-là, le gris.* If the color is not in stock, ask an alternative: *Et en noir ?* (And in black?) These three moves cover most color-based shopping problems.

When words fail completely, show the item and ask *Vous pouvez me le montrer ?* (Can you show me it?). Shopkeepers handle this request constantly, and it is more useful than a confused silence.

Afterwards, notice what the exchange did not need. There was no grammar lecture, no perfect accent, and no complicated vocabulary. You named a garment, a color, and a second color, and the shop assistant did the rest. That is the realistic standard for shopping French: enough words to show what you want, delivered calmly, with a question at the end to keep the conversation moving.

To practice before your next trip, pick three items you can see right now, such as a cup, a book, and a jacket on a chair. Describe each one with a color, in the order *noun, then color*: *une tasse verte*, *un livre bleu*, *une veste noire*. Say each phrase aloud once, check the ending, and move on.
