# Listener Reactions: Being a Good Audience

Telling a story is only half the skill — French conversation is deeply collaborative, and a good listener reacts constantly, in real time, to keep the storyteller going. Staying silent while someone tells you an anecdote reads as bored or confused in French the way it might read as polite, attentive listening in English. Learn to react audibly and often.

## Expressing disbelief or surprise

> *— J'ai gagné au loto.*
> *— Sans blague ?*
>
> "I won the lottery." "No kidding?"

*Sans blague ?* (literally "without joking?") is the go-to reaction for genuine surprise, roughly matching English "No kidding?" or "Seriously?" It works equally well as a light, teasing reaction or a sincere one — tone of voice decides which.

*C'est pas vrai !* ("that's not true!") pushes the surprise further, closer to "No way!" or "You're kidding me!" — it's an exclamation, not a real accusation of lying:

> *— Il a quitté son travail sans rien dire à personne.*
> *— C'est pas vrai !*
>
> "He quit his job without telling anyone." "No way!"

## Mild surprise: keeping it low-key

Not every reaction needs to be dramatic. *Ah bon ?* is the everyday, lower-intensity version — closer to "oh really?" — and it's one of the most frequent things you'll hear in any French conversation, reaction or not:

> *— Je déménage le mois prochain.*
> *— Ah bon ? Où ça ?*
>
> "I'm moving next month." "Oh really? Where to?"

It also doubles as a mild, slightly skeptical acknowledgment when someone tells you something you're not sure you believe, depending entirely on tone.

## Asking for more: et alors ?

*Et alors ?* has two very different jobs depending on how it's said. With rising, curious intonation, it means "and then? what happened next?" — a direct request to keep the story going:

> *— Il m'a dit non.*
> *— Et alors, tu as fait quoi ?*
>
> "He told me no." "And then, what did you do?"

Said flatly, with a shrug, the same words mean something closer to "so what? who cares?" — a dismissive reaction rather than an invitation to continue. Context and tone carry the entire distinction; there's no grammatical marker to tell them apart.

## Why this matters more than it looks

A French listener who never reacts audibly can come across as checked out, even if they're following every word closely. Scattering *ah bon ?*, *sans blague ?*, and *et alors ?* through someone else's story is not interruption — it's what signals genuine engagement, and it's exactly what keeps a French conversation feeling alive rather than like two people taking turns giving monologues.

- *Sans blague ?* / *C'est pas vrai !* — surprise, disbelief
- *Ah bon ?* — mild, everyday surprise or skepticism
- *Et alors ?* — "and then?" (curious) or "so what?" (dismissive), tone decides which
