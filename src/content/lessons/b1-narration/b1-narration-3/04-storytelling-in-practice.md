# Storytelling in Practice: A Full Anecdote

Here's how all three pieces of this lesson — openers, reactions, and floor-holding — sound together in a real exchange between two friends.

## At the café

> **Léa :** *Je te raconte : l'autre jour, j'étais dans le métro, tranquille, et tout à coup, un type est arrivé en jouant de l'accordéon.*
>
> **Nour :** *Ah bon ?*
>
> **Léa :** *Oui, attends, c'est pas fini. Il jouait super mal, du coup tout le monde riait. Et alors, une dame a commencé à chanter par-dessus, complètement fausse aussi.*
>
> **Nour :** *Sans blague ! Et alors, qu'est-ce qui s'est passé ?*
>
> **Léa :** *Bref, à la fin, tout le wagon applaudissait, même si c'était horrible. Finalement, c'était un des meilleurs trajets de métro de ma vie.*

Notice the rhythm: Léa opens with *je te raconte* and *l'autre jour*, pivots with *tout à coup*, uses *attends* to hold the floor mid-story, *du coup* to link cause and effect, and closes with *bref* and *finalement*. Nour, meanwhile, never goes quiet — *ah bon ?*, *sans blague !*, and *et alors* keep signaling that she's engaged and wants more.

## Spot the mistake

Each line below has one register or usage error for this lesson's vocabulary. Find it before checking the answer.

1. *(In a formal work email) Du coup, veuillez trouver ci-joint le rapport.*
2. *Finalement, j'ai attendu le bus pendant longtemps avant qu'il arrive enfin.*
3. *— J'ai raté mon train. — Et alors ?* (said to comfort a clearly upset friend)
4. *Bref, je vais maintenant commencer mon histoire.*

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. *Du coup* is spoken/informal register and reads as too casual in a formal email; *par conséquent* or simply dropping the connector would be more appropriate.
2. *Finalement* is used here to mean "at last," but that's *enfin*'s job, not *finalement*'s — *finalement* signals an unexpected outcome, not the end of a wait. The sentence should use *enfin* for "before it finally arrived."
3. Said flatly, *et alors ?* reads as dismissive ("so what?"), which is exactly the wrong tone for comforting an upset friend — a warmer *ah bon ? Ça va ?* would fit the moment better.
4. *Bref* signals wrapping up or cutting a story short — using it to *announce the start* of a story reverses its job; *je te raconte* is the correct opener here instead.

</details>

## Self-check

- What's the difference in register between *tout à coup* and *soudain*? *(tout à coup = spoken/everyday; soudain = more literary)*
- Which word means "as it turns out," not "at last"? *(finalement — enfin is "at last")*
- Give one reaction phrase for genuine surprise and one for a mild, everyday reaction. *(sans blague ? / ah bon ?)*
- Which phrase announces you're about to cut a tangent short and get back to the point? *(bref, or enfin bref)*

With grammar, sequencing, and now delivery in place, you have everything you need to tell — and survive — a proper French anecdote at your next dinner party.
