# Il y a and Dans: Mirror Images Across the Timeline

Once *depuis* is under control, two more markers round out how French talks about "when": *il y a* for the past and *dans* for the future. Picture a timeline with today in the middle. *Il y a* points left; *dans* points right. Same distance, opposite direction.

> *Je suis arrivé il y a deux ans.* — I arrived two years ago.
>
> *Le train part dans dix minutes.* — The train leaves in ten minutes.

Both are followed by a duration and both describe a single point, not a stretch. That's the key difference from *depuis*: *depuis* describes a span that connects to now and keeps going; *il y a* and *dans* each mark one specific moment, one in the past, one in the future.

## Il y a is not a verb here

Learners sometimes confuse this *il y a* with the *il y a* that means "there is / there are" (*il y a du pain sur la table*). They're spelled identically but function completely differently. As a time marker, *il y a* always sits *after* the action and *before* a duration:

> *[action] + il y a + [duration]*
>
> *Nous avons déménagé il y a six mois.* — We moved six months ago.

It never combines with *depuis*-style ongoing meaning. *Il y a six mois* fixes a point on the calendar; it says nothing about whether anything is still happening now.

## Dans marks a future point, not a countdown of completion

*Dans* answers "how far away is this, starting from now?" — always about something that hasn't started yet:

> *Elle revient dans une semaine.* — She's coming back in a week.
>
> *On mange dans dix minutes.* — We're eating in ten minutes.

This is easy to confuse with *en*, which describes how long an action *takes* once it's already underway or finished (covered in the next chapter). A useful test: if you can rephrase the English as "in X time from now, this will happen," you want *dans*. If you can rephrase it as "it took X time to get this done," you want *en*.

## Building a full sentence around both

Storytelling often uses *il y a* to set the scene and *dans* to signal what's coming:

> *Il y a longtemps, dans un petit village, un homme attendait un train qui n'arrivait jamais.* — A long time ago, in a small village, a man was waiting for a train that never arrived.

Here *il y a longtemps* opens the narrative distance ("once upon a time," essentially), while the rest of the sentence uses the imparfait to paint the ongoing backdrop — a reminder that time markers and verb tenses work together, not separately.

- *il y a* + duration = a fixed point in the past ("ago")
- *dans* + duration = a fixed point in the future ("in")
- Neither one describes an ongoing span — that's what *depuis* is for
