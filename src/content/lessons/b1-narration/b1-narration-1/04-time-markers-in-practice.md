# Time Markers in Practice: Telling a Real Story

Grammar rules mean nothing until you have to reach for the right word mid-sentence, at speed, in front of someone. Here are two short exchanges that mix every marker from this lesson.

## At a dinner party

> **Camille :** *Ça fait longtemps que tu vis à Paris ?*
>
> **Tom :** *Oui, j'habite ici depuis quatre ans. Avant, j'étais à Lyon pendant deux ans.*
>
> **Camille :** *Et avant Lyon ?*
>
> **Tom :** *Avant Lyon, j'étais aux États-Unis. Je suis arrivé en France il y a six ans, en tout.*
>
> **Camille :** *Et tu repars quand ?*
>
> **Tom :** *Dans trois mois, si tout va bien !*

Notice how Tom switches tools depending on what he's describing: *depuis quatre ans* because he still lives in Paris, *pendant deux ans* because the Lyon chapter is closed, *il y a six ans* to fix the single moment he first arrived, and *dans trois mois* for a plan still ahead of him.

## Spot the mistake

Each sentence below has exactly one time-marker error. Find it before checking the answer.

1. *J'ai habité à Marseille depuis 2018.*
2. *Elle a fini ses devoirs pendant une heure.*
3. *On se voit il y a dix minutes.*
4. *Ça fait deux mois que j'apprenais le français.*

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. Wrong tense with *depuis* for an ongoing state: should be *J'habite à Marseille depuis 2018* (présent, because she — presumably — still lives there). If the stay is genuinely over, *pendant* would work instead, but *depuis* never pairs with the passé composé for an ongoing meaning.
2. Wrong marker: *pendant* measures a span you spent doing something, but "finished in one hour" measures completion time, so it should be *Elle a fini ses devoirs en une heure.*
3. Wrong marker: *il y a* only ever points to the past, never sets up a future meeting. Correct: *On se voit dans dix minutes.*
4. Wrong tense after *ça fait ... que* for an action still true at the time of speaking: it should be *Ça fait deux mois que j'apprends le français* (présent), unless the whole sentence is deliberately framed as a completed period further in the past.

</details>

## Self-check

Before moving to the next lesson, make sure you can answer these without hesitating:

- Which two markers point in opposite directions along the timeline? *(il y a and dans)*
- Which marker needs the présent tense for an action still going on? *(depuis, and its spoken twin ça fait ... que)*
- What's the difference in meaning between *pendant trois jours* and *en trois jours*? *(pendant = the span spent doing it; en = how long it took to finish it)*
- Rewrite "I have been waiting for twenty minutes" correctly. *(J'attends depuis vingt minutes — présent, because you're still waiting.)*

If all four came naturally, you're ready for plus-que-parfait sequencing, where these same markers reappear inside longer stories.
