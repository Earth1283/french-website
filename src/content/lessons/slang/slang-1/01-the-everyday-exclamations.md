# The Everyday Exclamations

Slang is where French starts to sound lived-in. The six expressions in this lesson cover disappointment, judgement, reassurance and small talk. You will hear them constantly among friends, and most of them are safe to use with people your own age. Learn them as reactions first, and as vocabulary second.

## Reacting to things going wrong

*Zut !* is the mildest exclamation in the language. It means "darn" or "shoot," and it fits a dropped spoon, a missed bus or a forgotten umbrella. *Zut alors !* adds emphasis, but the short version does the job.

*C'est galère* and *C'est la galère* describe a situation that is tedious, exhausting or badly organised. It is the phrase for a bureaucratic queue or a broken train. The noun *la galère* means "the struggle" or "the nightmare."

## Judging things

*C'est nul* means "it's no good" or "it's rubbish." Use it about a film, a meal or an idea. *Sympa !* is the opposite: "cool" or "nice." It works for a person, a plan or a gift.

## Moving on

*Laisse tomber* means "forget it" or "drop it." It closes a topic when you do not want to explain. Friends use it constantly, and it saves you from a long story about the metro.

## Complimenting appearance

*T'as l'air bien* means "you look good," and *T'as l'air fatigué* means "you look tired." The verb *avoir l'air* means "to look" or "to seem," and it takes an adjective. Use it carefully: the adjective must match the person being described.
