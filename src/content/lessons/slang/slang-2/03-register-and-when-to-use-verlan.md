# Register: When to Use Verlan (and When Not To)

Verlan is informal. Before using any verlan word, you need to know who you are speaking to and what the situation is. Using it in the wrong place sounds odd, and even disrespectful.

## The informal spectrum

French has levels of formality:

| Level | Example |
|---|---|
| formal | *Je vous prie de m'excuser.* |
| standard | *Désolé.* |
| familiar | *C'est nul.* |
| slang | *C'est ouf !* |

Verlan belongs at the slang end. It sits with words such as *bouquin* (book) and *bouffer* (to eat).

## Where verlan works

- among friends and peers
- in text messages and social media
- in songs, films, and comedy
- in casual conversations with younger people

> *T'as vu ce truc ? C'est ouf !* — Did you see that thing? It's crazy!

## Where to avoid it

- in a job interview or business meeting
- with older acquaintances, unless they use it first
- with someone you've just met
- in writing for school or work
- with your teacher, unless they use it

Using *meuf* in a business meeting would sound out of place.

## Foreigners and verlan

A beginner who uses verlan can sound as though they are trying too hard. A French friend may smile, but they may also find it funny. The safest approach is to **recognize it, and use only a few common words with friends**, such as *ouf*, *relou*, and *sympa*.

## Words that have become mainstream

Some verlan words are now so ordinary that they appear in everyday speech and the media: *ouf*, *relou*, *meuf*, *teuf*, *chelou*. Even these remain informal. Others, such as *ripou* (a corrupt cop), turn up even in newspapers and films.

## Respect

Some verlan words refer to groups of people and can be offensive depending on who says them. Others are neutral among friends but sharp in a different setting. If you are not sure of a word, wait and listen. It's better to be slightly too polite than to cause offense.

## Tip

Use this simple test: *Would I be comfortable saying this to a friend, but not to a boss?* If yes, it is slang. In unfamiliar situations, stick with standard French. You will never be wrong for being polite.
