# How Verlan Words Are Formed

Verlan seems chaotic, but there is a method behind it. Understanding the method helps you decode new words without memorizing a list.

## Step 1: split into syllables

Take a two-syllable word and cut it in half:

- *louche* → *lou* + *che*
- *bizarre* → *bi* + *zarre*
- *café* → *ca* + *fé*
- *fête* → *fê* + *te*

## Step 2: swap the syllables

Reverse the order, then simplify the sound:

| Original | Swapped | Verlan |
|---|---|---|
| *louche* (lou-che) | che-lou | *chelou* |
| *bizarre* (bi-zarre) | zarre-bi | *zarbi* |
| *café* (ca-fé) | fé-ca | *féca* |
| *pourri* (pou-rri) | rri-pou | *ripou* |

You can see that the written form follows the *sound*, not the spelling. *Pourri* becomes *ripou* because the sound is /ri-pu/.

## One-syllable words

When the word has only one syllable, speakers invert the sounds themselves:

- *fou* (f-ou) → *ouf* (ou-f)
- *mec* (me-k) → *keum* (keu-me), adding an "e" sound so it can be said
- *femme* (fam) → *meuf* (meuf), a more irregular transformation
- *lourd* (lour) → *relou* (re-lou), reversing with an added syllable

These one-syllable words don't follow strict rules. They are shaped by how they are pronounced and what sounds natural.

## Verlan of verlan

Some words get reversed a second time. *Arabe* became *beur*, and then *rebeu*. This is why verlan keeps shifting, and why a word that was fresh ten years ago can sound dated today.

## Why you can't predict everything

If someone invents a new verlan word on the spot, you cannot "decode" it by rule. Context helps more: if a friend says *Il est trop zarbi*, you can guess from the setting that it is a description.

## Quick exercise

Match each verlan form to its source:

1. *zy-va* → from ___ ("go ahead")
2. *tébé* → from ___ ("stupid")
3. *laisse béton* → from *laisse* ___ ("forget it")

**Answers:** 1. *vas-y*. 2. *bête*. 3. *tomber* (*tomber* → *béton*).

Verlan is more about spirit than formula. The point is to recognize the pattern, enjoy it, and learn the high-frequency words.
