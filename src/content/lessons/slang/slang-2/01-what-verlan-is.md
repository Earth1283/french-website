# What Verlan Is

If you spend time with young people in France, you will hear words that sound like French but don't appear in a standard dictionary: *meuf*, *keum*, *ouf*, *chelou*, *relou*. These are **verlan**, a form of slang built by reversing the syllables of a word.

## The name

*Verlan* is itself an example. It comes from *l'envers* (the reverse): *l'en-vers* → *vers-l'en* → *verlan*. The name teaches you the method.

## Why it exists

Verlan has roots in older French argot and spread widely from the 1980s through the suburbs of Paris, then through music, film, and the streets. It started as a way to speak without outsiders understanding, and it became a marker of youth culture. Today many verlan words are used by people of all ages.

## Common examples

| Verlan | From | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| *meuf* | *femme* | woman, girl |
| *keum* | *mec* | guy, dude |
| *ouf* | *fou* | crazy, intense |
| *chelou* | *louche* | shady, weird |
| *relou* | *lourd* | annoying |
| *zarbi* | *bizarre* | weird |
| *teuf* | *fête* | party |
| *féca* | *café* | coffee |
| *ripou* | *pourri* | corrupt |

Some of these, such as *meuf*, *ouf*, and *relou*, are so common that you will hear them everywhere. Others, such as *féca* and *zarbi*, sound more specific to certain groups.

## Examples in sentences

> *Ce film est ouf !* — This film is crazy!
>
> *Ce type est chelou.* — That guy is shady.
>
> *C'est relou de rater le train.* — It's annoying to miss the train.
>
> *On fait une teuf ce soir ?* — Are we having a party tonight?

## What it is not

Verlan is **not** a separate language, and it does not follow strict rules for every word. It is a playful habit. Speakers choose which words to invert, and many forms are irregular. Learning it is more about recognizing than producing.

## What to do with it

For now, learn to **understand** a few high-frequency words. You will hear them in films, songs, and conversations with younger friends. Using them yourself, in the right setting, is a bonus, not a requirement.
