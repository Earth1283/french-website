# *Le, la, les, un, une, des*: The Everyday Articles

French uses articles far more than English does. Where English happily drops them (*I love coffee*, *Dogs are loyal*), French insists. Understanding the two families of articles removes most of the confusion.

## The definite family: "the"

| | Masculine | Feminine | Before a vowel | Plural |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| the | *le* | *la* | *l'* | *les* |

*L'* hides gender: *l'ami* (male friend) and *l'amie* (female friend) sound identical. You find out the gender from the adjective or from context.

## The indefinite family: "a / an / some"

| | Masculine | Feminine | Plural |
|---|---|---|---|
| a / some | *un* | *une* | *des* |

*Un livre, une table, des livres.* The plural *des* is the part English has no equivalent for. It means "some" or just "plural, unspecified." *J'ai des amis à Paris* is simply "I have friends in Paris."

## Where French uses "the" and English does not

In general statements about a whole category, French uses the definite article:

- *J'aime le café.* — I like coffee.
- *Les Français aiment le pain.* — French people like bread.
- *La vie est belle.* — Life is beautiful.
- *Je déteste les épinards.* — I hate spinach.

English drops the article here. French cannot. Missing it sounds like a telegram.

## Where French drops the article

The reverse happens in a few frozen places, such as after *être* with a profession (*Je suis médecin*) and after many expressions of quantity (*beaucoup de gens*). These are covered in later lessons.

## Agreement in a sentence

The article, the noun, and the adjective must match:

> *Le petit garçon* — masculine singular
>
> *La petite fille* — feminine singular
>
> *Les petits garçons* — masculine plural
>
> *Les petites filles* — feminine plural

Notice that the adjective changes too: *petit, petite, petits, petites*. The article is the first clue to the whole phrase. When you hear *la*, you already know what is coming next is feminine.

## A quick drill

Fill in the article, then check:

1. ___ livre (masculine)
2. ___ voiture (feminine)
3. ___ ami (a male friend, with *un/une*)
4. ___ enfants (plural, "the")

**Answers:** 1. *le* or *un*. 2. *la* or *une*. 3. *un*. 4. *les*.
