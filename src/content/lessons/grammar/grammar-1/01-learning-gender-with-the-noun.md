# Gender: Learn the Noun With Its Article

Every French noun is either masculine or feminine. A table is feminine (*la table*), a desk is masculine (*le bureau*), and the sun is masculine while the moon is feminine. There is rarely a logical reason, and no amount of searching for one will help. What helps is a habit.

## The one habit that matters

**Never learn a noun alone. Learn it with its article.** Not *table*, but *la table*. Not *problème*, but *le problème*. The article is part of the word, like the spelling. When you need the noun later, the article comes back with it, and you get the grammar for free.

Flashcards that show *soleil* with no article train you to guess. Flashcards that show *le soleil* train you to know.

## Endings that predict gender

You cannot learn every noun by rule, but endings give you a decent first guess:

| Ending | Usually | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| *-tion, -sion* | feminine | *la station, la réservation, la télévision* |
| *-té* | feminine | *la liberté, la santé, la sécurité* |
| *-ure* | feminine | *la voiture, la culture* |
| *-age* | masculine | *le voyage, le fromage, le garage* |
| *-ment* | masculine | *le moment, le gouvernement* |
| *-eau* | masculine | *le bateau, le gâteau, le bureau* |
| *-isme* | masculine | *le tourisme, le journalisme* |
| *-ème* | masculine | *le problème, le système* |

## Exceptions you will actually meet

Every rule has rebels. These are common enough to learn right away:

- *la page, la plage, l'image, la cage* — feminine despite *-age*
- *l'eau, la peau* — feminine despite *-eau*
- *l’été, le côté, le comté* — masculine despite ending in *-té*

## Groups that are always masculine

Some categories are reliable: **days of the week** (*le lundi*), **months** (*le mois de mars*), **languages** (*le français*), **colors used as nouns** (*le rouge*), and **seasons** (*le printemps*, *l'hiver*).

## What gender is not

Gender is **grammatical**, not biological. *La personne* is feminine even when the person is a man. *Le monde* is masculine even though it includes everyone. People are the only group where gender often follows reality: *un homme, une femme, un frère, une sœur*.

If you are wrong, you will almost always be understood. Gender errors are common among learners and rarely block communication. They do mark you as a learner, so the best investment is the habit: noun plus article, every time.
