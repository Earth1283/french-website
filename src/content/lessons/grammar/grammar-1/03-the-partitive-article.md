# The Partitive: *du, de la, de l', des*

English says "I want bread." French says *Je veux du pain*. The *du* is called the **partitive article**, and it has no direct equivalent in English. It means "some of," an unspecified part of something you cannot count.

## When to use it

The partitive is for things you measure rather than count: food, drink, materials, abstract ideas.

| Form | Used with | Example |
|---|---|---|
| *du* | masculine singular | *du pain, du fromage, du vin* |
| *de la* | feminine singular | *de la soupe, de la viande, de la chance* |
| *de l'* | any noun starting with a vowel | *de l'eau, de l'argent* |
| *des* | plural | *des pâtes, des légumes* |

> *Je voudrais du pain et de l'eau, s'il vous plaît.*
>
> I'd like some bread and some water, please.

## The two tests

A noun gets a partitive when the answer to **"how many?" is not a number but "some".** Compare:

- *Je mange une pomme.* — one apple (countable, so *une*)
- *Je mange de la pomme.* — some apple (a portion of it)

Another test: you can usually insert "some" or "any" in English, even if you would not say it.

## After a negative: *de*, no matter what

In negative sentences, the partitive collapses to *de* (or *d'*):

- *Je mange du pain.* → *Je ne mange pas **de** pain.*
- *Il y a de l'eau.* → *Il n'y a pas **d'**eau.*
- *J'ai des frères.* → *Je n'ai pas **de** frères.*

This is one of the most useful rules in A1 French. The only exception: with *être*, the full article stays (*Ce n'est pas du pain*).

## After quantities: *de* again

Words that name an amount take *de* with no article:

> *beaucoup de pain* — a lot of bread
>
> *un verre d'eau* — a glass of water
>
> *un kilo de tomates* — a kilo of tomatoes
>
> *trop de sucre* — too much sugar

## Don't confuse it with the contraction

*Du* is also the contraction of *de + le* (*Je viens du marché*). The spelling matches, the grammar does not. Use the substitution test from the pronunciation lessons: if it means "from the", it is a contraction. If it means "some", it is partitive.
