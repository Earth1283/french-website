# Plurals, Agreement, and a Practice Round

Once you know the articles, the last step is making nouns and adjectives agree. French adds a few small rules to the simple "add -s" you already know.

## Making nouns plural

For most nouns, add **-s**, and note that it is **silent**:

- *un livre → des livres*
- *une table → des tables*
- *le chat → les chats*

Because the *s* is silent, you hear the plural only through the article: *le/les*, *un/des*. Here are the common exceptions:

| Ending | Plural | Example |
|---|---|---|
| *-eau* | *-eaux* | *un bateau → des bateaux* |
| *-al* | *-aux* | *un journal → des journaux* |
| *-s, -x, -z* | unchanged | *un fils → des fils; un prix → des prix* |

## Making adjectives agree

Adjectives match the noun's gender and number. The feminine usually adds **-e**, which changes the spoken form when the consonant becomes audible:

| Masculine | Feminine | Sound change |
|---|---|---|
| *petit* | *petite* | the *t* is now heard |
| *grand* | *grande* | the *d* is now heard |
| *blanc* | *blanche* | becomes *sh* |
| *vert* | *verte* | the *t* is heard |
| *beau* | *belle* | irregular |
| *content* | *contente* | the *t* is heard |

Adjectives ending in *-e* already (*rouge, jaune, rapide*) do not change in the feminine.

## Putting it together

> *Les petites filles sont contentes.* — The little girls are happy.
>
> *Une grande maison blanche.* — A big white house.
>
> *Un bon gâteau.* — A good cake.

Most adjectives follow the noun (*une voiture rouge*), but a small group of short, common ones come before: *grand, petit, bon, beau, jeune, vieux*.

## Practice round

Complete each sentence, then check:

1. J'aime ___ musique française. (the)
2. Elle a ___ chat noir. (a)
3. Je voudrais ___ eau, s'il vous plaît. (some)
4. Il n'y a pas ___ pain. (some, negative)
5. Les ___ (journal) sont sur la table.

**Answers:** 1. *la*. 2. *un*. 3. *de l'*. 4. *de*. 5. *journaux*.

If you got four or five right, the article system is working for you. If not, reread the partitive chapter, which is the one that costs learners the most points.
