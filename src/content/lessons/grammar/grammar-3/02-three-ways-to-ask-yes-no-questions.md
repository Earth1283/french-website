# Three Ways to Ask a Yes-or-No Question

French offers three common question structures. They ask the same basic thing but differ in register and construction.

## 1. Intonation: common in conversation

Keep normal statement order and raise the voice at the end:

> *Vous avez une réservation ?* — Do you have a reservation?
>
> *Tu parles français ?* — Do you speak French?

This is natural in everyday speech. In writing, the question mark is essential because word order alone looks like a statement.

## 2. *Est-ce que*: neutral and reliable

Place *est-ce que* before a statement:

> *Est-ce que vous avez une réservation ?*
>
> *Est-ce qu'elle parle anglais ?*

Before a vowel, *que* becomes *qu'*. This structure is excellent for learners because the subject and verb remain in familiar statement order. It works in most ordinary formal and informal situations.

## 3. Inversion: more formal

Reverse the conjugated verb and subject pronoun, joined by a hyphen:

> *Avez-vous une réservation ?*
>
> *Parlez-vous anglais ?*

With *il, elle,* or *on*, French sometimes inserts *-t-* to separate vowel sounds: *Parle-t-elle français ?* The *t* carries no meaning; it makes pronunciation smoother.

## Match subject and register

Do not combine an informal subject with a formal social context accidentally. *Est-ce que tu… ?* suits someone you address as *tu*; *est-ce que vous… ?* suits formal or plural *vous*.

All three forms below are correct:

- *Vous êtes prêt ?*
- *Est-ce que vous êtes prêt ?*
- *Êtes-vous prêt ?*

For production, make *est-ce que* your dependable default, recognize intonation questions in speech, and learn frequent inverted chunks such as *avez-vous, êtes-vous,* and *parlez-vous*.
