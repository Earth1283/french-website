# Building the Conditionnel Présent

If you learned the futur simple's ten irregular stems in lesson one, the conditionnel présent is almost free: same stems, different endings. This chapter shows you exactly how the two tenses share their skeleton.

## The formula

**Futur stem + imparfait endings.**

The imparfait endings are: *-ais, -ais, -ait, -ions, -iez, -aient* — the same endings the imparfait uses everywhere else in French. Take whatever stem you'd use for the futur simple, and attach these instead of *-ai, -as, -a, -ons, -ez, -ont*.

> *parler* → futur stem *parler-* → conditionnel *je parlerais, tu parlerais, il parlerait, nous parlerions, vous parleriez, ils parleraient*

## The irregular stems carry over exactly

Every irregular stem from lesson one reappears unchanged:

- *être* → *ser-* → *je serais*
- *avoir* → *aur-* → *j'aurais*
- *aller* → *ir-* → *j'irais*
- *faire* → *fer-* → *je ferais*
- *pouvoir* → *pourr-* → *je pourrais*
- *vouloir* → *voudr-* → *je voudrais*
- *devoir* → *devr-* → *je devrais*
- *venir* → *viendr-* → *je viendrais*
- *savoir* → *saur-* → *je saurais*
- *voir* → *verr-* → *je verrais*

> *Si j'avais plus de temps, je voyagerais davantage et je verrais plus souvent mes amis.*
> — If I had more time, I'd travel more and see my friends more often.

## Don't confuse it with the imparfait

Because the endings are identical to the imparfait's, the only thing distinguishing conditionnel from imparfait is the stem. Regular verbs make this easy to check: imparfait of *parler* is *je parlais* (stem *parl-*, from the *nous* form of the present: *nous parlons*); conditionnel is *je parlerais* (stem *parler-*, the infinitive). Listen for that extra "-er-" sound in the middle — it's the fingerprint of the conditionnel.

> *je parlais* (imparfait — I used to speak) vs. *je parlerais* (conditionnel — I would speak)

For irregular verbs the difference is often sharper: imparfait of *être* is *j'étais*, conditionnel is *je serais* — completely different stems, so there's no risk of confusing them.

## What the conditionnel actually communicates

Broadly, the conditionnel présent expresses something not yet real: a possibility, a wish, a polite request, unconfirmed information, or the result of a hypothetical (lesson three covers that last use in depth). In every case, the conditionnel signals distance from fact — "this would happen, under the right circumstances," never "this is happening."

The next two chapters walk through its most common everyday jobs: being polite, giving advice, making wishes, and reporting things you can't fully vouch for. All of them use exactly the forms you just built here.
