# The Ten Irregular Stems You Actually Need

Good news first: the endings never change. Bad news: about a dozen high-frequency verbs use a stem that has nothing to do with their infinitive, and you have to memorize it directly. These verbs show up constantly, so the investment pays off fast.

## The list

- *être* → **ser-** : *je serai, tu seras, il sera...*
- *avoir* → **aur-** : *j'aurai, tu auras, elle aura...*
- *aller* → **ir-** : *j'irai, tu iras, nous irons...*
- *faire* → **fer-** : *je ferai, vous ferez, ils feront...*
- *pouvoir* → **pourr-** : *je pourrai, tu pourras...*
- *venir* (and *tenir*, *devenir*, *revenir*) → **viendr-** / **tiendr-** : *je viendrai, elle deviendra...*
- *vouloir* → **voudr-** : *je voudrai, nous voudrons...*
- *devoir* → **devr-** : *je devrai, vous devrez...*
- *savoir* → **saur-** : *je saurai, ils sauront...*
- *voir* (and *envoyer*) → **verr-** / **enverr-** : *je verrai, j'enverrai...*

> *Quand je serai grand, j'aurai une voiture et j'irai partout en vacances.*
> — When I'm big, I'll have a car and I'll go everywhere on vacation.

## Patterns inside the chaos

The list looks random until you notice two groups:

- **The double-r club**: *pourrai, viendrai, voudrai, devrai, verrai* all end their stem in a doubled or near-doubled *r* sound. Say them out loud a few times — the rhythm does most of the memorizing for you.
- **Short, punchy stems**: *ser-, aur-, ir-, fer-, saur-* are one syllable and easy to drill as a set, especially since *être* and *avoir* appear in almost every sentence you'll ever build.

Also worth noting: *falloir* (it is necessary) has only one form, *il faudra*, since it's impersonal. You'll meet it constantly in advice and warnings: *Il faudra réserver à l'avance* (You'll need to book in advance).

## Compound verbs inherit the irregularity

If a verb is built on one of these ten, it keeps the same irregular stem: *revenir* behaves like *venir* (→ *je reviendrai*), *retenir* like *tenir* (→ *je retiendrai*), *apercevoir* like *recevoir* — which itself follows *devoir*'s pattern (→ *j'apercevrai*). Learn the base verb well and the family comes for free.

## A quick drill habit

Because these ten stems recur in nearly every conversation about the future, it's worth drilling them as fixed chunks rather than deriving them each time: *je serai, j'aurai, j'irai, je ferai, je pourrai* said together, five times, is a better use of thirty seconds than re-deriving each one from first principles mid-sentence. The endings you already know from regular verbs; only the stem is new information here.

One more habit worth building now: these irregular stems are exactly the same stems the conditionnel présent uses (lesson two of this unit). Learn *ser-, aur-, ir-, fer-, pourr-, viendr-, voudr-, devr-, saur-, verr-* solidly here, and the conditional will feel like free vocabulary rather than a new grammar point.
