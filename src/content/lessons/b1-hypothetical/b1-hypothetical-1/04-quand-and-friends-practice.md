# Quand, Dès Que, and the Big Trap — Practice

English says "when you arrive, call me" — present tense, even though the arriving hasn't happened yet. French speakers find this odd: if the event is still in the future, French insists on the futur simple after *quand*, *dès que*, *aussitôt que*, and *tant que*. This is the single most common mistake English speakers make with the futur simple, so this chapter is entirely devoted to catching it.

## The rule, stated plainly

> *Quand tu arriveras, appelle-moi.* — When you arrive, call me.
>
> *Dès qu'il aura des nouvelles, il nous écrira.* — As soon as he has news, he'll write to us.
>
> *Je te préviendrai aussitôt que je saurai.* — I'll let you know as soon as I know.

Notice both clauses look toward the future here — French keeps them matched. English mismatches them (present + future); French does not.

## Mini-dialogue: planning a trip

> **Léa** : Tu pars bientôt ?
> **Marc** : Oui, dès que j'aurai mon visa, je réserverai le billet.
> **Léa** : Et quand tu arriveras là-bas, tu me téléphoneras ?
> **Marc** : Promis. Et toi, qu'est-ce que tu feras pendant mon absence ?
> **Léa** : Je m'occuperai de ton chat, comme toujours.

Every verb touching a still-future event here — *aurai, réserverai, arriveras, téléphoneras, feras, m'occuperai* — sits in the futur simple, because from the moment of speaking, none of it has happened yet.

## Spot the mistake

Each sentence below has one error. Find it before checking the answer.

1. *Quand je serai riche, j'achète une maison.*
2. *Dès qu'elle arrive, dis-le-moi.* (referring to an arrival next week)
3. *Il m'appellera quand il aurait le temps.*
4. *Aussitôt que tu finiras, on partira.*

**Answers:**

1. *j'achète* should be *j'achèterai* — both clauses need the futur simple since the purchase hasn't happened.
2. *elle arrive* should be *elle arrivera* — the arrival is next week, still future, so *dès que* needs the futur simple, not the présent (the présent here would only work for a habitual or immediate situation, not a specific future one).
3. *il aurait* should be *il aura* — never conditionnel after *quand*; the futur simple is required when both events are future.
4. Correct as written — no error. Both *finiras* and *partira* are futur simple.

## Self-check

Before moving to lesson two, make sure you can do the following without hesitating:

- [ ] Conjugate *être, avoir, aller, faire* and *pouvoir* fully in the futur simple.
- [ ] Explain why *quand tu arrives, appelle-moi* is wrong when the arrival is still ahead.
- [ ] Choose between futur proche and futur simple for a sentence about tonight's dinner plans vs. a sentence about your career in ten years.
- [ ] Build a sentence with *dès que* where both clauses are future.

If all four feel solid, you're ready for the conditionnel — which reuses every stem you just learned, just with different endings.
