# Si + Présent: Realistic Conditions

Si-clauses link a condition to a result: "if X, then Y." French has strict rules for which tense goes where, and the good news is they're consistent — once you know the pattern, you can build any si-clause without guessing.

## The pattern

**Si + présent** describes a realistic, open condition — something that could genuinely happen. The result clause can take three different forms, depending on what kind of result you're describing.

### Si + présent → futur simple (a likely future result)

> *Si tu viens, je serai content.* — If you come, I'll be happy.
>
> *Si nous partons maintenant, nous arriverons à temps.* — If we leave now, we'll arrive on time.

This is the most common combination: a realistic condition, paired with a result that will happen if the condition is met.

### Si + présent → présent (a general truth or habit)

> *Si on chauffe l'eau à cent degrés, elle bout.* — If you heat water to 100 degrees, it boils.
>
> *Si j'ai le temps le week-end, je fais du sport.* — If I have time on the weekend, I do sports.

Here the result isn't a one-off future event — it's something generally or habitually true. Both clauses stay in the présent.

### Si + présent → impératif (an instruction or suggestion)

> *Si tu as faim, mange quelque chose.* — If you're hungry, eat something.
>
> *Si vous avez des questions, n'hésitez pas à demander.* — If you have questions, don't hesitate to ask.

The condition sets the scene; the result is a direct instruction rather than a prediction.

## Why si + présent, and not something else

The présent after *si* doesn't mean the condition is happening right now — it means the condition is treated as realistically possible, open, undecided. Compare that to the imparfait after *si* (next chapter), which frames something as unlikely, imaginary, or contrary to current fact. The tense of *si* itself is your signal for how real the speaker thinks the condition is.

## A small but real trap

*Si* never combines with the futur simple in the *if*-clause itself, even though the sentence is entirely about the future:

> *Si tu arrives tôt* — not *si tu arriveras tôt* — *on aura le temps de discuter.*

Only the result clause can be futur simple; the *si* clause stays in the présent. This mirrors a rule you'll meet again immediately in the next chapter, with an even bigger trap attached: never a conditionnel directly after *si* either.

## Quick recap

- *Si* + présent + futur simple → a realistic future result.
- *Si* + présent + présent → a general truth or habit.
- *Si* + présent + impératif → an instruction.
- Never futur simple inside the *si*-clause itself.
