# Feet in Dishes and Elbows Together

## Tone comes with the image

Before using a colourful phrase, ask who is listening. Friends can laugh when someone *met les pieds dans le plat*; a manager may prefer “that was an awkward question.” *Se serrer les coudes* is warmer and works in many team settings, while *casser les pieds* is a complaint that needs familiarity. Try saying each expression first with a smiling, then an irritated, voice. The literal image stays the same but the social effect changes. That awareness is what turns idiom knowledge into genuinely useful French rather than a collection of amusing translations.

Some body idioms describe social behaviour with gratifying precision. They are useful because they label a familiar situation in a single vivid phrase: someone says the awkward thing, annoys everyone, or rallies a group.

> *Il a mis les pieds dans le plat en demandant pourquoi elle divorçait.* — He put his foot in it by asking why she was divorcing.

*Mettre les pieds dans le plat* means blurting out an awkward truth or question. It is closer to tactlessness than a simple accident. The French foot lands in a dish, not in a mouth, so resist the English image.

> *Arrête de me casser les pieds.* — Stop bothering me.

*Casser les pieds à quelqu'un* is common and informal. It is milder than its vulgar alternatives but still not ideal in a job interview. Use *m'ennuyer* or *me déranger* when you need a neutral version.

> *Dans les moments difficiles, il faut se serrer les coudes.* — In hard times, people need to stick together.

This reciprocal phrase has a warm, supportive tone: a team closes ranks and helps one another. It is especially natural during a difficult project, family problem, or sporting challenge.

## Quick check

- Awkward revelation: *mettre les pieds dans le plat*
- Repeated nuisance: *casser les pieds*
- Mutual support: *se serrer les coudes*

The best way to retain them is to attach each idiom to a social scene, not to translate every body part. You will then recognise the tone before you try to use it.
