# Ropes of Rain and a Cockroach Mood

## Match the phrase to the scale

Save *il pleut des cordes* for rain that would genuinely change your plans, soak a jacket, or fill the gutters. Use *avoir le cafard* for a passing low mood, not for every inconvenience. These choices make your French sound measured: an idiom has force because it is not used at maximum volume all the time. Try contrasting a neutral sentence with the idiom: *Il pleut beaucoup* versus *il pleut des cordes*; *je suis un peu triste* versus *j'ai le cafard*. The second version adds personality and a conversational tone.

French weather and mood idioms are extremely ordinary despite their surreal pictures. A rope-like downpour and an internal cockroach are both perfectly normal ways to describe a bad day.

> *Il pleut des cordes, prends ton parapluie.* — It is pouring rain; take your umbrella.

This means genuinely heavy rain, not a light shower. *Il pleut des seaux* is another informal option. For a neutral weather report, use *il pleut fortement* or simply *il y a de fortes pluies*.

> *Depuis dimanche, j'ai le cafard.* — I have had the blues since Sunday.

*Avoir le cafard* describes a low, often temporary mood. It is informal but sincere; it does not automatically mean clinical depression. A friend may answer *Ça va passer* or ask what is wrong. Do not translate it as literally owning an insect, however much the image may explain your week.

## Form matters

The weather expression uses impersonal *il*: *il pleut des cordes*. The mood expression uses *avoir*: *j'ai, tu as, elle a le cafard*. Neither phrase takes an article chosen freely; the chunks are fixed.

## Quick drill

- *Hier, il pleuvait des cordes.*
- *S'il pleut des cordes, on restera ici.*
- *J'ai un peu le cafard ce soir.*
- *Elle avait le cafard après son départ.*

Learn both as expressive but informal tools. They make a story more vivid without demanding a theatrical performance.

Use a weather idiom when the physical situation is obvious to both speakers; use a mood idiom when the relationship permits a little emotional openness. Context does much of the work, so keep the expression short and let the conversation supply the rest.
