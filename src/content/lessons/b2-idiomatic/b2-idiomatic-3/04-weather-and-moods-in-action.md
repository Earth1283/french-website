# Weather and Mood Idioms: Final Practice

## Close with a realistic choice

For each situation, decide whether an idiom helps or whether neutral French is clearer. Telling a close friend that you have *le cafard* can invite a warm conversation. Telling a doctor that you fainted may call for the plain *je me suis évanoui(e)* as well as details about what happened. Saying a team has *du pain sur la planche* can build solidarity; a project update may need a precise workload instead. Advanced vocabulary is powerful because you can choose the tone deliberately, not because every sentence is decorated.

This practice mixes weather, mood, work and everyday setbacks. Read for tone first: some expressions are warm and conversational; some are too familiar for a formal message.

## A difficult Monday

> **Inès:** *Il pleut des cordes et je ne suis pas du matin. Belle combinaison.*  
> **Omar:** *Tu as le cafard ?*  
> **Inès:** *Un peu. Et j'ai du pain sur la planche avant midi.*  
> **Omar:** *Courage, finir ce tableau, ce n'est pas la mer à boire.*  
> **Inès:** *Peut-être, mais hier je me suis fait rouler dans la farine par un vendeur en ligne.*

## Spot the mistake

1. *Elle a tombé dans les pommes.* → *Elle est tombée dans les pommes.*
2. *Il pleut des cordes aujourd'hui, j'ai pris un parapluie.* → Correct; the phrase is impersonal.
3. *J'ai le cafard de travail.* → *J'ai le cafard à cause du travail* or simply *J'ai le cafard.*
4. *Ce dossier n'est pas une mer à boire.* → *Ce n'est pas la mer à boire.* The idiom is fixed.

## Self-check

- heavy rain: *il pleut des cordes*
- feeling low: *avoir le cafard*
- fainting: *tomber dans les pommes*
- being deceived: *se faire rouler dans la farine*
- lots of work: *avoir du pain sur la planche*

You do not need to use every idiom you recognise. The goal is to understand them instantly and choose one when its tone fits the relationship and moment.

Retell the dialogue once without idioms, then once with them. Compare the tone: the plain version is useful for clarity; the idiomatic version sounds more personal and conversational. Being able to switch between both is a real B2 skill, not a loss of colour.
