# Calling for Help and Knowing the Numbers

Emergencies in France are handled by a small set of numbers, and it is worth knowing them before you need them. Two phrases matter most: *Au secours !* for a shout that draws attention, and *Appelez le SAMU !* for a direct request for an ambulance.

## The numbers to memorize

| Number | Service |
|---|---|
| *15* | SAMU, the emergency medical service |
| *17* | Police |
| *18* | Fire brigade (*les pompiers*) |
| *112* | European emergency number, valid across the EU |

Save these in your phone now. The SAMU is the number for medical emergencies, so you will call 15 for an ambulance. The number *112* works from any phone in the European Union and will connect you to the right service.

## The shout

*Au secours !* means "Help!" It is a loud, urgent call, and it works when people nearby can hear you. Use it when someone is in danger, not for routine questions. Follow it immediately with what you need:

> *Au secours ! Appelez le SAMU !*

*Appeler* is a regular *-er* verb. The *-ez* ending in *Appelez* is the polite plural imperative, used whether you are speaking to one person or several. Do not soften it into a question.

## Asking a passer-by

When you need someone else to call, use *Vous pouvez appeler le SAMU ?* ("Can you call the SAMU?"). The *vous* form is polite and suitable for strangers.

## What to say on the line

> *Allô, le 15 ? C'est urgent.*

The operator will ask where you are and what happened. Give the address first, then the problem. Keep your sentences short. The difference between a helpful call and a confused one is often just the order in which you give information.
