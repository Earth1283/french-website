# An Accident, From Start to Finish

Here is a complete emergency exchange with an operator, with the purpose of each line. The structure is the same as a pharmacy visit, but the stakes are higher and the sentences must be shorter.

## The call

> **You:** *Allô, le 15 ? Au secours ! Mon ami a eu un accident.*
>
> **Operator:** *Où êtes-vous ?*
>
> **You:** *Au 12, rue Victor-Hugo, à Lyon. Il est blessé à la jambe.*

*Allô* opens the call. The operator asks where you are first, so you answer the address before anything else.

## The details

> **Operator:** *Qu'est-ce qui s'est passé ?*
>
> **You:** *Il est tombé. Il saigne beaucoup. Il a perdu connaissance pendant une minute.*
>
> **Operator:** *Il est conscient maintenant ?*
>
> **You:** *Oui, il parle, mais il ne peut pas bouger.*

*Qu'est-ce qui s'est passé ?* is the standard question. The answer is the short sequence you rehearsed: a fall, bleeding, and a loss of consciousness.

## The condition

> **Operator:** *Est-ce qu'il a une maladie particulière ?*
>
> **You:** *Oui. Il est diabétique.*

The operator needs to know this before anything else. Say it at once, even if you have already given other details.

## Repeating and confirming

> **Operator:** *Ne bougez pas. Les secours arrivent dans dix minutes.*
>
> **You:** *Pardon, pouvez-vous répéter ?*
>
> **Operator:** *Les secours arrivent dans dix minutes.*
>
> **You:** *D'accord, merci.*

The operator's instruction is short, so you wait. If you miss the time, ask again. Repetition is not weakness on an emergency line.

## What to say if it goes wrong

If you cannot follow what is said, use *Plus lentement, s'il vous plaît* or *Je ne comprends pas.* If the operator is still unclear, say *Pouvez-vous m'envoyer quelqu'un ?* ("Can you send someone?") Clarity matters more than grammar, so a few correct words delivered firmly will do the job.
