# Describing an Injury

When someone is hurt, the emergency operator needs three things: the state of the person, the nature of the injury, and what is happening now. The vocabulary is compact, and the grammar hinges on one agreement rule.

## The state of the person

> *Il est blessé.* — He is injured.
>
> *Elle est blessée.* — She is injured.

*Blessé* is the masculine form. Add a final *e* for a feminine subject. *Blessé* and *blessée* both sound /blɛse/, so you can't get it wrong out loud, but the spelling matters on paper and in messages. Use *il* or *elle* to match the person you are describing.

## Actions and sensations

> *Je saigne.* — I'm bleeding.
>
> *Je ne peux pas bouger.* — I can't move.
>
> *Il a mal à la jambe.* — His leg hurts.

*Je ne peux pas bouger* uses the negation *ne... pas* around the verb *peux*. The *ne* is often dropped in speech, but *pas* must stay. *Je ne peux pas bouger* is therefore the form you want in any written message.

*Avoir mal à* uses the same frame as the body-parts vocabulary, with the body part after *à*. Use *à la* for a feminine body part, *au* for a masculine one beginning with a consonant (*au ventre*), and *à l'* before a vowel sound (*à l'épaule*).

## What happened

For an accident, use *avoir eu* in the past: *J'ai eu un accident.* ("I've had an accident.") For a fall, use *tomber* with *être*: *Il est tombé.* ("He fell.") Both forms are standard, and both are easy to repeat under pressure.

When you describe what happened, keep the events in order and short: *Il est tombé. Il saigne. Il ne peut pas se lever.* This sequence is more useful to the responder than a long story.

Gestures help. Point to the injured body part and say its name.
