# Conditions You Must Report

Some medical information is more urgent than a bruise. If someone has a chronic condition that affects treatment, or if they have lost consciousness, responders need to know immediately. Say it early, clearly, and before any details about the injury.

## Chronic conditions

> *Je suis diabétique.* — I'm diabetic.
>
> *Je suis épileptique.* — I'm epileptic.
>
> *Je suis asthmatique.* — I'm asthmatic.

Each adjective takes the form *-ique* whether the speaker is male or female. The feminine version is the same as the masculine, so *je suis diabétique* works for everyone. Put the condition at the start of your sentence, not at the end of a long explanation.

These are facts, not opinions. If you know someone takes a medicine for the condition, mention it next: *Il prend de l'insuline.* ("He takes insulin.")

## Loss of consciousness

> *J'ai perdu connaissance.* — I lost consciousness.

*Perdre connaissance* is the idiom for fainting or passing out. The verb is *perdre*, not *avoir*, so you say *j'ai perdu*. Use this phrase only when it is true. An unconscious person cannot say it, which is why a bystander must report it instead.

## The nearest hospital

> *Où est l'urgence la plus proche ?* — Where is the nearest emergency room?

This is the phrase from your vocabulary list. In everyday speech you will also hear *Où sont les urgences ?* ("Where is the emergency department?") Both are correct, and the second is more common in conversation.

## Urgency

> *C'est urgent.* — It's urgent.

Two words signal the level of risk. Say them plainly, and do not wait for a question before adding them. A calm but direct *C'est urgent* does more than a long explanation that buries the point.
