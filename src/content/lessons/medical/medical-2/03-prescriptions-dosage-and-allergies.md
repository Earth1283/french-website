# Prescriptions, Dosage and Allergies

Some medicines are sold freely. Others require a prescription. The pharmacist will tell you which category your request belongs to, and they will also explain how much to take. Understanding three words covers most of the conversation: *ordonnance*, *posologie*, and *allergie*.

## Prescription or not

*Une ordonnance* is a prescription from a doctor. If a medicine needs one, the pharmacist will say:

> *Il faut une ordonnance.* — You need a prescription.

*Il faut* means "it is necessary." It is followed directly by the noun, with its article. For a medicine that is sold without a prescription, you may hear *sans ordonnance*, which means "without prescription."

## Dosage

*La posologie* is the dosage instruction printed on the box. It tells you how much to take and how often. The pharmacist may explain it aloud:

> *Prenez un comprimé trois fois par jour.* — Take one tablet three times a day.

*Prenez* is the polite imperative of *prendre*. You will hear it often. To check, ask: *Combien de fois par jour ?* ("How many times a day?") or *Pendant combien de jours ?* ("For how many days?")

## Allergies

Allergies are the most important part of the conversation. Say the sentence slowly and clearly before you accept any medicine:

> *J'ai une allergie à la pénicilline.* — I'm allergic to penicillin.

Notice the contraction. *À* plus *la* becomes *à la*, and *à* plus *le* would become *au*. The noun after *allergie à* takes the article that fits it. *Je suis allergique à* is also correct and common.

Pharmacists often ask first: *Vous avez des allergies ?* ("Do you have any allergies?") Answer that question even if you think the medicine is harmless. Penicillin, for example, is among the most frequently reported allergens.
