# The Pharmacy Is Your First Stop

In France, the pharmacy is often the first place to go for minor ailments. The green cross is visible from across the street, and the pharmacist is trained to advise on common problems without an appointment. Knowing this changes your plan. You do not always need a doctor for a cold, but you do need the right words.

## Recognizing the place

*La pharmacie* is identified by a green cross, often glowing or flashing. Inside, you will find a counter where the pharmacist works, plus shelves of products you can choose yourself: throat lozenges, nasal sprays, sunscreen, and vitamins.

Opening hours are regular, and in larger towns a rota covers nights and Sundays. If the door is closed, a sign in the window or the local *pharmacie de garde* listing will tell you where the nearest open one is.

## The core frame

The phrase you will use most is:

> *J'ai besoin de quelque chose contre la toux.* — I need something for a cough.

*Avoir besoin de* means "to need." Keep *de* before a noun; before a vowel it becomes *d'*, which is why you say *j'ai besoin d'aide*. The second half, *quelque chose contre...*, literally means "something against," and it is the standard way to ask for a remedy for a specific ailment.

Other ailments follow the same pattern: *contre le rhume* (for a cold), *contre la fièvre* (for fever), *contre les douleurs* (for pain).

## What to expect

The pharmacist will ask questions. Be ready for *Depuis quand ?* ("Since when?") and *Vous avez de la fièvre ?* ("Do you have a fever?"). Answer briefly and directly. A clear description of your symptoms gets you a better recommendation than a vague one.

The pharmacy is not a shop where you should guess. It is a professional counter. Describe the problem, ask for what you need, and let the person behind the counter choose from what is available.
