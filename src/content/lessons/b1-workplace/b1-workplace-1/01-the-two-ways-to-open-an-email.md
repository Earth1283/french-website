# The Two Ways to Open a French Email

English emails drift between "Hi," "Hello," and a first name with barely a thought. French professional email has a stricter menu, and picking the wrong item is noticed even when nobody says anything about it. There are really only two safe openings, and the choice between them is not stylistic — it depends entirely on whether you can name the person you're writing to.

## Madame, Monsieur — when you don't know who's reading

If you're writing to a general inbox — *contact@*, *recrutement@*, a "message us" form — you address the reader as *Madame, Monsieur,*. It covers both genders because you have no idea who will open the message.

> *Madame, Monsieur,*
>
> *Je me permets de vous contacter au sujet de l'offre publiée sur votre site.*

Two details trip up English speakers immediately. First, it ends in a comma, not a colon and not an exclamation mark — "Madame, Monsieur!" reads as if you're shouting at a stranger. Second, resist the urge to warm it up with *Cher* or *Chère*. "Dear Sir or Madam" and "Cher Monsieur" occupy different registers in French: *cher/chère* implies you already have some kind of relationship, however slight, with the person. To someone you've never spoken to, it can land as presumptuous or even faintly mocking.

## Bonjour + Name — once you have one

The moment you know a name — from a previous email, a business card, a colleague's introduction — the default shifts to *Bonjour* plus the name, honorific optional depending on formality:

> *Bonjour Madame Dupont,*
>
> *Suite à notre appel de ce matin, voici les documents demandés.*

This has become the standard opener among colleagues, clients you've already met, and anyone with whom you have an ongoing thread. It's warmer than *Madame, Monsieur* without tipping into false familiarity — crucially, it keeps *vous*.

## Vous is the default, full stop

Whichever opening you use, *vous* is the assumed pronoun for anyone outside your immediate team, until they explicitly invite you to use *tu* — often with a phrase like *"on peut se tutoyer"*. Switching to *tu* on your own initiative, especially in a first email, can come across as overly casual or even disrespectful. When genuinely unsure, *vous* is never wrong; *tu* sometimes is.

## Quick checklist

- No name at all → *Madame, Monsieur,*
- A name you can use → *Bonjour* + name,
- Comma after the greeting, never an exclamation mark
- *Cher/Chère* only for people you already know
- *Vous* until told otherwise
