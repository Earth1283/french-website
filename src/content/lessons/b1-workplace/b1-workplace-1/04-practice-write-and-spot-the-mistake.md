# Practice: Write, Read, and Spot the Mistake

Time to put the greeting, the connecting formulas, and the closing together in a realistic exchange, then hunt for errors in a version that gets it wrong.

## A realistic exchange

> **From: a candidate, to a recruiter she has already spoken with on the phone**
>
> *Bonjour Madame Lefèvre,*
>
> *Suite à notre échange téléphonique de ce matin, je me permets de vous envoyer mon CV et ma lettre de motivation.*
>
> *Vous trouverez ci-joint les deux documents demandés. N'hésitez pas à me contacter si vous avez besoin d'informations complémentaires.*
>
> *Dans l'attente de votre retour, bien cordialement,*
>
> *Camille Rousseau*

Notice how each formula earns its place: *suite à* ties it to the phone call, *je me permets de* softens the act of sending unsolicited documents, *vous trouverez ci-joint* points at the attachment, and the closing pair — bridge sentence plus *bien cordialement* — is appropriate because these two people have already spoken directly.

## Spot the mistake

Here is the same email, written by someone who learned the formulas but not their register. Find the four problems before reading the answer key.

> *Salut Madame Lefèvre !*
>
> *Suite à notre conversation, je me permets de te envoyer mon CV.*
>
> *Vous trouverez ci-joint le document. Bisous,*
>
> *Camille*

1. *Salut* + exclamation mark — far too casual for a recruiter, and the greeting should end in a comma, not "!".
2. *je me permets de te envoyer* — mixes *vous* (used elsewhere) with *te*; also, *te* would need to become *t'* before a vowel-initial verb (*t'envoyer*), and switching pronouns mid-email is jarring regardless.
3. No bridge sentence before the sign-off — the message ends abruptly, without *dans l'attente de votre retour* or anything comparable.
4. *Bisous* — a sign-off for close friends and family, never for a professional contact, however friendly the call was.

## Self-check

Cover the answers and test yourself.

1. You're writing to *recrutement@entreprise.fr* with no named contact. How do you open? → *Madame, Monsieur,*
2. What single word signals you're continuing an existing conversation? → *suite à*
3. Which sign-off is the safe, neutral default for almost anyone? → *Cordialement,*
4. In what kind of correspondence might you still see "Je vous prie d'agréer..."? → Postal letters, official administration, legal documents
5. True or false: switching to *tu* on your own initiative in a first professional email is a safe move. → False — wait to be invited, e.g. with *"on peut se tutoyer"*

If you got four or more right, the formulas have landed — the next lesson moves from the inbox to the meeting room.
