# Closing an Email Without a Disaster

The sign-off is where a lot of English speakers relax, because in English "Best," "Thanks," and "Regards" are nearly interchangeable. In French, the closing formula is a small but real register decision, and getting it visibly wrong — too cold, too warm, too antique — is the kind of thing colleagues notice even if they never mention it.

## The bridge sentence: dans l'attente de votre réponse

Before the actual sign-off, many professional emails add a bridge sentence that signals you're now waiting on the other person:

> *Dans l'attente de votre réponse, bien cordialement.*
> — Looking forward to your reply, best regards.

It's a fixed phrase — *dans l'attente de* + noun — and it does a lot of quiet work: it makes clear the ball is now in the reader's court, politely.

## The cordialement ladder

*Cordialement* and its variants form a small register scale, and moving up or down it sends a real signal:

- *Cordialement,* — the neutral default. Safe for almost anyone: a stranger, a supplier, your boss.
- *Bien cordialement,* — a notch warmer. Common once you've exchanged a few emails with someone.
- *Bien à vous,* — warmer still, closer to genuine goodwill. Reserved for people you actually know, not a first contact.

> *Bien à vous* to someone you've never met can read as presumptuous — save it for colleagues and known contacts.

Mixing registers within one email — a stiff opening with a casual sign-off, or vice versa — reads as inconsistent, so keep the whole message on one level.

## When "Je vous prie d'agréer..." still shows up

The full ceremonial closing — *"Je vous prie d'agréer, Madame, Monsieur, l'expression de mes salutations distinguées"* — is not dead, but its habitat has shrunk. You'll still meet it in:

- Postal letters and official correspondence
- Replies from public administration (*la préfecture*, *les impôts*, a court)
- Legal or contractual documents

In an everyday email to a colleague or client, it now reads as oddly formal — almost as a joke, the way "I remain your most obedient servant" would in English. *Cordialement* has quietly taken over that job for day-to-day exchanges.

## Putting it together

A safe, all-purpose closing for most professional emails:

> *N'hésitez pas à me contacter pour toute question. Dans l'attente de votre retour, bien cordialement.*

That single pattern — invitation to reply, bridge sentence, cordialement variant — will get you through the overwhelming majority of French work email without a single raised eyebrow.
