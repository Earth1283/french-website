# Practice: A Meeting and a Phone Call

Two short scenes, then a chance to catch the mistakes in a version that gets the formulas wrong.

## Scene one: opening a meeting

> **Manager:** *Bonjour à tous. Voici l'ordre du jour : le budget, puis le planning de la semaine prochaine.*
>
> **Colleague:** *Avant ça, je peux prendre la parole deux minutes sur le point précédent ?*
>
> **Manager:** *Oui, allez-y.*
>
> **Colleague:** *On avait dit qu'on ferait le point sur les livraisons aujourd'hui, c'est toujours prévu ?*
>
> **Manager:** *Oui, c'est dans les questions diverses, à la fin.*

Notice *prendre la parole* used exactly as a request to speak, and *faire le point* used for a status check on deliveries — both doing ordinary, unremarkable work in this exchange, which is exactly how often they come up in real meetings.

## Scene two: a phone call gone slightly wrong

> **Camille:** *Bonjour, Camille Rousseau à l'appareil. Je voudrais parler à Monsieur Lefebvre.*
>
> **Receptionist:** *Je vous passe son bureau, ne quittez pas.*
>
> *(silence, then a bad connection)*
>
> **Camille:** *Allô ? Pourriez-vous répéter, s'il vous plaît ? La ligne est mauvaise.*
>
> **Lefebvre:** *Je disais que je suis en réunion. Je vous rappelle dans une heure ?*
>
> **Camille:** *Parfait, on se tient au courant.*

## Spot the mistake

Here is a flawed version of the same phone call. Find the three problems.

> **Camille:** *Salut, je voudrais parler à Monsieur Lefebvre.*
>
> **Receptionist:** *Attendez, je vous passe son bureau.*
>
> **Lefebvre:** *Je suis en réunion. Je me rappelle dans une heure.*

1. *Salut* to open a call with a receptionist at an unfamiliar company is too casual — a simple *Bonjour* fits the situation better, and Camille never gives her name, which is expected at the start of a professional call.
2. *Attendez* instead of *ne quittez pas* — grammatically fine, but it lands closer to a command than the standard, more courteous hold phrase.
3. *Je me rappelle dans une heure* — this uses the reflexive *se rappeler* ("to remember") where the speaker means *rappeler* ("to call back"). As written, it means "I remember myself in an hour," not "I'll call back in an hour."

## Self-check

1. What phrase introduces a meeting's agenda? → *l'ordre du jour*
2. What do you say to ask permission to speak in a meeting? → *Je peux prendre la parole ?*
3. What's the standard phrase for "please hold" on the phone? → *Ne quittez pas*
4. Why is *pourriez-vous répéter* especially useful on the phone rather than in person? → Because tone and facial expression, which would normally soften a blunt request, are unavailable
5. What is *la pause déjeuner*, and how should a meeting treat it? → The lunch break, typically a full hour and rarely interrupted for work

Five for five means you're ready to walk into a French office and not just survive the meeting — actually follow it.
