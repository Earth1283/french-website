# Office Culture and la Pause-Café

Vocabulary alone doesn't tell you when it's appropriate to use it — French office life runs on a few unwritten rules around time and informal contact that are worth knowing before you accidentally break one.

## La pause-café

*La pause-café* is a short, informal break, often taken standing around a coffee machine, and it functions as one of the main channels of casual office information: who's leaving, what's changing, what actually happened in yesterday's meeting that the official minutes left out. Declining every coffee break to keep working can read less as diligence and more as standoffishness.

> *Tu viens à la pause-café ? On en profite pour faire le point sur les rumeurs du service.*
> — Are you coming to the coffee break? We can use it to catch up on the department gossip.

Note the *tu* here — the coffee machine is one of the first places colleagues shift to informal address with each other, even when the same people use *vous* in a meeting five minutes later. Register in French office life isn't fixed per person, it's fixed per situation.

## La pause déjeuner

*La pause déjeuner*, the lunch break, is treated with more weight than the term "break" suggests in English. It's frequently a full hour, sometimes more, and often taken away from the desk — at a restaurant, a cafeteria, or at least a proper table. Scheduling a meeting that runs into it, or asking someone to eat at their desk to keep working, can land as a real imposition rather than a minor scheduling inconvenience.

> *On ne fait jamais de réunion pendant la pause déjeuner, c'est sacré.*
> — We never schedule meetings during the lunch break, it's sacred.

## Reading the room between formal and informal

The coffee break and the lunch table are two of the few spaces where the *vous* that governs email and meetings can loosen into *tu*, jokes, and personal conversation — without that shift meaning the working relationship has become any less professional once everyone's back at their desks. Following that rhythm, rather than staying rigidly formal everywhere or going casual too early, is part of what makes someone read as fitting into a French office rather than just working alongside it.

## Quick reference

- *la pause-café* — short, informal, a real source of office information
- *la pause déjeuner* — often a full hour, rarely interrupted for work
- Register can shift by situation, not just by person — *vous* in the meeting, *tu* at the coffee machine, with the same colleague
