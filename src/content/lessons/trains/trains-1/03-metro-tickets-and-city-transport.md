# Metro Tickets and City Transport

Inside a city, tickets work differently from intercity trains. Paris is the largest example, and many cities have similar systems.

## The basic ticket

For decades the standard Paris metro ticket was *un ticket t+*, sold singly or as *un carnet* (a book of ten). It was valid for one journey on the metro, and for bus, tram, and parts of the RER within certain zones.

> *Un carnet de dix tickets, s'il vous plaît.* — A book of ten tickets, please.

Paris has been moving away from paper tickets toward rechargeable cards and phone-based tickets, so what you can buy at a machine today may differ from older guides. Check the current offer at the station.

## Cards and passes

| French | Meaning |
|---|---|
| *le Navigo Easy* | a rechargeable card for single tickets |
| *le pass Navigo* | a weekly or monthly pass |
| *un ticket à l'unité* | a single ticket |
| *une zone* | a fare zone |

Visitors often use *Navigo Easy*, which can be loaded with several tickets. It saves you from buying a ticket each time.

## Validating in the city

Instead of a yellow stamping post, you pass through a **turnstile** (*un portillon*) and touch your card or ticket to the reader. This is *valider*, not *composter*.

> *Où est-ce que je peux valider mon ticket ?* — Where can I validate my ticket?

## Useful phrases for the metro

- *Quelle ligne pour la gare du Nord ?* — Which line for the Gare du Nord?
- *C'est dans quelle direction ?* — Which direction is it?
- *Il faut changer à Châtelet.* — You have to change at Châtelet.
- *La prochaine station, c'est…* — The next station is…

A metro line is named by its number and by its end stations: *ligne 1, direction La Défense.* Follow the **direction** sign, not the line number alone.

## Zones

The RER and Transilien trains charge by zone. A trip to the airport or Versailles may require a different ticket than a metro-only journey. Ask: *Ce ticket est valable jusqu'à l'aéroport ?* ("Is this ticket valid to the airport?").

## Etiquette

- Let people off before boarding.
- Offer your seat to older people, pregnant women, and those with disabilities.
- Say *Pardon* when you push past.
- Keep your ticket until you exit, since inspectors may ask for it.
