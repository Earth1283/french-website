# Buying a Train Ticket

Trains are one of the best ways to travel in France, and buying a ticket is straightforward once you know the vocabulary. You can buy at a counter, at a machine, or online.

## Where to buy

| French | Meaning |
|---|---|
| *le guichet* | the ticket counter |
| *la borne automatique* | the ticket machine |
| *l'application / le site SNCF* | the app / website |

Machines are quicker for simple trips, and most have an English option. The counter is better for complex journeys or questions.

## Types of train

- *le TGV* — high-speed train (reservation required)
- *le TER* — regional train
- *l'Intercités* — long-distance, slower than the TGV
- *le RER* — Paris suburban express line
- *le métro* — city underground

## The key request

> *Un aller simple pour Lyon, s'il vous plaît.* — A one-way ticket to Lyon, please.
>
> *Un aller-retour pour Bordeaux.* — A return ticket to Bordeaux.

*Aller* means "outward" and *retour* "return." You can add a date: *pour demain* or *pour le quinze mars.*

## What the clerk asks

| Question | Meaning |
|---|---|
| *Pour quelle date ?* | For which date? |
| *À quelle heure ?* | At what time? |
| *En quelle classe ?* | Which class? |
| *Plein tarif ou avec une carte de réduction ?* | Full price or with a discount card? |
| *Vous voulez réserver une place ?* | Do you want to reserve a seat? |

*La première classe* costs more and has more space. *La seconde classe* is cheaper and comfortable on most trains.

## Reservations

On the TGV, a ticket includes a **reserved seat**. Your coach and seat number are printed on the ticket. On regional trains (TER), seats are not reserved, and you sit anywhere.

## Tips

- Book early for long-distance trains: prices are lower.
- Have your destination and date ready.
- If you are not sure, ask: *Quel est le train le plus tôt pour Marseille ?* ("What is the earliest train to Marseille?")

Online booking is simple: choose your cities, dates, and passengers, pay by card, and either print the ticket or keep it on your phone.
