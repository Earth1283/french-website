# Validating Your Ticket: *Composter*

The verb *composter* is one of the most important in French travel vocabulary. It means to validate a ticket by stamping it in a machine, and in some cases, doing it wrong can cost you a fine.

## What *composter* means

On certain tickets, the machine imprints the date and time when you start your journey. Without that stamp, the ticket might look unused.

The machines are usually **yellow or orange posts on the platform** or at the station entrance. You slide the ticket into the slot, and the machine prints on it.

> *N'oubliez pas de composter votre billet avant de monter dans le train.*
>
> Don't forget to validate your ticket before boarding the train.

## Which tickets need it

- **Paper tickets without a fixed date or time**, such as many regional (TER) tickets, which are marked *à composter*

## Which tickets do not need it

- **Electronic tickets** (QR code on your phone or print-out)
- **Tickets with a specific train and reserved seat** (most TGV tickets)

If you are not sure, check the ticket for the words *à composter* or *billet non daté*, and ask: *Je dois composter mon billet ?*

## If you forget

Inspectors on the train check tickets. If an inspector finds a ticket that has not been validated when it should have been, you may need to pay a penalty. If you notice before boarding, go back to the machine. If you notice on board, find the inspector before they find you:

> *Excusez-moi, j'ai oublié de composter mon billet.*
>
> Excuse me, I forgot to validate my ticket.

In many cases, a polite explanation and a quick correction avoids a large fine, although the rules can be strict.

## Related words

- *valider* — to validate (used for electronic tickets and passes)
- *une amende* — a fine
- *un contrôle* — an inspection
- *le contrôleur* — the inspector

> *Contrôle des billets, s'il vous plaît.* — Ticket inspection, please.

## Why it exists

The stamp proves that the ticket was used on a certain day. Without it, someone could reuse the same ticket on another day. The rule is a simple security measure, and it is one that French railways still enforce.
