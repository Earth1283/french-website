# Reading the Departures Board Before the Train Arrives

Most confusion at a French station is not linguistic. It is a timing problem: a platform appears late, a board changes, and a carriage number matters more than your dignity. The vocabulary here is small, so learn it before you stand under the screen.

## The board tells you almost everything

Look for the *tableau des départs*, the departures board. Find your destination, then read the time and the platform. At large stations the screens are often yellow. The track is usually confirmed only ten to twenty minutes before departure, so a blank platform earlier in the evening is not a sign that something has gone wrong.

French has two words for the platform:

- ***le quai*** — the platform you stand on
- ***la voie*** — the track, the word that usually appears on the board

In everyday speech they are interchangeable. *Le train part du quai numéro cinq* and *Le train part de la voie cinq* describe the same departure.

## Status words

Two sentences cover most of what the board can tell you:

> *Le train est en retard.* — The train is late.
>
> *Le train est à l'heure.* — The train is on time.

*Retard* is a noun, and *en retard* describes being late, which is why it takes *être* in the sentence. *À l'heure* is literally "at the hour." The elision in *à l'heure* is standard, so you will hear the phrase as one smooth unit.

## Carriage numbers

Your ticket gives a *voiture* number. This word means both "car" and "railway carriage," so the context does the work. Find the number on the ticket, then find the same number on the platform's composition display, which shows where each carriage will stop. Walk to that spot before the train arrives, because the crowd will not wait for you to read the sign.

## What to do when you are unsure

If the board is ambiguous, ask a member of staff: *Excusez-moi, c'est bien le quai pour Lyon ?* ("Excuse me, is this the platform for Lyon?"). The French answer is usually short and accurate. Learn to recognise *oui, c'est ça*, and you will know whether you are standing in the right place.
