# Platform Grammar and the Small Words That Carry Meaning

The station vocabulary is mostly nouns, but a few small words do the real work. Once you see how the sentences are built, you can say things you have never memorised.

## Nouns carry their gender

Every French noun has a gender, and articles follow it. *Le quai* and *le train* are masculine, so they take *le*. *La voie*, *la voiture* and *la correspondance* are feminine, so they take *la*. The article is not decoration: it is what tells you the noun is singular or plural and which adjective ending to use.

When you learn a new station word, learn it with its article: *le contrôleur*, *la correspondance*, *le tableau des départs*. The article is part of the noun.

## Using *numéro*

*La voiture numéro douze* means "carriage number twelve." *Numéro* is invariable, so it never changes. The number follows the noun, which is the usual French order for identifying things: *le quai numéro cinq*, *la voie numéro six*.

## *Être en retard* and *avoir du retard*

Two verbs appear with *retard*, and they mean slightly different things:

> *Mon train est en retard.* — My train is late.
>
> *Le train a dix minutes de retard.* — The train is ten minutes late.

*Être en retard* describes you or the train as a state. *Avoir du retard* emphasises the amount of delay. Both are natural, and you will hear both in announcements.

## *Une correspondance à...*

To say you have a connection, use *une correspondance* followed by *à* and the city: *J'ai une correspondance à Paris.* Note that French cities take *à* without an article, so there is no *au* or *à la* here.

## Pronunciation traps

*Voie* is pronounced [vwa], which rhymes with English "wah." *Quai* is pronounced [kɛ], and it is easy to say it as a second syllable by mistake. *Contrôleur* stresses the final syllable and sounds more like *contrôl-EUR* than the English word "controller." Read each word aloud once before the train, not during the announcement.

The small words matter as much as the nouns. *Le train est en retard* is three ideas; drop *est* and you have nothing a French person will understand as a full sentence.
