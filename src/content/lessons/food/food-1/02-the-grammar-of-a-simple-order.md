# The Grammar of a Simple Order

Ordering coffee sounds like a single phrase, but it contains several small grammar choices. Understanding them helps you order anything, from a croissant to a glass of wine, without relying on memorized sentences alone.

## Articles: *un* and *le*

*Un café* uses the indefinite article *un*, meaning "a coffee." It is the right choice for ordering, because you are asking for one unspecified serving. *Le café* means "coffee" in general, the way you would say "I love coffee." Use *un* when you order.

*Un croissant* and *un café* both use *un* because both nouns are masculine. *Une baguette* and *une carafe* are feminine and take *une*. Pay attention to the article, because it signals gender for every noun you learn after this.

## *S'il vous plaît*

*S'il* is a contraction of *si il*, and the *i* drops before *il*. It is one of the most common elisions in French. *Vous plaît* comes from the verb *plaire*, "to please," so the phrase literally means "if it pleases you." It always comes at the end of the request.

## Adjectives after the noun

*Un café allongé* means "a lengthened coffee." In French, most adjectives follow the noun, so the description comes after the thing you are ordering. This is a major difference from English word order.

## Agreement in *C'est délicieux*

*C'est* plus an adjective is a fixed pattern. *C'est délicieux* means "it is delicious," and the adjective stays in the masculine form here because it describes a general idea, not one specific noun. You can use *C'est bon* in the same frame, but *délicieux* gives your praise more weight.

## Elision in *l'addition*

*La addition* would be hard to say, so French drops the *a* of *la* before the vowel *a* of *addition*. The result is *l'addition*, and the same rule applies to *l'eau*, *l'hôtel*, and *l'aéroport*. Elision is not optional. Saying *la addition* sounds unfinished.

## Pronunciation check

*Café* ends in a clear *é* sound, with no *r*. *Croissant* is nasal at the end. *Addition* has a soft *d* and a clear *s*. You do not need perfect accents to be understood, but getting the vowels right makes your French easier to follow.
