# A Morning at the Café, Start to Finish

Here is a complete visit to a café, from entering to paying. Read each exchange, identify the function of each line, and notice how little vocabulary a good order really needs.

## Arriving and ordering

> **You:** *Bonjour, monsieur.*
>
> **Waiter:** *Bonjour. Vous désirez ?*
>
> **You:** *Un café au lait et un croissant, s'il vous plaît.*
>
> **Waiter:** *Et comme boisson, rien d'autre ?*
>
> **You:** *Non, ça ira. Merci.*

*Vous désirez ?* is the standard question. *Comme boisson* means "to drink," and *ça ira* means "that will be fine." The greeting comes first, then a short, complete order.

## Enjoying the coffee

Once your order arrives, you can comment on it:

> **You:** *C'est délicieux !*
>
> **Waiter:** *Merci beaucoup.*

Compliments are welcome, and they tend to be met with real warmth. Keep it sincere.

## Asking for the bill

> **You:** *L'addition, s'il vous plaît.*
>
> **Waiter:** *Voilà. Ce sera tout ?*
>
> **You:** *Oui, c'est tout. Je peux payer par carte ?*
>
> **Waiter:** *Bien sûr.*

*Ce sera tout ?* means "Will that be all?" Answering *Oui, c'est tout* closes the exchange. *Je peux payer par carte ?* is a useful question, since some small cafés still prefer cash for tiny amounts.

## When something is wrong

Suppose your bill includes a second coffee you did not order:

> **You:** *Pardon, il y a une erreur. Je n'ai pris qu'un seul café.*
>
> **Waiter:** *Ah, je suis désolé. Je corrige tout de suite.*

*Je n'ai pris qu'un seul café* means "I only had one coffee." Keep the sentence short and factual. If the waiter does not understand, repeat it slowly or point at the bill.

## Leaving

> **You:** *Merci, bonne journée !*

*Bonne journée* is the standard farewell at lunchtime or during the day. The coffee ritual ends with a smile and a simple phrase.

The complete café visit uses only a handful of phrases. Greet, order, enjoy, pay, and say thanks. Once that shape is familiar, the vocabulary can grow around it.
