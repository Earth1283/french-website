# Prices, Change and Other Bakery Traps

Bread is cheap in France, and that is part of its charm. The traps here are not about money in a large sense. They are small moments where a few words make the difference between a smooth purchase and an awkward pause.

## Asking the price

> *C'est combien ?* — How much is it?

The literal meaning is "it is how much?" It works for almost anything in a bakery. A baguette is usually a little over one euro, so if you see a much higher price, check whether you are buying something specialized or whether you are in a very touristy area.

## Reading prices aloud

Prices are often spoken in a casual shorthand. *Un euro vingt* means one euro and twenty cents, and *deux euros cinquante* means two euros fifty. You do not need perfect numbers to pay, but being able to say the amount you are handing over prevents mistakes.

## Handing over money

When you pay, the usual phrase is *Voilà* as you hand over the coins or notes. It is not a question. It simply means "here you go." If you want change, ask:

> *Vous avez de la monnaie ?* — Do you have change?

Some bakers prefer exact amounts, especially early in the morning, so have coins ready when possible.

## Ordering more than one

If you want two baguettes, say *Deux baguettes, s'il vous plaît.* Do not repeat the article, since the number already tells the baker what you need. A common mistake is to say *deux une baguette*, which is not correct.

## Closing the transaction

> *Ce sera tout ?* — Will that be all?

You may hear this at the end of the order. A simple *Oui, c'est tout, merci* is enough. When you leave, say *Bonne journée*, and a smile will be returned.

## When the order is wrong

If the baker gives you the wrong item, be direct and polite:

> *Pardon, ce n'est pas ce que j'ai demandé.* — Excuse me, this is not what I asked for.

Then name the item you wanted: *Je voulais une baguette bien cuite.* This is a calm way to correct the mistake without making it a scene.

Most bakery exchanges are short. The skill is knowing which small phrase fits each moment.
