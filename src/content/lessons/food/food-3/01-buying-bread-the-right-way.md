# Buying Bread the Right Way

In France, the bakery is a daily habit rather than a special outing. Many people buy bread every day, often on the way home from work. The baguette is the centre of that routine, and asking for one correctly is simple.

## The basic request

> *Une baguette, s'il vous plaît.* — A baguette, please.

Greet the baker first with *Bonjour*. A bakery can feel busy and fast, but the greeting matters just as much here as it does in a café. A queue often sits behind you, so keep your request short and clear.

## What makes a traditional baguette

In France, a loaf sold as *baguette de tradition française* is made on the premises from a simple recipe without additives. The rules were set by decree in the 1990s, and bakers take the label seriously. If you see the words *tradition* on the sign, you are looking at a bread that follows those standards.

## Asking for a crisper loaf

If you prefer a darker, crunchier crust, you can ask for it directly:

> *Une baguette bien cuite, s'il vous plaît.* — A well-baked baguette, please.

*Bien cuite* means "well cooked" or "well baked," and it agrees with *baguette*, which is feminine. Some bakers will smile when you ask. Others simply nod and reach for the darkest loaf on the rack.

## Buying other bread

*Un pain* means a loaf of bread in general, while *une baguette* is the long thin stick. You can ask for *un pain de campagne* for a round country loaf, but keep the baguette as your default request. Naming the loaf you want avoids the question *Quel pain ?*

## Mornings and timing

Bakeries often sell out of the best loaves by late morning. If you want the freshest bread, go early. This is one of the simplest pieces of local knowledge you can use.

Start with *Bonjour*, then the loaf, then *s'il vous plaît*. That pattern is the foundation of every bakery visit.
