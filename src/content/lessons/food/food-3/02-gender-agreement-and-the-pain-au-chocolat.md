# Gender, Agreement and the Pain au Chocolat

The bakery vocabulary looks simple, but it contains the same grammar you will meet everywhere in French: gender, agreement, and small words that carry big meaning. This chapter uses the bakery to explain those patterns.

## Gender in the bakery

*Une baguette* is feminine, so the article is *une*. *Un pain* is masculine, so the article is *un*. *Un pain au chocolat* is also masculine, even though the final word *chocolat* is masculine too. The gender belongs to the head noun, which is *pain*.

Once you know this rule, you can predict a lot of vocabulary. *Une tarte* is feminine, *un gâteau* is masculine, and *une brioche* is feminine.

## Agreement in *bien cuite*

*Cuit* is the past participle of *cuire*, "to cook" or "to bake." When it describes a feminine noun, it adds a silent *e*: *cuite*. In *une baguette bien cuite*, the adjective agrees with *baguette*. The adverb *bien* means "well" and stays unchanged.

If you ask for a masculine item such as *un pain bien cuit*, the ending changes to *cuit* with no *e*. The spoken sound is almost the same, but the spelling shows the agreement.

## The *au* in *pain au chocolat*

*Au* combines *à* and *le*. The phrase is a bread with chocolate, and the word *au* signals the flavour. You will hear the same pattern in *un café au lait* and *un gâteau au citron*.

## Chocolatine

In the southwest of France, many people say *chocolatine* for the same pastry. In Paris and most of the country, *pain au chocolat* is the standard term. Ordering with *pain au chocolat* is always understood, so use that by default.

## Pronunciation

*Baguette* ends in a short *-ette*, with a clear *t* sound at the end. *Chocolat* is pronounced *sho-ko-LA*, with the stress on the final syllable. *Pain* is a nasal vowel, so do not add an *n* sound at the end.

## Asking for the pastry

> *Un pain au chocolat, s'il vous plaît.* — A chocolate pastry, please.

The sentence follows the same pattern as the baguette request. Change the item and keep the frame, and your bakery French becomes a reusable template.
