# A Complete Bakery Visit, from Hello to Goodbye

Here is a full visit to a bakery, including a small mistake and its fix. Read each line, notice what it does, and then try saying the whole exchange aloud until it flows.

## Entering and ordering

> **You:** *Bonjour, madame.*
>
> **Baker:** *Bonjour. Qu'est-ce qu'il vous faut ?*
>
> **You:** *Une baguette bien cuite, s'il vous plaît, et un pain au chocolat.*
>
> **Baker:** *Voilà. Ça fait un euro quatre-vingts.*
>
> **You:** *Voilà.*

*Qu'est-ce qu'il vous faut ?* means "What do you need?" It is a common bakery question. *Ça fait un euro quatre-vingts* means the total is one euro eighty. Handing over money with *Voilà* completes the exchange.

## Getting change

If you have a larger note, the baker may return change:

> **You:** *Vous avez de la monnaie ?*
>
> **Baker:** *Oui, voici.*

*Voici* means "here it is," and it is the natural counterpart to *Voilà* when someone else is handing you something.

## The mistake

Imagine the baker gives you a plain baguette, not the crispy one you asked for:

> **You:** *Pardon, je voulais une baguette bien cuite.*
>
> **Baker:** *Ah, pardon. Je la change.*
>
> **You:** *Merci, c'est très gentil.*

*Je voulais* is the imperfect tense, which sounds softer than *je veux*. It gives you a polite way to correct an order. Once the baker fixes it, thank them and move on.

## The farewell

> **You:** *Bonne journée, au revoir !*
>
> **Baker:** *Bonne journée, au revoir !*

Saying goodbye is part of the transaction. A bakery is a small social place, and a clear farewell makes the visit feel complete.

## If something unexpected happens

If you do not understand a question, use *Pardon, je n'ai pas compris, pouvez-vous répéter ?* Then point at the item or hold up fingers for the number. Bakers see many tourists, and they are usually patient when you try honestly.

The bakery is one of the easiest places to practise French every day. Greet, order, pay, and thank. That routine is enough for many weeks of happy bread.
