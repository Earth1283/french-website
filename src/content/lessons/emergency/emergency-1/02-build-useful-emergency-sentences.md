# Build Useful Emergency Sentences

You do not need advanced grammar to give essential information. Combine a small number of frames with a place, person, object, or symptom.

## Frame 1: *J'ai besoin de…*

*Avoir besoin de* means “to need.” Conjugate *avoir*, keep *besoin de*, and add the thing needed:

| French | Meaning |
|---|---|
| *J'ai besoin d'aide.* | I need help. |
| *J'ai besoin d'un médecin.* | I need a doctor. |
| *J'ai besoin de la police.* | I need the police. |
| *J'ai besoin de mon médicament.* | I need my medication. |

Before a vowel, *de* becomes *d'*, which is why you say *d'aide*.

## Frame 2: *Je suis…*

Use *être* for a condition or location-related state:

- *Je suis perdu(e).* — I am lost.
- *Je suis malade.* — I am sick.
- *Je suis blessé(e).* — I am injured.
- *Je suis ici.* — I am here.

For many physical sensations, French instead uses *avoir*: *j'ai mal* (“I am in pain”), *j'ai froid* (“I am cold”), and *j'ai peur* (“I am afraid”). Do not translate the English verb mechanically.

## Frame 3: *Il y a…*

*Il y a* means “there is” or “there are” and points attention toward a problem:

> *Il y a un accident.* — There is an accident.
>
> *Il y a quelqu'un de blessé.* — Someone is injured.
>
> *Il y a un incendie.* — There is a fire.

## Give location before details

On a call, say where you are early and repeat it if needed:

> *Je suis au 12, rue Victor-Hugo, à Lyon.*

French addresses normally place the building number before the street name. State the town, street, building number, floor (*au troisième étage*), and any visible landmark. If pronunciation is difficult, spell the street name or ask: *Je peux vous envoyer l'adresse par message ?* (“Can I send you the address by message?”)

The most effective emergency sentence is not elegant. It identifies the problem, the location, and the help needed.
