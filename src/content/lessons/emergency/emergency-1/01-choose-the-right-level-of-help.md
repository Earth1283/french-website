# Choose the Right Level of Help

In a stressful situation, a short correct sentence is more useful than a complicated sentence you cannot retrieve. The six phrases in this lesson form a ladder from ordinary communication trouble to immediate danger. Learn what each level signals.

## Level 1: communication trouble

Use *Je ne parle pas français* (“I do not speak French”) when the main problem is language. It is honest, but it can end the conversation unless you follow it with a request:

> *Excusez-moi, je ne parle pas bien français. Parlez-vous anglais ?*
>
> Excuse me, I do not speak French well. Do you speak English?

Adding *bien* softens the absolute statement: “I do not speak French well.” This is often more accurate after you have learned a few phrases.

## Level 2: practical help

Use *J'ai besoin d'aide* (“I need help”) for a missed connection, a lost bag, a broken phone, or another serious but not immediately dangerous problem. French expresses this as “I have need of help”: *j'ai* + *besoin de* + noun.

> *J'ai besoin d'aide avec mon billet.* — I need help with my ticket.
>
> *J'ai besoin d'un médecin.* — I need a doctor.

## Level 3: being lost

*Je suis perdu* is said by a masculine speaker; *je suis perdue* is written by a feminine speaker. The final *e* does not change the pronunciation here. Follow it with your destination or show an address:

> *Je suis perdue. Je cherche cet hôtel.* — I am lost. I am looking for this hotel.

## Level 4: immediate danger

*Au secours !* is a loud call for urgent help. Use it when someone is in danger, not when you need routine directions. *Appelez la police !* is a direct command: “Call the police!” In a medical emergency, *Appelez une ambulance !* means “Call an ambulance!”

The key skill is matching the phrase to the urgency. Calm practical language helps people identify what you need; an alarm phrase gets immediate attention when seconds matter.
