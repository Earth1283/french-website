# Slow the Conversation Down

Knowing how to ask for help is only half the task. The other person will answer, often quickly. These repair phrases let you control the pace and confirm essential information.

## Ask for repetition and slower speech

> *Pouvez-vous répéter, s'il vous plaît ?* — Can you repeat, please?
>
> *Plus lentement, s'il vous plaît.* — More slowly, please.
>
> *Je n'ai pas compris.* — I did not understand.

These are not admissions of failure. They are tools for preventing a dangerous misunderstanding. Ask for one instruction at a time if necessary: *Une chose à la fois, s'il vous plaît* (“One thing at a time, please”).

## Confirm what you heard

Repeat numbers, addresses, and actions back as a question:

> *Dix-sept ?* — Seventeen?
>
> *Rue Pasteur, numéro quarante-deux ?* — Pasteur Street, number forty-two?
>
> *J'attends ici ?* — I wait here?

For yes/no confirmation, use *C'est bien ça ?* (“Is that correct?”). If someone gives directions, point and ask *Par là ?* (“That way?”).

## Words you are likely to hear

| French | Meaning |
|---|---|
| *Attendez.* | Wait. |
| *Ne bougez pas.* | Do not move. |
| *Restez ici.* | Stay here. |
| *Vous êtes où ?* | Where are you? |
| *Qu'est-ce qui s'est passé ?* | What happened? |
| *Est-ce que vous êtes blessé(e) ?* | Are you injured? |
| *Les secours arrivent.* | Emergency services are coming. |

In spoken French, *ne* is often dropped, so *Ne bougez pas* may sound like *Bougez pas*. The meaning remains negative because *pas* is present.

## If your mind goes blank

Use isolated facts and show supporting information: *accident*, *adresse*, *allergie*, a medication label, a map pin, or an emergency contact. Clear fragments supported by gestures are better than silence. Start with *Bonjour* or *excusez-moi* when the situation permits, but in immediate danger, clarity and speed take priority over ceremony.
