# Building Numbers Without Panic

The numbers from zero to sixteen are single words you simply memorize. From seventeen upward, French starts to combine parts, and that is where most learners stall. The system is logical once you see the pieces.

## Zero to sixteen: memorize them

*Zéro, un, deux, trois, quatre, cinq, six, sept, huit, neuf, dix, onze, douze, treize, quatorze, quinze, seize.* Practice them in order, then out of order. The emergency numbers you need most, such as *quinze*, *dix-sept*, and *dix-huit*, appear in this sequence.

## Seventeen to nineteen

French builds these by adding the units to *dix*:

- *dix-sept* — ten plus seven, 17
- *dix-huit* — ten plus eight, 18
- *dix-neuf* — ten plus nine, 19

The hyphen is part of the spelling, but in speech you hear one continuous unit. *Dix-sept* is pronounced close to *dis-set*.

## The unit *un* and the gender *une*

Numbers ending in *un* change for gender when they are attached to a noun. *Un* is masculine, *une* is feminine. *Vingt et un* means 21, and *vingt et une* is the feminine form used with *minutes* or *heures*:

> *Il est vingt et une heures.* — It is 9 p.m.

Most people will understand you without the agreement, but using it shows care.

## Twenty and beyond

*Vingt* is 20, *trente* is 30, *quarante* is 40, and so on. Sixty-plus has its own logic. *Soixante-dix* is 70, which literally means "sixty-ten." *Quatre-vingts* is 80, with a plural *s*, and *quatre-vingt-dix* is 90.

Belgium and Switzerland use *septante* and *nonante*, but these forms are not standard in France. If you hear them, you are probably near the border.

## Pronunciation of *six*, *dix*, and *cinq*

These numbers change sound depending on what follows. *Six* is pronounced *sis* alone but *si* before a consonant. *Dix* is *dis* alone, and before a vowel it becomes *diz*. *Cinq* is *sɛ̃k* alone but *sɛ̃* before a consonant. In *dix-huit*, the *h* is silent and the *x* links as a *z*: *diz-wheet* /di.zɥit/. *Dix-neuf* goes the other way, with a *z* too: /diz.nœf/.

Pronunciation changes are small, but they help people recognize the number you are saying. Practice the numbers in short phrases, not only as isolated words.
