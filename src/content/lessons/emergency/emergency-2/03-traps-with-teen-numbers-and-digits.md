# Traps with Teen Numbers and Digits

Numbers cause problems in exactly the moments when you have the least patience for them. The traps below are common, and they are easier to avoid once you see them coming.

## *Quinze* and *cinquante*

These two sound similar to a tired ear. *Quinze* is 15, and *cinquante* is 50. The first has a clear *k* and a short ending, while the second has a longer vowel sound and the *cin-* beginning. If you are not sure, repeat the number back: *Quinze ?* Confirming the digit is cheaper than guessing.

## *Seize* and *dix-sept*

French has a single word for 16, *seize*, while 17 is *dix-sept*. Do not invent *seize-un* or *dix-six*. The structure changes after sixteen, so the memorized list matters more than a clever guess.

## Reading a phone number in pairs

French speakers usually read phone numbers in pairs of digits, not one by one. The number 06 12 34 56 78 becomes:

> *Zéro six, douze, trente-quatre, cinquante-six, soixante-dix-huit.*

Mixing these two styles can confuse a listener. If someone reads digits individually, ask gently: *Pouvez-vous répéter par deux ?* — Can you repeat in pairs?

## The number 15 is not a time

When someone says *quinze heures*, they mean 3 p.m., not the emergency service. The context is almost always clear, but if you hear the number in a sentence about a meeting or a train, check the time rather than assuming an emergency.

## Room numbers and apartment floors

Hotel rooms and building numbers are read as ordinary numbers, but the floor is different. *Au deuxième étage* means "on the second floor," but in French, *le rez-de-chaussée* is the ground floor, so the first floor above it is *le premier étage*. This difference between floor numbers and room numbers causes confusion. Ask *À quel étage ?* if you are not sure.

## When the number refuses to cooperate

If a number keeps slipping, write it down. A pen and paper are more reliable than memory under pressure. You can also say *Je ne comprends pas le numéro, pouvez-vous l'écrire ?* — I do not understand the number, can you write it down?

Accuracy beats speed in an emergency. A clear, repeated number is worth more than a rushed one.
