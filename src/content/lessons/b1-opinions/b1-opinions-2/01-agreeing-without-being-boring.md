# Agreeing Without Being Boring

"I agree" gets repetitive fast in any language. French has a small toolkit of agreement words that let you signal enthusiasm, precision, or mild concession, instead of saying *oui, oui, oui* on a loop.

## The base verb: être d'accord (avec)

*Être d'accord avec quelqu'un* is the full, grammatically complete way to say "to agree with someone." The preposition matters: *d'accord* alone, without *avec*, usually just means "OK" or "deal," as a standalone reply.

> *Je suis d'accord avec toi.* — I agree with you.
>
> *— On part à huit heures ? — D'accord.* — "Shall we leave at eight?" "OK."

Drop the *avec* when you actually mean "I agree with you specifically," and you've accidentally just said "OK" to something else entirely.

## Tout à fait and exactement: strong agreement

Both intensify a plain "yes" into something closer to "absolutely" or "exactly." They're safe in essentially every register — a dinner party, a business meeting, a text to a friend.

> *Tout à fait, tu as raison.* — Absolutely, you're right.
>
> *Exactement, c'est ce que je voulais dire.* — Exactly, that's what I meant.

*Exactement* works best when the other person has just articulated precisely what you were thinking; *tout à fait* is the more general-purpose intensifier.

## Why intensity matters here

English speakers often flatten every agreement to a plain "I agree," which can come across as unenthusiastic or perfunctory in French conversation, where reacting with energy is part of showing you were actually listening. Choosing *tout à fait* over a bare *oui* costs nothing extra and signals real engagement.

## Building a full agreeing turn

A natural agreement often layers an intensifier with the full verb phrase:

> *Tout à fait, je suis complètement d'accord avec toi sur ce point.*
>
> Absolutely, I completely agree with you on that point.

You don't need all of that every time — a bare *tout à fait* is a perfectly complete reply on its own — but knowing how to expand it lets you match the weight of what you're agreeing with. A throwaway comment gets a quick *exactement*; a genuinely persuasive argument might earn the fuller version.

## A small habit to build

Before your next opinion exercise, try replying to a claim with three different levels of agreement: a bare intensifier, the full *être d'accord avec* sentence, and a combination of both. Feeling which one fits a given moment is exactly the instinct this lesson is building toward — and it sets up the next chapter, where the same instinct applies to disagreeing.
