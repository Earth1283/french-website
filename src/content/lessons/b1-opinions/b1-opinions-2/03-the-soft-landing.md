# The Soft Landing: Conceding, Softening, and One Word for Friends Only

Not every disagreement needs to be flat. French has a set of moves for pushing back gently — conceding a point before you contradict it, or showing you followed someone's reasoning even if you don't share their conclusion.

## C'est vrai, mais...: concede, then pivot

This is the single most useful disagreement softener in the language. You grant the other person's point is valid, which signals you actually listened, and then you pivot with *mais* into your own view.

> *C'est vrai, mais ce n'est pas si simple.* — That's true, but it's not that simple.

Compare the tone of that to a flat *je ne suis pas d'accord*. Both express disagreement in the end, but *c'est vrai, mais...* lands as collaborative rather than confrontational — useful when you want to keep a discussion constructive, at work or with someone you'd rather not upset.

## Je vois ce que tu veux dire: acknowledge without conceding

*Je vois ce que tu veux dire* — "I see what you mean" — doesn't grant that the other person is right. It grants that you understood them, which is a different and smaller claim. It's a useful bridge phrase before you explain why you still disagree.

> *Je vois ce que tu veux dire, mais je ne suis pas convaincu.*
>
> I see what you mean, but I'm not convinced.

Used alone, without a follow-up, it can also function as a polite way to end a disagreement without either side backing down — a conversational shrug that lets everyone move on.

## Mouais: the skeptical half-agreement

*Mouais* sits between *oui* and *non* — a drawn-out, skeptical "yeah..." that signals you're not buying it, without committing to open disagreement.

> *— Ce restaurant est excellent, non ? — Mouais, je ne suis pas sûr.*
>
> "This restaurant is excellent, right?" "Yeah... I'm not sure."

This one is strictly spoken and informal. It belongs with friends, family, or casual acquaintances — never in an email, a meeting, or anything written for someone you address as *vous*. Using it in a formal context would land as flippant, not just casual.

## Matching the tool to the moment

- Disagreeing with a stranger's strong claim, want to stay diplomatic → *c'est vrai, mais...*
- Understood the argument, still unconvinced → *je vois ce que tu veux dire, mais...*
- Chatting with a friend, mildly skeptical → *mouais.*
- Formal, direct, backed by a reason → *je ne suis pas d'accord, parce que...*

Real conversations mix all of these depending on who you're talking to and how strongly you feel. The skill this lesson is building isn't memorizing four phrases — it's reading the room correctly, which is exactly what the worked practice in the next chapter will test.
