# Worked Practice: Agreeing and Disagreeing

Read each exchange for register as much as meaning, then work through the spot-the-mistake section and the self-check.

## Two friends after a concert

> **Marc:** *C'était incroyable, non ?*
>
> **You:** *Mouais, la première partie était longue.*
>
> **Marc:** *Ah bon ? Moi, je trouve que c'était le meilleur concert de l'année.*
>
> **You:** *C'est vrai, mais je préfère leurs anciens albums.*

*Mouais* is fine here — casual friends, casual setting. Notice how *c'est vrai, mais...* lets you disagree about the albums without contradicting Marc's enthusiasm outright.

## A team meeting

> **Manager:** *Je propose qu'on lance le projet en janvier.*
>
> **You:** *Je vois ce que vous voulez dire, mais je ne suis pas d'accord. Le calendrier est trop serré.*
>
> **Manager:** *Tout à fait, vous avez raison. Regardons février.*

Here the register shifts: no *mouais*, and the disagreement is stated plainly but backed by a reason — exactly what French professional norms expect. *Tout à fait, vous avez raison* shows the manager conceding cleanly, without any loss of face on either side.

## Spot the mistake

1. *Tu es raison, j'ai mal compris.*
2. *Je suis d'accord toi.*
3. *Mouais, je pense que ce projet est excellent.* (said in a formal client email)
4. *— Ça vous dérange si j'ouvre la fenêtre ? — Pas du tout, je ne suis pas d'accord.*

**Fixes:**

1. *Tu as raison, j'ai mal compris.* — *Raison* is a noun; it pairs with *avoir*, never *être*.
2. *Je suis d'accord avec toi.* — *Être d'accord* needs *avec* before the person you're agreeing with.
3. *Wrong register, not wrong grammar* — *mouais* is casual, spoken French; a client email calls for *je pense que ce projet est excellent* without it.
4. *— Ça vous dérange si j'ouvre la fenêtre ? — Pas du tout.* — *Pas du tout* here answers "does it bother you," so it already means "no problem"; adding *je ne suis pas d'accord* contradicts it by importing the other meaning of the same phrase.

## Self-check

1. What preposition does *être d'accord* need before the person you agree with?
   *Avec.*
2. Which two words does French use for "you're right" and "you're wrong," and which verb do they pair with?
   *Raison and tort, paired with avoir.*
3. Name one phrase that concedes a point before disagreeing, and one that acknowledges understanding without conceding.
   *C'est vrai, mais... to concede; je vois ce que tu veux dire to acknowledge without conceding.*
4. Why can't *mouais* appear in a work email?
   *It's a strictly spoken, informal register marker — it would sound flippant in writing to a colleague or client.*

With agreement and disagreement under control, the final lesson in this unit adds the last skill: hedging an opinion so it's neither a flat yes nor a flat no.
