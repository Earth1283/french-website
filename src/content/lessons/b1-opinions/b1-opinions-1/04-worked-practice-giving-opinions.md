# Worked Practice: Giving Opinions

Read each exchange, then work through the spot-the-mistake section before checking the self-check list.

## A film discussion

> **Léa:** *Alors, tu as vu le nouveau film de Dupieux ?*
>
> **You:** *Oui. Je trouve que c'est vraiment original, mais un peu long.*
>
> **Léa:** *Ah bon ? Moi, j'ai l'impression que c'est surtout bizarre pour être bizarre.*
>
> **You:** *Peut-être. À mon avis, il faut le voir deux fois pour vraiment comprendre.*

*Trouver que* names your personal reaction to something you watched. *J'ai l'impression que* signals Léa's is more of a hunch than a firm claim. *À mon avis* closes the exchange with a free-standing opinion — no *que* required.

## A work email

> *Bonjour Madame,*
>
> *Suite à notre réunion, il me semble que le calendrier proposé est trop serré. Selon moi, il faudrait prévoir deux semaines supplémentaires. Je reste à votre disposition pour en discuter.*

Notice the register shift from the film conversation: *il me semble que* and *selon moi* both read as measured and professional — exactly what you want in writing to a colleague, where *j'ai l'impression que* or *pour moi* would feel underdressed.

## Spot the mistake

Each sentence below has one error. Identify it before reading the fix.

1. *À mon avis que ce restaurant est trop cher.*
2. *Je trouve ce livre intéressant.*
3. *Il me semble, la réunion commence à 14h.*
4. *Pour moi, il a raison, mais je ne pense pas qu'.*

**Fixes:**

1. *À mon avis, ce restaurant est trop cher.* — Adverbial phrases like *à mon avis* never take *que*; drop it and let the clause follow directly.
2. *Je trouve que ce livre est intéressant.* — *Trouver que* needs its *que* and a full clause; you can't drop the conjunction the way English drops "that."
3. *Il me semble que la réunion commence à 14h.* — *Il me semble* is a verb phrase, not an adverbial one; it always needs *que* before its clause.
4. *Pour moi, il a raison, mais je ne suis pas sûr.* — *Que* can't dangle alone at the end of a sentence with nothing after it; if you're not finishing a clause, don't leave a bare *que* hanging.

## Self-check

1. Which three verbs take *que* + a judgment clause, and which one is best for reacting to something you just experienced?
   *Penser, croire, trouver que — trouver que is the one for direct personal reactions.*
2. Name the three adverbial opinion phrases that never take *que*.
   *À mon avis, selon moi, pour moi.*
3. Which of the two "impression" phrases is more casual: *il me semble que* or *j'ai l'impression que*?
   *J'ai l'impression que.*
4. True or false: negating *je pense que* has no grammatical effect on the verb that follows.
   *False — it changes the mood of the following verb, a rule covered in a later lesson.*

If you answered all four without hesitating, you're ready to start agreeing — and disagreeing — with other people's opinions in the next lesson.
