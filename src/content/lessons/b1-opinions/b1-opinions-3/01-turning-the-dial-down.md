# Turning the Dial Down: Plutôt, Assez, Un Peu, Pas Vraiment

A flat "yes" or "no" opinion is rare in real conversation. Most opinions live somewhere in between, and French has a small set of dial-adjusting words that let you say exactly how strongly you mean something, instead of overstating or understating your view.

## Plutôt: correcting the aim

*Plutôt* means "rather" in the sense of "more accurately" — it refines a description rather than just softening it.

> *C'est plutôt compliqué.* — It's rather complicated.

It's also useful for correcting your own or someone else's word choice mid-sentence:

> *Je dirais plutôt que c'est risqué.* — I'd say, rather, that it's risky.

## Assez: a genuine false-friend trap

*Assez* is one of the more dangerous words at this level, because it means two different things depending on where it sits in the sentence.

Before an adjective, *assez* softens it to "fairly" or "quite":

> *C'est assez intéressant.* — It's fairly interesting.

After a verb, describing a quantity, *assez* means "enough":

> *J'ai assez mangé.* — I've eaten enough.

English speakers sometimes read *assez intéressant* as "interesting enough" (implying it barely cleared some bar), when it actually means "quite interesting" — a plainly positive, moderate-strength opinion. Position is everything here; there's no shortcut but to notice which structure you're in.

## Un peu: a small, often polite, downgrade

*Un peu* — "a little" — softens a statement, sometimes for accuracy and sometimes for politeness, especially when the honest opinion is mildly negative.

> *Je suis un peu fatigué.* — I'm a little tired.
>
> *C'est un peu cher, non ?* — It's a little expensive, isn't it?

That second example is a very French move: rather than a flat "it's too expensive," *un peu* plus a tag question invites the other person to agree, which feels less confrontational than a direct complaint.

## Pas vraiment: a gentle no

*Pas vraiment* — "not really" — softens a negative opinion the way *un peu* softens a positive one. It's less blunt than a flat *non* and leaves room for explanation.

> *Pas vraiment, ce n'est pas mon avis.* — Not really, that's not my view.

## Stacking the dial

These words combine naturally with the opinion phrases from earlier lessons to fine-tune a view:

> *Je trouve que c'est un peu cher, mais plutôt bon.* — I find it's a little expensive, but rather good.

That kind of layered, qualified opinion — expensive, yes, but good, and I'm not fully committing to either point — reads as measured and considerate in French, the sound of someone thinking carefully rather than reacting impulsively.
