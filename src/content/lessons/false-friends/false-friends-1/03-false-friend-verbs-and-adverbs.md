# False Friends: Verbs and Adverbs

Verb false friends are especially tricky, because verbs shape the whole sentence. This chapter covers the ones you are most likely to say or hear.

## Verbs

| French | Looks like | Actually means | English verb is… |
|---|---|---|---|
| *attendre* | to attend | to wait | *assister à* |
| *rester* | to rest | to stay | *se reposer* |
| *demander* | to demand | to ask | *exiger* |
| *passer un examen* | to pass an exam | to take an exam | *réussir un examen* |
| *supporter* | to support | to tolerate | *soutenir* |
| *décevoir* | to deceive | to disappoint | *tromper* |
| *ignorer* | to ignore | to not know | *ne pas tenir compte de* |

## Examples in context

> *J'attends le bus.* — I'm waiting for the bus.
>
> *Je reste à la maison.* — I'm staying at home.
>
> *Je demande l'addition.* — I'm asking for the bill.
>
> *Je passe mon examen demain.* — I'm taking my exam tomorrow.

Each sentence would mean something else in English. *J'attends la conférence* means "I'm waiting for the conference", not "I'm attending."

## *Passer un examen*

This is the one that hurts students the most. If you say *J'ai passé l'examen*, you took it, but you did not necessarily succeed. To say you passed, use *réussir*:

> *J'ai réussi mon examen.* — I passed my exam.

## *Supporter* and *décevoir*

*Je ne supporte pas le bruit.* means "I can't stand noise." Using it for "support" is a typical error. A French speaker would say *Je soutiens cette idée* or *Je suis pour.*

*Il m'a déçu* means "he disappointed me." The noun is *une déception*. The word for deceiving someone is *tromper.*

## Adverbs

| French | Looks like | Actually means | English adverb is… |
|---|---|---|---|
| *actuellement* | actually | currently | *en fait* / *vraiment* |
| *éventuellement* | eventually | possibly | *finalement* / *à la fin* |
| *finalement* | finally | in the end | *enfin* |

The first two are classic. *Actuellement, j'habite à Lyon* means "currently I live in Lyon." If you want "actually", say *en fait.*

> *En fait, je ne suis pas d'accord.* — Actually, I don't agree.

*Éventuellement* often appears in business emails: *Nous pourrons éventuellement vous rappeler* means "we may possibly call you back."

## The pattern

In every case, the French word keeps an older or narrower meaning. If a word seems too obvious, and the sentence feels odd, pause. French usually has a different word for the English meaning you want.
