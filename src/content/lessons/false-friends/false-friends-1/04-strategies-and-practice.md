# Strategies and Practice

You do not need to memorize every false friend. You need a method for noticing them and a short list of the ones you will meet most often. This chapter gives you both.

## Strategy 1: learn in pairs

Instead of learning *librairie = bookshop*, learn the pair:

- *librairie* = bookshop
- *bibliothèque* = library

Each pair gives you both the false friend and the correct word, so you can use either one confidently.

## Strategy 2: write a sentence

Write one sentence for each false friend you want to remember:

> *Je cherche une librairie pour acheter un livre.*
>
> *Je suis blessé au genou.*
>
> *Actuellement, j'habite à Paris.*

A sentence ties the word to a situation, which makes it easy to recall.

## Strategy 3: watch for warning signs

Slow down when:

- a word seems surprisingly easy
- the sentence sounds strange in English
- you are about to say something embarrassing

These are the moments when false friends strike.

## Practice round

Choose the correct meaning.

1. *Je vais rester à l'hôtel.* → I will (stay / rest) at the hotel.
2. *Elle est très sensible.* → She is very (sensitive / sensible).
3. *J'attends mon ami.* → I am (waiting for / attending) my friend.
4. *Actuellement, je travaille ici.* → (Currently / Actually), I work here.
5. *J'ai passé mon permis hier.* → I (took / passed) my driving test yesterday.

**Answers:** 1. stay. 2. sensitive. 3. waiting for. 4. Currently. 5. took.

## Last note

Everyone falls for false friends at first, including advanced learners. A French speaker who hears *Je suis excité* from a learner will smile, but understand. Keep going: catch one new false friend a week, and you will finish with a very strong, accurate vocabulary.
