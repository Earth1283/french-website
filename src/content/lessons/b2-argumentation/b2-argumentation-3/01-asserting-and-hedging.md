# Asserting and Hedging

A confident argument needs two opposite gears: phrases that assert something as certain, and phrases that hedge it as merely likely or debatable. French keeps these grammatically distinct in a way that trips up even strong B2 learners, because two expressions that both affirm the same fact can require opposite moods.

## Il est indéniable que: stating a certainty

*Il est indéniable que* — "it's undeniable that" — opens with maximum confidence and takes the indicative, exactly as you'd expect from a phrase announcing certainty.

> *Il est indéniable que le climat change.* — It's undeniable that the climate is changing.

Straightforward: the clause after *que* behaves like any ordinary factual statement.

## On ne peut nier que: the same certainty, the opposite mood

*On ne peut nier que* — "one cannot deny that" — means almost exactly the same thing as *il est indéniable que*. Both assert a fact as beyond question. But *on ne peut nier que* takes the SUBJUNCTIVE, because the verb *nier* ("to deny"), once negated, pulls the subjunctive in behind it — a grammatical habit French carries over from verbs of doubt and opinion, even when the actual meaning expressed is certainty, not doubt.

> *On ne peut nier qu'il y ait un problème.* — One cannot deny that there is a problem.

This is a genuine trap: the logic of the sentence says "certain," the grammar says "subjunctive." Learn it as a fixed pairing — *nier* negated, subjunctive follows — rather than trying to reason your way to the mood from the meaning.

## Why French does this

Verbs like *nier*, *douter*, and *penser* change their mood behaviour depending on whether they're affirmative or negative, because the negation reintroduces a trace of uncertainty into the sentence's logic, even when the speaker doesn't intend any. *Je pense qu'il a raison* (indicative — a real opinion, stated as fact) versus *je ne pense pas qu'il ait raison* (subjunctive — negating the opinion reopens the question). *Nier* behaves the same way once you negate it, which explains why *on ne peut nier que* keeps the subjunctive even though its message is total certainty.

- *Il est indéniable que* + indicative.
- *On ne peut nier que* + subjunctive — same meaning, opposite grammar.
- The trigger is the verb and its negation, not how certain the idea actually is.
