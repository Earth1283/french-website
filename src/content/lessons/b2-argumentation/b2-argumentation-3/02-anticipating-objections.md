# Anticipating Objections

The most sophisticated move in an argument isn't answering an objection after someone raises it — it's raising a strong version of it yourself, before anyone else can. This chapter covers two phrases built for exactly that.

## On pourrait objecter que

"One could object that" — this phrase lets you state the opposing view fully and fairly, in your own words, and then answer it in the very next sentence. It signals to the listener or reader that you've already thought the counterargument through, which is far more persuasive than leaving it for them to raise and looking caught off guard when they do.

> *On pourrait objecter que cette solution est irréaliste.* — One could object that this solution is unrealistic.
>
> *On pourrait objecter que cette mesure coûte cher à mettre en œuvre ; néanmoins, son coût reste inférieur à celui de l'inaction.* — One could object that this measure is expensive to implement; nevertheless, its cost remains lower than the cost of doing nothing.

The pattern is: state the objection with *on pourrait objecter que*, then answer it — often with a connector from lesson two, like *néanmoins* or *il n'en reste pas moins que*.

## Loin de moi l'idée de

"Far be it from me to" — a softening phrase used right before saying something that could sound harsh, dismissive, or provocative. It's slightly ironic in tone: the speaker signals humility while still making a pointed statement.

> *Loin de moi l'idée de vous contredire, mais les chiffres racontent une autre histoire.* — Far be it from me to contradict you, but the figures tell a different story.

Grammatically it's fixed: *loin de moi l'idée de* + infinitive. It doesn't change form for person or tense — the "far be it from me" framing does all the work regardless of who's speaking.

## Using both together

These two phrases pair naturally in a debate: *loin de moi l'idée de* softens the delivery, *on pourrait objecter que* handles the substance of anticipating pushback.

> *Loin de moi l'idée de minimiser vos efforts, mais on pourrait objecter que les résultats ne sont pas encore visibles.* — Far be it from me to minimise your efforts, but one could object that the results aren't visible yet.

- *On pourrait objecter que* — states the opposing view yourself, then answer it.
- *Loin de moi l'idée de* + infinitive — softens a pointed statement.
- Together, they let you disagree while sounding entirely reasonable — a very French combination.
