# Nuance and Debate Etiquette

The last piece of arguing like you went through the French system isn't a connector at all — it's knowing how to qualify your own claims, and how the conversation itself is allowed to move.

## Il convient de

"It is fitting to" or "it is appropriate to" — an impersonal, formal way to recommend an action or a way of looking at something, common in essays and official writing.

> *Il convient de nuancer cette affirmation.* — It is fitting to qualify this statement.

It softens a recommendation compared to a blunt imperative (*Nuancez cette affirmation !*), which would sound like an order rather than a suggestion — exactly the tone a formal argument avoids.

## Dans une certaine mesure

"To a certain extent" — the essential French hedge, used to agree partially without agreeing fully, or to limit the scope of a claim you're about to make.

> *Dans une certaine mesure, vous avez raison.* — To a certain extent, you're right.

This is often paired with a following clause that specifies the limit: *Dans une certaine mesure, vous avez raison, mais cela ne s'applique pas à tous les cas.*

## À mon sens

"In my view" or "to my mind" — a more literary register than the everyday *à mon avis*. Reach for *à mon sens* in a formal debate or written argument; save *à mon avis* for everyday conversation.

> *À mon sens, cet argument est faible.* — In my view, this argument is weak.

## Interruption is not rude

English-speaking learners often hold back in French conversation, waiting politely for a full pause before responding — and then find themselves never getting a turn. French debate culture, especially on televised panel shows and in oral exams, treats a well-timed interruption as a sign of genuine engagement, not bad manners. Staying completely silent can actually read as having nothing to contribute.

The key is interrupting *with* the right phrase, not just talking over someone:

> *Permettez-moi de vous interrompre une seconde.* — Allow me to interrupt you for a second.

This buys you a polite entry point into the exchange. Pair it with a hedge to soften the disagreement that usually follows:

> *Permettez-moi de vous interrompre : dans une certaine mesure, je suis d'accord, mais il convient de nuancer ce point.* — Allow me to interrupt: to a certain extent, I agree, but it's worth qualifying that point.

- *Il convient de* + infinitive — a formal, softened recommendation.
- *Dans une certaine mesure* — hedges a claim without abandoning it.
- *À mon sens* — literary "in my view"; keep *à mon avis* for casual talk.
- A polite interruption phrase is a genuine debate skill in French, not a rudeness to avoid.
