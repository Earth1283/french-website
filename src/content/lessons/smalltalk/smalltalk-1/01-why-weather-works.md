# Why Weather Works in France

A stranger in a queue, a neighbour on the stairs, the shopkeeper waiting for your card to clear: weather is the safest opening line in any French conversation. It is neutral, shared and easy to answer, and nobody needs to know your politics to comment on rain. The French do it constantly, often with the same two words.

## The core phrases

> *Il fait beau.* — The weather is nice.
>
> *Il fait mauvais.* — The weather is bad.
>
> *Il pleut.* — It's raining.
>
> *Il neige.* — It's snowing.
>
> *Il fait chaud / il fait froid.* — It's hot / it's cold.

Five short sentences cover almost every exchange you will have about the sky. Pair them with a sigh or a shrug and you sound local.

## Weather as a ritual

Weather talk is less a report than a greeting. *Il fait beau, non ?* — Nice out, isn't it? — invites agreement rather than information. You do not need to add anything. A simple *Oui, c'est vrai* is a complete reply.

If you want to ask the question yourself, use *Quel temps fait-il aujourd'hui ?* — What's the weather like today? For a dramatic reaction, *Quel temps !* — What weather! — works on almost any day.

## Seasons and expectations

France has real seasons, and Paris in August can be hot enough to make the phrase *Il fait chaud* feel urgent. Expect small talk about heat to turn into a discussion of where people are going on holiday. Winter brings *Il fait froid* and, now and then, *Il neige*. Do not be surprised if people talk about the weather for longer than the forecast warrants. That is the point.
