# A Small Talk Exchange at the Bakery

Here is a realistic exchange at the bakery counter, where weather is a reliable way to fill the pause while the bread is wrapped. Read the French, then explain each line to yourself before checking the translation.

> **Baker:** *Bonjour ! Il fait beau aujourd'hui, non ?*
>
> **You:** *Oui, il fait très beau. Mais on dit qu'il va pleuvoir cet après-midi.*
>
> **Baker:** *Ah, c'est dommage. Vous voulez autre chose ?*
>
> **You:** *Non, merci. Une baguette, s'il vous plaît.*
>
> **Baker:** *Voilà. Bonne journée !*
>
> **You:** *Merci, vous aussi.*

The first reply agrees, then adds a fact using *on dit* ("they say") and the near future *il va pleuvoir*. That is enough to show you have listened. *Voilà* is the baker's way of handing over the bread, and *vous aussi* returns the good wish without any fuss.

## When it goes wrong

If you miss the forecast, ask *Pardon, vous pouvez répéter ?* If you heard *pleuvoir* but are not sure, confirm with *Il va pleuvoir ?* and a rising tone. Bakers are used to repeating themselves, and a confirmed fact is always better than a guess.

The point of the exchange is not the forecast. It is the willingness to say something, however small, and then get back to the bread.

## Listening for the weather word

You do not need every word in a forecast. Listen for the weather word itself, *pleuvoir*, *neiger*, *beau*, *froid*, and the time phrase that comes with it, such as *demain* or *cet après-midi*. Those three pieces are enough to decide whether to take an umbrella.
