# The Grammar of Feeling and Praise

The expressions in this lesson are short, but they hide some useful grammar. Most of it is about agreement, and a little of it is about where the negation goes.

## Adjectives agree with the speaker

*Je suis fatigué* is said by a man. A woman says *je suis fatiguée*, adding an *-e* that you hear in the pronunciation. The same is true of *content*, which becomes *contente*. In the masculine, the final *t* is silent. In the feminine, it is pronounced. The *(e)* in the textbook shows you where the difference lies.

## *C'est* with an adjective is masculine

*C'est magnifique* uses the masculine form because *c'est* refers to a general idea, not to a specific noun. For a feminine noun, the adjective agrees: *cette vue est magnifique*, a magnificent view, or *elle est magnifique* for a woman. The shortcut works for everyday exclamations, but do not stretch it too far.

## *Sympa* never changes

*Sympa* never changes for gender: *il est sympa*, *elle est sympa*. In the plural it takes a silent *-s* (*ils sont sympas*), so it sounds the same everywhere, which makes it one of the easiest compliments to use.

## Where the negation sits

In *je n'aime pas trop ça*, the *ne* and *pas* wrap around the verb *aimer*. *Trop* comes after *pas* and means "very much" in a negative sentence, so *pas trop* means "not really." Pronounce the elision as *ʒə nɛm*, with the *e* of *je* dropped before the vowel.

## Verbs of liking

*J'adore ça* uses the regular *-er* verb *adorer*. The pronoun *ça* points back to something you have seen or eaten. *Ça* is also the everyday word for "that," which makes it useful for almost any reaction.
