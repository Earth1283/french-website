# A Dinner Party Exchange

Here is a realistic exchange at a dinner party. The host is a stranger, so the register is *vous*, and the compliments are measured. Read the French first, then explain each line in your own words.

> **Host:** *Vous avez fait bon voyage ? Entrez, entrez !*
>
> **You:** *Merci. Je suis fatigué(e), mais je suis content(e) d'être là. Votre appartement est magnifique !*
>
> **Host:** *Merci beaucoup ! Vous aimez le fromage ?*
>
> **You:** *J'adore ça !*
>
> **Host:** *Très bien. Il y a du poisson aussi.*
>
> **You:** *Je n'aime pas trop le poisson, mais c'est très gentil.*

The first reply names your state, then your pleasure, then a compliment on the flat. Each line does one job. The reply about fish is the key move: it refuses politely, then thanks the host for the effort.

## When it goes wrong

If you did not catch the question, say *Pardon, je n'ai pas bien compris.* If the food is not to your taste, a famous French phrase helps: *C'est intéressant.* It means "that's interesting," and in polite company it signals that you are not enthusiastic without saying so outright. Use it sparingly, and never with the wrong tone.

The goal is not to praise everything. It is to be warm without performing, and honest without being rude.

## Why the structure works

Each of your replies follows a simple shape: a feeling, a specific compliment, then a polite response to the offer. Hosts rarely expect perfect grammar from a guest. They do notice whether you respond to what they said, and this exchange does that without a single superlative too many.
