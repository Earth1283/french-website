# A Journey from Taxi to Platform

Here is a full trip put together: a taxi to the station, a ticket, a question about the platform, and a plan for when something goes wrong. Read it aloud once and identify the sentence that does each job.

## The taxi

> **You:** *Bonjour. Je voudrais aller à la gare de Lyon, s'il vous plaît.*
>
> **Driver:** *Vous êtes pressé ?*
>
> **You:** *Oui, mon train part dans vingt minutes.*
>
> **Driver:** *D'accord, on y va.*

*Vous êtes pressé ?* means "Are you in a hurry?" *Dans vingt minutes* means "in twenty minutes," and the sentence gives the driver a reason to choose the fastest route.

## The ticket counter

> **You:** *Bonjour. Un aller-retour pour Marseille, s'il vous plaît.*
>
> **Clerk:** *Pour quelle date ?*
>
> **You:** *Aujourd'hui, et retour demain.* 
>
> **Clerk:** *Voilà votre billet. Le train part du quai numéro douze.*

The clerk will often answer with a platform number. Listen for *quai*, which means platform, and repeat the number back: *Quai douze ?*

## On the platform

> **You:** *Pardon, c'est bien le train pour Marseille ?*
>
> **Passenger:** *Oui, c'est ça.*
>
> **You:** *Merci. Il faut composter mon billet ?*

If your ticket is electronic, you will not need the machine. If it is paper and marked *à composter*, stamp it before you board.

## When something goes wrong

Trains are reliable in France, but delays happen. Keep these lines ready:

> *Je me suis trompé de quai.* — I got the wrong platform.
>
> *Mon train a du retard.* — My train is delayed.
>
> *Pouvez-vous me montrer sur la carte ?* — Can you show me on the map?
>
> *Je n'ai pas compris, pouvez-vous répéter ?* — I did not understand, can you repeat?

If you took the wrong train, *Je me suis trompé de train* is the sentence you need. It is honest, short, and gives staff what they need to fix it.

Notice the pattern: greet, state the destination, confirm details, and ask for help clearly when the plan breaks. The same structure works for buses, metro lines, and taxis.
