# Traps for English Speakers on Transport

English gives you one verb, "to want," and one word for everything you can ride. French separates these ideas more carefully. The traps below come from direct translation, and each one has a simple fix.

## *Je veux* versus *je voudrais*

English "I want to go to the station" translates literally to *Je veux aller à la gare*. It is grammatical, but it sounds blunt to a ticket clerk. *Je voudrais* softens the request without making it complicated. Use it by default.

## *Ticket* versus *billet*

In the metro, French people say *un ticket*. For trains, the word is *un billet*. A clerk who hears "un ticket de train" will understand you, but *un billet* is what appears on the screen and on the paper. Using it shows that you know which system you are in.

## *Simple* does not mean "simple ticket"

*Un aller simple* is the correct phrase for a one-way ticket. Asking for *un ticket simple* may produce a confused look, because the adjective does not carry the meaning you intend.

## The *à* contraction

English speakers often say *à le* or *à les*. The French forms are *au* and *aux*. Before a feminine noun, use *à la*, and before a vowel sound, use *à l'*. A quick mental check solves most problems: if the next word starts with a consonant and is masculine, the form is probably *au*.

## Asking "when" versus "what time"

*Quand* asks about a date or a general moment. *À quelle heure* asks for a specific time. If you want the departure time, use *à quelle heure*. Using *quand* can get you a day of the week rather than a clock time.

## Forgetting *s'il vous plaît*

French requests often end with *s'il vous plaît*. Put it at the end, not at the beginning. *Un billet, s'il vous plaît* is correct, and it signals courtesy after the request has been stated.

## Assuming all trains work the same way

In some regional stations, paper tickets labeled *à composter* must be validated before boarding. The machine is usually yellow and easy to miss. If you are unsure, look at the ticket and at the machines on the platform. Nobody will be impressed by a confident guess.
