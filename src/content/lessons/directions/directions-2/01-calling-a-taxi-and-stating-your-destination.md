# Calling a Taxi and Stating Your Destination

Taxis and trains are where French efficiency meets French paperwork. Most exchanges are short, so a few well-built sentences will carry you through. This chapter covers the taxi and the ticket counter. The next chapter explains the grammar behind them.

## Calling a taxi

An app is usually faster, but a phone call is a useful backup, especially late at night or when your phone is nearly dead:

> *Bonjour, appelez-moi un taxi, s'il vous plaît.* — Hello, call me a taxi, please.
>
> *Je suis à l'hôtel Lafayette, rue de la Paix.* — I am at the Lafayette hotel, rue de la Paix.

Give your location first, then your destination. Dispatchers can see a map, but they cannot see you.

## Stating where you want to go

The reliable frame is *Je voudrais aller à…* followed by a place:

> *Je voudrais aller à la gare du Nord.* — I would like to go to the Gare du Nord.
>
> *Je voudrais aller à l'aéroport.* — I would like to go to the airport.
>
> *Je voudrais aller à la Tour Eiffel.* — I would like to go to the Eiffel Tower.

Use *je voudrais* rather than *je veux*. Both are understood, but *je voudrais* sounds like someone who has planned the trip and is asking politely.

## At the train station

Buying a train ticket usually means choosing between two options:

| French | Meaning |
|---|---|
| *un aller simple* | one-way ticket |
| *un aller-retour* | round trip |

Ask for *Un aller-retour pour Lyon, s'il vous plaît.* Then check the timing:

> *À quelle heure part le train ?* — What time does the train leave?

*Partir* means "to leave," so this is literally "at what hour leaves the train?" The question is short, standard, and understood across France.

## Validating your paper ticket

Some paper tickets are marked *à composter* and must be stamped in a yellow machine before boarding. You will see the instruction:

> *Compostez votre billet !* — Validate your ticket!

Skipping this step is not a small oversight. Inspectors can issue a fine, and "I did not know" is not a reliable defense. E-tickets and reserved-seat tickets do not need stamping, but check the label before you assume.

Treat the taxi and the ticket counter as one short story: call, state the destination, confirm the plan. That pattern covers most transport situations you will meet.
