# Check Distance and Repair Misunderstandings

Before following a route, find out whether walking makes sense. *C'est loin ?* means “Is it far?” Useful answers include:

- *Non, c'est tout près.* — No, it is very close.
- *C'est à cinq minutes à pied.* — It is five minutes on foot.
- *C'est assez loin.* — It is fairly far.
- *Il vaut mieux prendre le métro.* — It is better to take the metro.

*À pied* means “on foot.” Other transport expressions use *en*: *en métro, en bus, en voiture,* and *en taxi*. A bicycle commonly uses *à vélo*.

## Ask for one missing detail

Do not request the entire explanation again if only one step is unclear. Ask a targeted question:

> *La première ou la deuxième rue ?* — The first or second street?
>
> *À gauche après la banque ?* — Left after the bank?
>
> *Quel arrêt ?* — Which stop?
>
> *Combien de minutes à pied ?* — How many minutes on foot?

For a full repeat, say *Pouvez-vous répéter plus lentement, s'il vous plaît ?* (“Can you repeat more slowly, please?”).

## Confirm with your own summary

The safest technique is to repeat the route in compressed form:

> *Alors, tout droit, deuxième rue à droite, puis en face de la poste. C'est bien ça ?*
>
> So, straight ahead, second street on the right, then opposite the post office. Is that correct?

*Alors* introduces your summary; *C'est bien ça ?* asks for confirmation. A map makes this even easier: *Vous pouvez me montrer sur la carte ?* (“Can you show me on the map?”).

## Know the limits of vague answers

*Par là* means “over that way,” and *ce n'est pas loin* only means the speaker does not consider it far. Ask for minutes, a number of streets, or a landmark when precision matters. Directions are successful when you can identify the next action—not when you understood every word spoken.
