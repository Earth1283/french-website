# Decode Route Instructions One Piece at a Time

Most directions use a small set of verbs and landmarks. Listen for the action first, then the location where it happens.

## The essential actions

| French instruction | Meaning |
|---|---|
| *Allez tout droit.* | Go straight ahead. |
| *Tournez à gauche.* | Turn left. |
| *Tournez à droite.* | Turn right. |
| *Prenez la première rue à gauche.* | Take the first street on the left. |
| *Traversez la rue.* | Cross the street. |
| *Continuez jusqu'au feu.* | Continue as far as the traffic light. |

These use the polite/plural command form, which often ends in *-ez*. You do not need to conjugate the verbs to understand the instruction; recognize *allez, tournez, prenez, traversez,* and *continuez* as actions.

## Sequence words

Directions are a chain. These words show the order:

- *d'abord* — first
- *puis / ensuite* — then / next
- *après* — after
- *jusqu'à* — as far as / until
- *enfin* — finally

> *D'abord, allez tout droit. Ensuite, tournez à droite après la banque.*
>
> First, go straight. Next, turn right after the bank.

## Landmark language

*Avant* means “before” and *après* means “after.” *En face de* means “opposite,” *à côté de* means “next to,” and *entre* means “between.”

> *La pharmacie est en face de la poste.* — The pharmacy is opposite the post office.
>
> *L'entrée est à côté du café.* — The entrance is next to the café.
>
> *Le distributeur est entre la banque et l'hôtel.* — The ATM is between the bank and the hotel.

Listen for nouns you can see around you. Even if you miss the grammar, “right—bank—opposite pharmacy” may be enough to reconstruct the route. Then confirm rather than pretending you understood everything.
