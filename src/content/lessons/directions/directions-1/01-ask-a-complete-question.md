# Ask a Complete Direction Question

*Où est… ?* (“Where is…?”) is the shortest useful direction frame. Add the place with the correct article and you can ask for almost anything.

> *Où est la gare ?* — Where is the train station?
>
> *Où est le musée ?* — Where is the museum?
>
> *Où sont les toilettes ?* — Where are the toilets?

Notice the verb: use *est* for one place and *sont* for a plural noun such as *les toilettes*. In spoken travel French, the singular frame will still often be understood, but learning the distinction lets you build accurate sentences.

## Ask how to get there

*Où est… ?* asks for location. *Comment aller à… ?* asks for a route:

> *Comment aller à la gare ?* — How do I get to the station?
>
> *Comment aller au Louvre ?* — How do I get to the Louvre?

The preposition changes with the noun:

| Pattern | Example |
|---|---|
| *à la* + feminine place | *à la gare* |
| *au* + masculine place | *au musée* |
| *à l'* + vowel sound | *à l'aéroport* |
| *aux* + plural place | *aux toilettes* |

*Au* is the required contraction of *à + le*; do not say *à le musée*.

## Open the interaction properly

With a stranger, the full pattern is:

> *Bonjour, excusez-moi. Où est la station de métro la plus proche, s'il vous plaît ?*

This means “Hello, excuse me. Where is the nearest metro station, please?” The greeting and *excusez-moi* claim attention politely; *la plus proche* adds “the nearest.”

If you are unsure whether the person knows, begin with *Vous savez où est… ?* (“Do you know where … is?”). In everyday speech, this sounds natural and gives the listener an easy way to say *désolé, je ne sais pas* (“sorry, I don’t know”).
