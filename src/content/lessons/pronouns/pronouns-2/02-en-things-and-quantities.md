# En: The Word That Carries a Quantity

Where *y* stands in for *à*, *en* stands in for *de* — and it has a second job English speakers find genuinely strange: *en* keeps a quantity alive in the sentence even after the noun itself has vanished.

> *Tu as des frères ?* — *Oui, j'en ai.*
>
> *Tu manges du fromage ?* — *Oui, j'en mange.*
>
> *Tu parles de ton travail ?* — *Oui, j'en parle.*

## Replacing de + noun

Just like *y* replaces *à + noun*, *en* replaces *de + noun* — the partitive article (*du, de la, de l'*), the plural indefinite article (*des*), or *de* after a verb that governs it (*parler de, avoir besoin de, se souvenir de*):

> *J'ai besoin de vacances.* → *J'en ai besoin.*
>
> *Elle se souvient de ce voyage.* → *Elle s'en souvient.*

## The quantity rule: en doesn't disappear with a number

This is the detail that separates a real command of *en* from a textbook one. When you state *how many* or *how much*, French still needs *en* to represent "of it/them," even though English drops the equivalent entirely:

> *Tu veux du café ?* — *J'en veux un peu.* (I want a little [of it])
>
> *Combien de frères as-tu ?* — *J'en ai deux.* (I have two [of them])
>
> *Est-ce qu'il reste des places ?* — *Il en reste plusieurs.* (There are several [of them] left)

Say "j'ai deux" on its own and it sounds like a sentence with a piece missing — because in French, it is. Whatever the quantity word is (*un, deux, plusieurs, beaucoup, un peu, trop*), *en* has to appear before the verb to anchor what you're counting.

## Fixed chunks worth memorizing whole

- *je m'en vais* — I'm leaving (s'en aller — the *en* is part of the idiom itself, not a stand-in for anything)
- *j'en ai marre (de...)* — I'm fed up (with...)
- *il y en a* — there is/are some (*y* + *en* stack, always in that order)

## Quick check

- *Tu as des questions ?* — *Non, je n'___ ai pas.* (en)
- *Combien de croissants tu veux ?* — *J'___ veux trois.* (en)
- What's the difference between *j'ai faim* and *j'en ai marre*? (the second is a fixed idiom — *en* isn't replacing anything, it's built into *en avoir marre*)
