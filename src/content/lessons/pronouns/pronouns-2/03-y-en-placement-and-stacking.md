# Placement, Negation, and Stacking Y with En

*Y* and *en* follow the same placement rules as the object pronouns from the previous lesson — but they also combine with each other and with other pronouns more often than *le/la/les* do, so it's worth seeing the patterns laid out together.

## Before the conjugated verb, always

Like *le/la/les*, both *y* and *en* sit directly before the verb they belong to:

> *J'y vais.* / *Je n'y vais pas.*
>
> *J'en veux.* / *Je n'en veux pas.*

In the negative, *ne* slides in front of the pronoun, exactly as it did with direct and indirect objects: *ne + y/en + verb + pas*.

## Before the infinitive when there's a second verb

> *Tu vas à la banque ?* — *Oui, je vais y aller.*
>
> *Tu veux du gâteau ?* — *Non merci, je ne veux pas en manger.*

Same rule as *le vais l'appeler* from the previous lesson: the pronoun attaches to the verb that actually governs it, which is usually the infinitive.

## The passé composé: no agreement

Unlike direct object pronouns, *y* and *en* never trigger agreement on the past participle, even when they come before *avoir*:

> *J'ai acheté des pommes.* → *J'en ai acheté.* (not *achetées* — no agreement with *en*)

This is a genuinely useful shortcut: if the pronoun is *y* or *en*, don't touch the participle's ending.

## Stacking y and en: il y en a

The single most common stack is *il y a* plus *en*, producing *il y en a* — "there is/are some." The order is fixed: *y* always comes before *en*.

> *Il y a du lait ?* — *Oui, il y en a.*
>
> *Il n'y en a plus.* — There's none left.

Try saying *il y en a* slowly, syllable by syllable, a few times — it's a tongue-twister for beginners but becomes one fluid sound with practice: roughly "eel-yan-na."

## Quick check

- *Tu vas aller au concert ?* — *Oui, je vais ___ aller.* (y)
- *Il reste du pain ?* — *Non, il n'___ ___ a plus.* (y / en)
- *Tu as mangé des huîtres ?* — *Oui, j'___ ai mangé.* — does the participle agree here? (en — no, en never triggers agreement)
