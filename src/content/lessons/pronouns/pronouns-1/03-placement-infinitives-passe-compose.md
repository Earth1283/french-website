# Where the Pronoun Goes: Infinitives, Passé Composé, and Agreement

A single-verb sentence is easy: the pronoun sits right before the conjugated verb. Add a second verb — an infinitive, or a compound past tense — and the pronoun has to pick a new spot. This chapter is about finding it.

## Before the infinitive, not the conjugated verb

When a conjugated verb is followed by an infinitive (*aller + infinitif*, *vouloir + infinitif*, *pouvoir + infinitif*), the object pronoun jumps past the conjugated verb and sits directly in front of the infinitive it actually belongs to:

> *Tu vas appeler le médecin ?* — *Oui, je vais l'appeler.*
>
> *Je peux vous aider.*
>
> *Elle veut leur parler.*

The logic: the pronoun belongs to whichever verb governs the object. *Appeler le médecin* — the calling is what's being done to the doctor, so the pronoun attaches to *appeler*, not to *vais*. Learners who default to English word order often place it before the conjugated verb instead (*"je le vais appeler"*) — that's never correct in modern French.

## In the passé composé: before avoir/être

With a compound tense, the pronoun goes before the auxiliary verb (*avoir* or *être*), not before the past participle:

> *Tu as vu le film ?* — *Oui, je l'ai vu.*
>
> *Elle a téléphoné à ses parents ?* — *Oui, elle leur a téléphoné.*

## Agreement: the one place le/la/les changes the spelling

Here is the detail that separates careful French from sloppy French: when a **direct object pronoun** comes *before* a verb conjugated with *avoir*, the past participle agrees with it in gender and number — exactly like an adjective would.

> *J'ai vu ma sœur.* → *Je l'ai vu**e**.* (feminine, so *vu* gains an *e*)
>
> *J'ai acheté les billets.* → *Je les ai achet**és**.* (masculine plural, so *acheté* gains *és*)
>
> *J'ai invité mes amies.* → *Je les ai invit**ées**.* (feminine plural)

This agreement **only** happens with a preceding direct object. Indirect objects (*lui*, *leur*) never trigger it, because agreement tracks the direct object specifically:

> *J'ai téléphoné à ma sœur.* → *Je **lui** ai téléphoné.* — no agreement; *téléphoné* stays as is, because *lui* is indirect.

Native speakers barely think about this rule in speech (many of the endings are silent), but it matters in writing and at this level examiners will look for it.

## Putting it together

Try building these mentally before checking:

- "I saw them (masc.)." → *Je les ai vus.*
- "I'm going to call her." → *Je vais l'appeler.*
- "He answered her." → *Il lui a répondu.* (no agreement — indirect)

If the third one tripped you up, go back to the previous chapter — the *à*-verbs are the single biggest source of errors in this whole topic.
