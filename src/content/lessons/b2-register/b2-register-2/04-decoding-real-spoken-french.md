# Decoding Real Spoken French

Let's put it all together with real-sounding dialogue, a decoding exercise, and a self-check.

## Dialogue — Two colleagues after work

> **Nora :** Ça va, t'as fini ?
>
> **Karim :** Ouais, enfin chais pas, j'ai encore un mail à envoyer. Du coup je pars dans genre dix minutes.
>
> **Nora :** Ok, on t'attend au bar en bas alors ?
>
> **Karim :** Ouais carrément, y a pas de souci. On est en mode "vendredi soir," on va pas se presser.

## Decode it

Write out (mentally or on paper) the "full written" version of Karim's second line before checking below.

**Full version:** *Oui, enfin je ne sais pas, j'ai encore un e-mail à envoyer. Par conséquent, je pars dans environ dix minutes.*

Notice how much shorter and more relaxed the spoken version feels once you can hear "chais pas," "du coup," and "genre" doing their jobs instead of treating them as noise.

## Spot the mistake

One of these three sentences contains a real spoken-French grammar error (not just an informal tone). Find it.

1. *On va au marché, tu viens ?*
2. *On allons voir le film ce soir ?*
3. *Y a plus de café, chais pas où on en achète.*

**Answer:** Sentence 2. *On* takes third-person singular conjugation, never the *nous* ending — it should be *On va voir le film ce soir ?* Sentences 1 and 3 are informal but grammatically consistent with how *on* and dropped *ne* actually work.

## Self-check

- [ ] Can you expand *"chais pas"* back to its full written form? (*je ne sais pas*)
- [ ] Do you know why *"on allons"* is wrong, even though "on" means "we"? (*on* conjugates like *il/elle* — third person singular — not like *nous*)
- [ ] Can you identify *du coup*, *genre*, and sentence-final *quoi* as fillers rather than content words carrying real meaning?
- [ ] Would you recognise *"y a"* as *"il y a"* if you heard it at conversational speed?

If you answered yes to all four, you're now equipped to follow a real French conversation instead of the textbook version of one. Next up: the opposite end of the spectrum — formal written French, and how to read (and write) it properly.
