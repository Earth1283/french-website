# Contractions and Flow: *du, au, des, aux*

Elision and liaison shape how sounds move between words. There is a third mechanism that works on spelling and grammar: **contraction**. Two small words fuse into one. Unlike elision, it is not about vowels meeting. It is about *à* and *de* meeting *le* and *les*.

## The four contractions

| Combination | Contracted form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| *à + le* | **au** | *Je vais au marché.* |
| *à + les* | **aux** | *Je parle aux enfants.* |
| *de + le* | **du** | *Je viens du cinéma.* |
| *de + les* | **des** | *Le nom des rues.* |

These are **mandatory**. You can never say *à le* or *de le*. French speakers simply do not produce those sequences.

## What does not contract

*La* and *l'* do **not** contract with *à* or *de*:

- *à la plage* (to the beach)
- *à l'hôtel* (to the hotel)
- *de la gare* (from the station)
- *de l'aéroport* (from the airport)

Only masculine singular *le* and the plural *les* fuse. That is why so many everyday places have different forms: *au cinéma, à la banque, à l'école, aux toilettes*.

## Contraction or partitive?

*Du* and *des* have a second job. They can also mean "some":

- *Je viens du marché.* — I come **from the** market (contraction).
- *Je veux du pain.* — I want **some** bread (partitive article).

The spelling is the same, but the grammar differs. Test by substitution: if you can replace it with *de la*, *de l'*, or "from the," you are dealing with contraction. If it means "some," it is partitive.

## A practice routine

Say each phrase aloud, and listen for elision, liaison, and contraction working together:

1. *Je vais au cinéma avec un ami.* (contraction + liaison)
2. *Il habite à l'hôtel.* (elision)
3. *Elle parle aux enfants.* (contraction)
4. *Nous venons des États-Unis.* (contraction + liaison)
5. *J'arrive de l'aéroport.* (elision twice)

## Shadowing for flow

Pick one audio clip from the lesson. Play one sentence, pause, and repeat it with the same rhythm. Do not translate while you do it. After five days, you will notice you are linking, eliding, and contracting without thinking. That is the goal: not to apply rules, but to stop noticing them.
