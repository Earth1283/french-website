# When Not to Link: Forbidden and Optional Liaison

If liaison were always applied, French would be simple. It is not. Some links are **forbidden**, and some are **optional** depending on style. Knowing the forbidden ones prevents the most noticeable mistakes.

## Never link

Liaison is **forbidden** in these situations:

1. **After *et* ("and").** *Un garçon et une fille* is *et* + pause. You never say *ay-tune*.
2. **Between a singular noun and the adjective or verb after it.** *Un restaurant italien* has no *t* sound. *Le soldat arrive* has none either.
3. **Between a plural noun subject and its verb.** *Les enfants arrivent* is *lay zahn-fahn | a-reev*. The noun and verb stay apart.
4. **Before an aspirated h.** *Les haricots* is *lay a-ree-koh*, with no *z* sound. Compare *les hôtels* → *lay zo-tel*, which does link.
5. **Before *oui*, *onze*, *huit*.** *Les onze enfants*, *des oui*.
6. **After a proper name or in front of one.** *Paris est belle* has no *z*.

The good news is that the forbidden cases are mostly about grammar structure: nouns separate from what follows them.

## Optional liaison

In some contexts, liaison depends on how formal you are being:

- After *pas*: *pas encore* can be *pa-zahn-kor* (careful) or *pa ahn-kor* (casual)
- After many verbs: *Ils sont arrivés* usually links in careful speech, less often in rapid casual talk
- After adverbs like *plus*, *moins*, *très*: more likely in formal registers

A good rule: **more careful speech means more liaison, more casual speech means less.** Radio presenters and news readers link heavily. Friends in a café link only the mandatory ones.

## The learner's strategy

You do not need to master the optional cases. Here is a practical plan:

1. **Always do the mandatory ones.** Article + noun, pronoun + verb, short adjective + noun.
2. **Never do the forbidden ones.** Especially after *et* and before an aspirated h.
3. **Skip the optional ones** until you hear them naturally.

This keeps you safe in all registers. You will never sound wrong by omitting an optional link. You can sound wrong by adding a forbidden one, or by missing a mandatory one.

## Why this matters for listening

If you expect liaison, you hear *vous-z-avez* as "vous avez," not as a mystery word beginning with *z*. Many beginners cannot parse speech because they do not realize the extra sounds are borrowed from the previous word.
