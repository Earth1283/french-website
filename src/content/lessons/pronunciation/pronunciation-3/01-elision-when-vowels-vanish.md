# Elision: When Vowels Vanish

French dislikes two vowels meeting in a row. When a short word ending in a vowel is followed by a word that begins with a vowel sound, the first vowel **drops** and is replaced by an apostrophe. This is called **elision**, and it is one of the main reasons French flows instead of stuttering.

## The words that elide

Only a small group of one-syllable words elide. Learn the list and you cover almost everything:

| Full word | Before a vowel | Example |
|---|---|---|
| *le* / *la* | *l'* | *l'ami, l'école* |
| *je* | *j'* | *j'aime, j'habite* |
| *me* | *m'* | *il m'aide* |
| *te* | *t'* | *je t'adore* |
| *se* | *s'* | *il s'appelle* |
| *de* | *d'* | *un verre d'eau* |
| *ne* | *n'* | *je n'ai pas* |
| *que* | *qu'* | *qu'est-ce que* |
| *ce* | *c'* | *c'est* |

You have been using this since lesson one: *J'ai besoin d'aide* contains two elisions, *c'est* contains one.

## *Si* is a special case

*Si* ("if") elides **only before *il* and *ils***: *s'il vous plaît* (literally "if it pleases you"), *s'ils arrivent*. Before anything else, it stays whole: *si elle vient*, *si on veut*.

## The silent h follows the vowel rule

Words that begin with a **mute h** elide as though they began with a vowel: *l'hôtel*, *l'homme*, *j'habite*. Words with an **aspirated h** block elision: *le haricot*, *la honte*. This was covered in the previous lesson.

## Three words that refuse elision

A few words begin with a vowel letter or a vowel-like sound but behave like consonants, so the article in front stays whole:

- *le onze* (the eleventh): *le onze novembre*, never *l'onze*
- *le huit* (the eighth): *le huit mars*
- *le oui*: *un grand oui*, *le oui et le non*

You will meet these rarely, but they explain why you hear *le onze* and not *l'onze*.

## Elision in writing and speech

In writing, elision is **mandatory**. Writing *je ai* instead of *j'ai* is a plain error. In speech it is automatic, because the vowel is simply not pronounced. Make elision a reflex by reading phrases aloud as single blocks: *jaimelecafé*, not *je / aime / le / café*.
