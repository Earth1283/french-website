# Liaison: The Hidden Consonant Wakes Up

In the previous lesson you learned that final consonants are usually silent. Now comes the twist: **before a word that starts with a vowel sound, a hidden final consonant can wake up and link to the next word.** This is **liaison**, and it is the other half of why French sounds smooth.

## The sound changes

Not every letter keeps its own sound in liaison. Memorize the shifts:

| Written final letter | Liaison sound | Example |
|---|---|---|
| *s, x, z* | /z/ | *les amis* → lay-za-MEE |
| *t, d* | /t/ | *petit ami* → puh-tee-ta-MEE; *grand homme* → grahn-TOM |
| *n* | /n/ | *un ami* → uh-na-MEE |
| *p* | /p/ | *trop aimable* → troh-pay-MAH-bluh |

The *d* of *grand* becomes a *t* sound. This is the most surprising rule, and the one worth practicing.

## Mandatory liaisons

These happen **every time**, in every register. Skipping them sounds foreign:

- **Article + noun:** *les enfants, un ami, des hôtels, mes amis*
- **Subject pronoun + verb:** *vous avez, nous allons, ils ont, elles arrivent, on a*
- **Short adjective + noun:** *petit ami, grand homme, bon anniversaire*
- **After one-syllable prepositions and adverbs:** *en avion, dans un hôtel, très utile, chez elle*
- **Set expressions:** *c'est-à-dire, de plus en plus, États-Unis*

Look at the subject pronoun line: *vous avez* with the *z* sound is perhaps the most common liaison in the whole language. Every time you say "you have," you do it.

## The four-line test

Say these aloud. Each contains a liaison:

> *Vous avez un ami ?*
>
> *Nous sommes en hiver.*
>
> *Ils ont deux enfants.*
>
> *C'est un petit hôtel.*

The shape in your mouth: the consonant *attaches to the next syllable*, not the previous one. You say *vou-za-vez*, not *vous-a-vez*. Thinking of liaison as a consonant that hops forward makes the rhythm easy to hear.

## What liaison does to rhythm

French stress falls on the end of a phrase group, not on individual words. Liaison helps bind words into those groups. The result is the steady, even flow that learners hear as "musical." If you treat liaison as optional decoration, your French will sound like separate English words with French spelling.
