# The Silent H: Mute and Aspirated

The letter **h** is never pronounced in French. Not in *hôtel*, not in *heure*, not in *homme*, not in *hiver*. English speakers who rasp an "h" at the start of these words are being very English.

But there is a twist that makes the silent h more than just a missing sound: French has **two kinds of silent h**, and they behave differently around the words that precede them.

## The *h muet* (mute h)

The mute h is truly invisible. The word acts exactly as if it began with a vowel:

| Phrase | What happens |
|---|---|
| *l'hôtel* | *le* shrinks to *l'* (elision) |
| *l'homme* | same |
| *l'heure* | same |
| *les hôtels* → *lez-ôtel* | the *s* of *les* links to the vowel (liaison) |
| *un homme* → *un-nomme* | the *n* links across |

Most h-words are *h muet*: *hôtel, homme, heure, histoire, hiver, hôpital, huile, humain*.

## The *h aspiré* (aspirated h)

The aspirated h is **also silent**, despite its name. No breathing sound is made. What it does is act as an invisible barrier, as though the word began with a consonant:

| Phrase | What happens |
|---|---|
| *le haricot* | *le* stays *le* (no elision) |
| *la honte* | *la* stays *la* |
| *les héros* → *lay ay-roh* | no liaison, so you hear a small break |
| *un hamburger* | no *n* link |

Common *h aspiré* words: *haricot* (bean), *héros* (hero), *honte* (shame), *hamburger*, *homard* (lobster), *hibou* (owl), *hockey*. Good dictionaries label them *h aspiré*.

## How to learn the difference

You do not need to memorize a list on day one. Use this order:

1. Say the h as silent in every word. You are already right.
2. For elision and liaison, treat the word as vowel-initial (*l'hôtel*).
3. When you meet a word that sounds wrong that way — *l'haricot* feels off — learn it as an exception.

## Other h-spellings

An h in the middle of a combination is a different matter. *ch* is "sh" (*chat*, *chaud*). *ph* is "f" (*pharmacie*). *th* is "t" (*thé*, *théâtre*), not English "th". Only a lone, initial h is silent.
