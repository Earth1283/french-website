# The Silent Final Consonant Rule

Open any French text and you will find words that end in letters nobody says: *Paris*, *vous*, *petit*, *grand*, *trop*, *beaucoup*. This is not a collection of random exceptions. It is the default. In French, **a final consonant is usually silent**, and learning to expect that saves you from a whole category of beginner errors.

## The default: drop the last letter

Say these words as if the final consonant were not there:

| Written | Sounds like | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| *Paris* | pah-REE | Paris |
| *vous* | voo | you (formal/plural) |
| *ils* | eel | they (masculine) |
| *petit* | puh-TEE | small |
| *grand* | grahn (nasal) | big, tall |
| *trop* | troh | too much |
| *beaucoup* | boh-KOO | a lot |
| *pas* | pah | not |

The English habit is to pronounce everything on the page. French readers do the opposite: they treat the last consonant as part of the spelling, not the sound.

## Why it matters for grammar

Silent endings carry a lot of French grammar, and hearing them is impossible. That is why spoken French sounds the same in many pairs that look different on paper:

- *il parle* and *ils parlent* — identical in speech
- *petit* and *petits* — identical in speech
- *un ami* and *des amis* — differ only in the article

When you listen, you rely on the **little word in front** (*le/les*, *un/des*, *il/ils*) to tell you singular from plural. When you write, you must remember the silent letters yourself. This is why French spelling feels harder than French speech.

## The four-letter exception: C, R, F, L

Many French teachers use the memory trick **CaReFuL**. The consonants **c, r, f, l** at the end of a word are *often* pronounced:

- *sac* (bag), *avec* (with), *bonjour* (hello — the r is heard)
- *chef*, *neuf* (nine), *bœuf* (beef)
- *mal* (bad), *hôtel* (hotel), *animal*

This is a tendency, not a law. Verbs in *-er* (*parler*, *manger*) lose their final *r*, and so do *premier* and *dernier*. If you see a word ending in *c, r, f* or *l*, pronounce it as a first guess, and expect to correct yourself.

## A first routine

When you meet a new word, cover its last letter and say it aloud. If it sounds natural, the letter was silent. If it feels cut off, you are probably looking at a CaReFuL word.
