# Reading Practice and Self-Check

Silent letters become automatic through repetition, not rules. This chapter gives you short passages, a routine, and an answer key so you can test your own pronunciation.

## Step 1: Cross out, then read

Take each sentence and mentally cross out the final consonant of every word that is not a CaReFuL word. Then say it aloud.

> *Vous avez beaucoup de temps ?*
>
> Voo-za-VAY boh-KOO duh TAHN?

> *Il est trop petit pour vous.*
>
> Eel eh troh puh-TEE poor voo.

> *Paris est un grand hôtel.*
>
> Pah-REE eh tuhn grahn-tôTEL.

Notice the *z* in "voo-za-vay" and the *n* in "tuhn": those are liaison. They are the subject of the next lesson. For now, listen for how the silent endings let the vowels flow into each other.

## Step 2: Spot the exceptions

Read these words and decide whether the last letter is heard. Cover the answer first.

1. *sac*
2. *chat*
3. *hôtel*
4. *vingt*
5. *neuf*
6. *trop*

**Answers:** 1. heard (c) — *sack*. 2. silent — *shah*. 3. heard (l) — *oh-TEL*. 4. silent — *vahn*. 5. heard (f) — *nuhf*. 6. silent — *troh*.

If you scored four or more, your instincts are good. If not, reread the CaReFuL section and try again tomorrow.

## Step 3: Record and compare

Record yourself saying ten words, then play them next to an audio clip from the lesson. Check two things only:

- Did any final consonant sneak in where it should be silent?
- Did the vowel stay clean — no extra "uh" added at the end?

French final vowels are crisp. Adding "uh" to *Paris* (*Paris-uh*) is the classic English accent tell.

## A short daily habit

Spend two minutes a day on **five words** from your own life: your street name, your favourite café, a person you know. Say them aloud, silent letters crossed out. After a week the instinct runs on its own, and you will notice you stop pronouncing letters that were never meant to be heard.
