# Clear E Sounds and Real Nasal Vowels

English speakers often turn several French vowels into an indistinct “uh.” That can make speech harder to understand. Two clean *e* sounds and one reliable nasal vowel will improve a surprising number of words.

## *É* /e/: closed and steady

The acute accent usually points to a closed *e*: *été, café, parlé*. It resembles the beginning of English “day,” but it does not glide toward *y*. Hold one pure sound from beginning to end.

Try *café*. If your jaw or tongue moves during the final vowel, slow down and remove the glide: *ca-fééé*. Shorten it only after it stays steady.

The same sound often appears in the verb ending *-er*: *parler* and *manger* end with /e/. This means *parlé* (“spoken”) and *parler* (“to speak”) sound alike even though their grammar differs.

## *È* /ɛ/: open like “bed”

The grave accent in *mère* and the circumflex in *être* commonly indicate an open *e*, close to the vowel in English “bed.” The jaw is slightly lower than for *é*.

Compare:

- *été* /ete/: both vowels are closed
- *mère* /mɛʁ/: the vowel is open
- *préférer* /pʁefeʁe/: the word moves between the two positions

Do not depend on accent marks alone forever; spelling has exceptions. Use them as strong beginner clues and store the sound with each new word.

## *On* /ɔ̃/: the consonant disappears into the vowel

In *bon, nom,* and *maison*, the letters *on* often represent one nasal vowel. Start with an open *o*. Lower the soft palate so some air passes through the nose, and do **not** finish with a separate English *n*. *Bon* is not “bonn”; it ends in resonance.

You can test the airflow by lightly touching the sides of your nose. You should feel vibration during the vowel. Do not pinch the nose while practicing; that only blocks the sound you are trying to create.

Compare *bonne* and *bon*. In *bonne*, the written double *n* belongs to the next syllable and is pronounced. In *bon*, the *n* usually nasalizes the vowel instead. That contrast shows why French spelling must be read in groups, not letter by letter.
