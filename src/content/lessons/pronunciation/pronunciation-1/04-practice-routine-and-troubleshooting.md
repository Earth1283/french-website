# A Ten-Minute Pronunciation Routine

Reading about sounds creates understanding; deliberate repetition creates a new habit. Use this routine with the lesson’s audio or a trusted French recording. Ten focused minutes beats an hour of unfocused imitation.

## Minutes 1–2: prepare the contrasts

Say each pair slowly five times:

- *tu — tout*
- *rue — roue*
- *du — deux*
- *é — è*
- *bon — bonne*

Pause between the words and exaggerate the physical difference. If both words sound the same, do not repeat the same mistake faster. Return to the mouth instructions, reset, and try once more.

## Minutes 3–5: build from sound to word

Practice one target inside several words:

- /ʁ/: *rue, Paris, merci, restaurant*
- /y/: *tu, rue, menu, salut*
- /ø/: *deux, feu, peu, monsieur*
- /e/: *été, café, parler, désolé*
- /ɔ̃/: *bon, bonjour, nom, maison*

Begin with the target sound alone, attach the surrounding consonants, then say the whole word naturally. This “sound → syllable → word” sequence is more productive than repeating a word you cannot yet control.

## Minutes 6–8: place words in short phrases

Say complete thought groups rather than isolated vocabulary:

> *Tu veux du café ?* — Do you want coffee?
>
> *Deux menus, s'il vous plaît.* — Two set menus, please.
>
> *Bonjour, je m'appelle Marie.* — Hello, my name is Marie.

Keep the sentence slow but connected. French rhythm is based more on groups of words than on heavily stressing every important word as English does.

## Minutes 9–10: record and diagnose

Record one phrase, then compare it with a model. Diagnose **one** feature only: Was *u* distinct from *ou*? Did the nasal vowel end without an *n*? Was *é* steady? Fixing one measurable feature is progress; judging the entire accent as “bad” teaches you nothing.

## Common problems

- **The French *r* hurts:** you are using too much pressure. Make it quieter.
- ***U* becomes *oo*:** establish *ee* first, then round only the lips.
- **Nasal vowels sound blocked:** let air use both mouth and nose; do not close the mouth or add a final *n*.
- **Every word sounds choppy:** connect a whole phrase at a comfortable speed, then increase speed gradually.

The goal is not to erase your identity. It is to build reliable sound distinctions so that the French you know can actually be heard.
