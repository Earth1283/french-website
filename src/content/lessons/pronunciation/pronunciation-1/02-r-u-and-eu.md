# The Three Unfamiliar Mouth Shapes

These sounds feel difficult because English does not use the same combinations of tongue and lip position. Treat each one as a physical exercise rather than a spelling problem.

## The French *r*: friction at the back

For an English *r*, the tongue pulls back without touching the roof of the mouth. For the common French *r* /ʁ/, the back of the tongue rises toward the soft palate and creates light friction. Try this sequence:

1. Say a gentle English *k* and notice where the back of your tongue rises.
2. Keep that area close, but do not seal it completely.
3. Let air pass through with a soft rasp, as if beginning a very gentle gargle.
4. Add vowels: *ra, ri, rou*, then words such as *rue*, *Paris*, and *merci*.

Do not force or scrape the throat. A quiet, imperfect back sound is better than a painful theatrical growl. Native speakers also use regional variants, so intelligibility matters more than one “perfect” *r*.

## The French *u* /y/: say *ee*, round the lips

Say *ee* and hold it. Your tongue is high and forward. Without moving the tongue, round your lips as if saying *oo*. The new sound between those two instructions is French *u*.

Practice the contrast slowly:

| French | Approximate target | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| *tu* /ty/ | *ee*-tongue + rounded lips | you |
| *tout* /tu/ | English-like *oo* | everything |
| *rue* /ʁy/ | rounded front vowel | street |
| *roue* /ʁu/ | back *oo* vowel | wheel |

If *tu* keeps becoming *tout*, reset with *ee → rounded ee* before trying the word again.

## The *eu* /ø/ sound: lower and looser

Start from French *u*, then lower and relax the tongue slightly while keeping the lips rounded. This produces the sound in *deux* and *feu*. Compare *du* /dy/ (“some/of the”) with *deux* /dø/ (“two”). The lips look similar; the tongue is a little lower for *eu*.

Use short contrast drills: *tu–tout*, *du–deux*, *rue–roue*. Listen, say each pair slowly, then place one word in a phrase: *deux cafés*, *du pain*, *tu arrives*. Contrasts train the ear and mouth at the same time.
