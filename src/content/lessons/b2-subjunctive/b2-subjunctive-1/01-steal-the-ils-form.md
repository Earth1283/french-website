# Steal the Ils-Form and Chop Off "-ent"

The subjunctive is not a new set of verbs to memorise from scratch. For the vast majority of French verbs, you already know the stem — you've been using it every time you conjugate *ils/elles* in the present tense.

Take the *ils* form, remove *-ent*, and you have the subjunctive stem for *je, tu, il/elle, ils/elles*:

> *ils parlent* → *parl-* → *que je parle, que tu parles, qu'il parle, qu'ils parlent*
>
> *ils finissent* → *finiss-* → *que je finisse, qu'elle finisse, qu'ils finissent*
>
> *ils vendent* → *vend-* → *que je vende, qu'il vende, qu'ils vendent*

Onto that stem, add the subjunctive endings: *-e, -es, -e, -ions, -iez, -ent*. Notice these are almost the same endings as the present indicative for *-er* verbs — which is exactly why *que je parle* looks identical to the indicative *je parle*. Only context (the *que* and the trigger before it) tells you which mood you're in.

## Nous and vous borrow from the imperfect

For *nous* and *vous*, regular verbs use the same *ils*-stem plus *-ions* / *-iez*:

> *que nous parlions, que vous parliez*
>
> *que nous finissions, que vous finissiez*

For regular verbs this causes no confusion, because the stem doesn't change. The complications start with verbs whose *nous* form looks nothing like their *ils* form — which is the subject of the next chapter.

## Why "que" always comes first

The subjunctive almost never appears on its own. It lives inside a subordinate clause introduced by *que*, hanging off a trigger expression: *il faut que je parte*, *je veux que tu viennes*. You will rarely conjugate a subjunctive verb in isolation — you'll always be attaching it to a sentence that needs it. Get comfortable saying the *que* out loud as part of the form: not "je fasse" in a vacuum, but "il faut *que* je fasse".

## The trap: silent endings

Because *-e, -es,* and *-ent* are all silent in speech, *que je parle*, *que tu parles*, and *qu'ils parlent* sound completely identical. English speakers instinctively want to hear a difference between singular and plural verb forms; French subjunctive endings don't give you one for *-er* verbs. Trust the spelling, not your ear, when you write.

## Quick self-check

- What's the subjunctive stem for *finir*, and where does it come from?
- Conjugate *vendre* in the subjunctive for *tu* and for *nous*.
- Why does *que nous parlions* look like the imperfect?

(*finiss-*, from *ils finissent* → *que tu vendes*; *que nous vendions* → because both forms are built the same way, off the *nous* stem plus *-ions*.)
