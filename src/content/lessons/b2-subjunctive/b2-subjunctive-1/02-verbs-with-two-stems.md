# Verbs With Two Stems

Some of the most common French verbs use one stem for *je, tu, il/elle, ils/elles* and a completely different-looking one for *nous* and *vous*. This isn't a new rule — it's the same "steal the *ils* form" method from the previous chapter, applied twice, because these verbs already have two stems in the present indicative.

## Venir, prendre, boire, devoir

Each of these verbs has a "strong" stem (used in *ils*) and a "weak" stem (used in *nous*):

> *ils viennent* / *nous venons* → *que je vienne, que tu viennes, qu'il vienne, qu'ils viennent* / *que nous venions, que vous veniez*
>
> *ils prennent* / *nous prenons* → *que je prenne, qu'elle prenne* / *que nous prenions, que vous preniez*
>
> *ils boivent* / *nous buvons* → *que je boive, qu'il boive* / *que nous buvions, que vous buviez*
>
> *ils doivent* / *nous devons* → *que je doive, qu'elle doive* / *que nous devions, que vous deviez*

The pattern is mechanical once you see it: the subjunctive simply photocopies whichever present-indicative stem already belongs to that person. *Je, tu, il, ils* get the *ils*-stem; *nous, vous* get the *nous*-stem.

## Why this trips learners up

English has nothing like this — verbs don't quietly swap their internal vowel depending on which pronoun is attached. So when learners meet *que je vienne* and *que nous venions* side by side, the instinct is to assume one of them is wrong, or to force the *vienn-* stem onto *nous* (*"que nous viennions"*) — which is incorrect. The safest fix: whenever you're unsure, mentally conjugate the verb in the present indicative first (*ils viennent, nous venons*), then attach the subjunctive endings to whichever stem matches the person you need.

> *Il faut que tu prennes tes médicaments.* — You need to take your medication.
>
> *Il faut que vous preniez la sortie 12.* — You need to take exit 12.

## Other verbs in this family

This two-stem pattern also covers verbs like *acheter* (*ils achètent* / *nous achetons*), *appeler* (*ils appellent* / *nous appelons*), and *voir* (*ils voient* / *nous voyons*). Any verb that already has a stem change between *ils* and *nous* in the present indicative carries that same split into the subjunctive.

## Quick self-check

- Conjugate *boire* in the subjunctive for *elle* and for *nous*.
- True or false: *que nous prenions* uses the same stem as *que je prenne*.
- Why is *"que nous viennions"* wrong?

(*qu'elle boive / que nous buvions* → False, it uses the *nous*-stem, not the *ils*-stem → because *nous* takes the weak stem *ven-*, not the strong stem *vienn-*: the correct form is *que nous venions*.)
