# Choose the Mood, Then Defend It

## A useful editing pass

After writing a paragraph, circle each *que*. For every clause that follows, name its relationship to the main clause: fact, certainty, opinion, wish, doubt, emotion, goal, concession, or unknown referent. Do not change a form simply because another sentence nearby uses subjunctive. A paragraph can legitimately contain both moods several times. This edit makes advanced grammar visible without making your writing mechanical. It also gives you a way to explain choices on an exam: you are not reciting a rule, but showing how the speaker frames the information.

At B2, the useful question is no longer “Does this expression appear on a trigger list?” It is “How is the speaker presenting this clause?” Indicative presents information, certainty, or an identified fact. Subjunctive presents attitude, uncertainty, requirement, evaluation, or an unrealised possibility.

> *Il est évident qu'elle comprend.* — It is obvious that she understands.

> *Il n'est pas évident qu'elle comprenne.* — It is not obvious that she understands.

The adjective changes the speaker's commitment, so the mood changes with it.

## Two sentences, two viewpoints

> *Je cherche le dossier qui est sur la table.* — I am looking for the file that is on the table.

> *Je cherche un dossier qui soit facile à lire.* — I am looking for a file that is easy to read.

The first file is known and specific. The second is a desired type. Grammar is reporting the difference your listener needs to understand.

## Register note

Spoken French sometimes levels distinctions that formal writing keeps. You may hear indicative where a grammar guide expects subjunctive after a question. Recognise the careful standard for exams and writing; use natural speech without pretending every variation is an error.

## A decision routine

1. Find the verb or conjunction before *que*.
2. Is it certainty or an identified fact? Start with indicative.
3. Is it desire, emotion, doubt, judgment, purpose, prevention, or a sought-but-unknown thing? Choose subjunctive.
4. Read the whole sentence: a negative or question can change the stance.

This routine will not eliminate every nuanced case, but it gives you a reasoned choice. That is better than throwing a subjunctive at every *que* and hoping it looks sophisticated.
