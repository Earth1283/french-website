# Subjunctive When the Referent Is Uncertain

## Turn the contrast into a habit

Make pairs that change only the article or reference: *Je connais une personne qui peut aider* versus *Je cherche une personne qui puisse aider*. In the first, the person exists and is known; in the second, the speaker is searching for an imagined solution. Do the same with objects: *le livre qui explique* and *un livre qui puisse expliquer*. This contrast is more useful than memorising isolated “subjunctive after someone” rules. It teaches you to notice whether your listener can identify the person or thing, or whether it remains a desire, possibility, or evaluation.

French often uses subjunctive after a superlative or an indefinite search because the person or thing is not presented as a settled fact. You are evaluating, hoping, or looking for something — not calmly pointing to it on a table.

> *C'est le meilleur film que j'aie vu.* — It is the best film I have seen.

The subjunctive *aie vu* follows *le meilleur*. The speaker is making a subjective evaluation, even if they sound extremely sure and have already told everyone twice.

## Searching for someone

> *Je cherche quelqu'un qui puisse m'aider.* — I am looking for someone who can help me.

The helper may not exist yet, so *puisse* is natural. Once the person is identified, indicative returns:

> *Je connais quelqu'un qui peut t'aider.* — I know someone who can help you.

## Indefinite expressions

*Je voudrais une chambre qui ait une vue sur la mer* expresses a desired, not identified, room. *Il n'y a personne qui sache la réponse* uses subjunctive because the existence of such a person is denied or uncertain.

Do not force subjunctive whenever you see *quelqu'un*. A specific person takes indicative: *C'est quelqu'un qui sait écouter.* The difference is whether the speaker has a real referent in mind.

## Quick check

- “the only person who understands” → *la seule personne qui comprenne*
- “a book that can explain it” → *un livre qui puisse l'expliquer*
- “the book that explains it” → *le livre qui l'explique*

The pattern is logical once you notice the contrast between an identified fact and an imagined or evaluated possibility.
