# Emotion and Doubt

Anything you feel strongly about, and anything you're not sure of, tends to trigger the subjunctive. The logic: emotion and doubt are both subjective — they describe your reaction to a fact, not the fact itself as a neutral certainty.

## Emotion triggers

> *Je suis content(e) que tu sois là.* — I'm glad you're here.
>
> *Elle est triste que son ami parte.* — She's sad that her friend is leaving.
>
> *Nous sommes surpris que vous ayez réussi.* — We're surprised that you succeeded.

Any adjective of emotion followed by *que* pulls in the subjunctive: *content, triste, surpris, furieux, déçu, ravi* — the pattern is the same regardless of which feeling you're expressing.

## Avoir peur que... ne: the ghost word

*Avoir peur que* ("to be afraid that") behaves like the other emotion triggers, but in careful written and spoken French it often comes with an extra *ne* before the verb that isn't a negation:

> *J'ai peur qu'il ne pleuve.* — I'm afraid it will rain.

This is the **ne explétif**: a leftover of an older grammatical habit, with no negative meaning at all. It shows up after verbs of fear, and (as you'll see in the next chapter) after a few conjunctions like *avant que*. In casual conversation, French speakers frequently drop it — *j'ai peur qu'il pleuve* means exactly the same thing. Recognise it so it doesn't confuse you in a text or a news article; you don't have to produce it yourself to sound correct.

## Douter que: doubt

*Douter que* ("to doubt that") is the cleanest example of the subjunctive marking uncertainty:

> *Je doute qu'il vienne ce soir.* — I doubt he's coming tonight.
>
> *Elle doute que nous ayons raison.* — She doubts we're right.

Compare this to *savoir que* or affirmative *penser que*, which express certainty and take the indicative instead (you'll meet this contrast properly in Lesson 3). For now, the rule of thumb: if the verb itself contains uncertainty — *douter*, or an emotion adjective describing a subjective reaction — the clause after *que* goes subjunctive.

## Quick self-check

- Translate: "I'm afraid he'll be late." (avoir peur que + être, il — with the ne explétif)
- Is the *ne* in *j'ai peur qu'il ne soit en retard* a real negation, given there's no *pas*?
- Translate: "She's glad you understand." (être content(e) que + comprendre, tu)

(*J'ai peur qu'il ne soit en retard.* → No — there's no *pas*, so this is the meaningless *ne explétif*; the sentence still means "he'll be late," not "he won't be late." → *Elle est contente que tu comprennes.*)
