# Necessity and Will

The subjunctive doesn't appear randomly — it's summoned by specific expressions. This chapter covers the two most common summoners: necessity and will.

## Il faut que: the workhorse

*Il faut que* is the single most common subjunctive trigger in French, and it never takes an exception:

> *Il faut que tu finisses tes devoirs.* — You have to finish your homework.
>
> *Il faut que nous partions maintenant.* — We have to leave now.

*Il faut* also works with an infinitive when there's no specific subject: *il faut partir* ("one must leave," nobody in particular). The moment you name who has to do it, *que* plus the subjunctive takes over: *il faut que je parte*.

## Vouloir que and aimer que: wanting someone else

*Vouloir que* and *aimer que* express a wish about someone else's actions:

> *Je veux que tu sois honnête avec moi.* — I want you to be honest with me.
>
> *J'aimerais que vous m'appeliez demain.* — I'd like you to call me tomorrow.

## The rule that catches everyone: same subject → infinitive

Here is the single most important habit to build in this lesson. *Vouloir que* (and every other trigger in this unit) only applies when **two different people** are involved: one who wants, and a different one who acts. When the same person does both, French drops *que* entirely and uses an infinitive instead:

> ✅ *Je veux partir.* — I want to leave. (I want, and I leave — same person)
>
> ❌ *~~Je veux que je parte.~~*
>
> ✅ *Je veux que tu partes.* — I want you to leave. (I want, you leave — different people)

English speakers often translate "I want to leave" too literally and reach for *que*, producing the ungrammatical *"je veux que je parte."* French simply never does this: same subject, same clause, infinitive, full stop. This rule will resurface with almost every trigger in this lesson, so get it right here first.

> *Il faut que je parte* is fine, by the way — *il faut* has no subject of its own (it's impersonal), so there's no "same person" conflict to trigger the infinitive rule.

## Quick self-check

- Rewrite correctly: *"Je veux que je mange."*
- Translate: "I want you to call me." (vouloir que + appeler, tu)
- Why does *il faut que je parte* not switch to an infinitive?

(*Je veux manger.* → *Je veux que tu m'appelles.* → because *il faut* has no subject of its own, so the same-subject rule doesn't apply.)
