# Purpose, Concession and the Subjunctive

Some conjunctions trigger the subjunctive regardless of how certain the speaker is. They frame an action as a goal, obstacle, limit, or condition rather than simply reporting a fact. Learn the conjunction with its mood; translating the English word alone is a surprisingly bad bargain.

## Purpose: *pour que*

Use *pour que* when the subjects differ. The goal clause takes subjunctive.

> *Je parle lentement pour que vous compreniez.* — I speak slowly so that you understand.

When one subject does both actions, use *pour + infinitif*: *Je parle lentement pour être compris*. Do not make the same person run through *pour que* just because English says “so that.”

## Concession: *bien que*

*Bien que* means “although” and is always followed by subjunctive.

> *Bien qu'il soit fatigué, il continue.* — Although he is tired, he continues.

Compare *même si*, which takes the indicative: *Même s'il est fatigué, il vient*. The first concedes a fact within an argument; the second states a condition. They can look interchangeable in English but they are not mood twins.

## Before, unless, without

- *avant que* + subjunctive: *Pars avant qu'il arrive.*
- *à moins que* + subjunctive: *Je viendrai à moins qu'il pleuve.*
- *sans que* + subjunctive: *Il est parti sans que je le sache.*

You may see a *ne explétif* after *avant que* or *à moins que*: *avant qu'il ne parte*. It is not negative; adding *pas* would change the meaning.

## Quick contrast

> *Je ferme la fenêtre pour que le chat ne sorte pas.* — I close the window so that the cat does not get out.

> *Je ferme la fenêtre avant que le chat sorte.* — I close the window before the cat gets out.

The forms look technical, but the logic is practical: purpose, obstacle, prevention and concession all place the action in a speaker's frame. That is exactly where French likes the subjunctive.

Write one sentence about a real goal, one about a concession, and one about avoiding a problem. If the two subjects are the same, check whether an infinitive is cleaner. That contrast is often the deciding detail between an accurate advanced sentence and a translated one.
